Which Oil Circuit Should Go First? A Screening Guide

Rank candidates by release pathway, technical fit and renewal timing, not reservoir size, to find where water delivers the biggest return. Keep water away from molten metal.

Testing & Adoption6 min read

Written by WaterQuest Solutions · Updated 2 November 2026 · About the distributor

Start where oil removal solves a visible problem

Two renewal candidates, one useful comparison

CandidateExposureDecision
Small gate actuator directly over a watercourseA short leak path reaches receiving water.Prioritize feasibility if water-rated components meet the duty.
Larger oil circuit in a contained dry plant roomContainment interrupts the release path.Compare energy, maintenance and renewal timing; reservoir size alone does not set priority.

Prioritization is a portfolio decision. A facility may have dozens of circuits competing for limited engineering time and renewal funding.

The aim is to identify where detailed engineering is worth doing next. A screening result is not a design approval or a commitment to purchase.

Start with consequence and the actual release route

Map where oil could travel from each candidate circuit.

Consider the available inventory and the operating state that could produce a release. Detection time, isolation and replenishment affect event volume.

Waterway-related candidates require particular attention to the receiving environment. Canadian Fisheries Act guidance explains why substances and their routes to fish-bearing waters matter. The screening exercise should identify where specialist environmental review is needed.

Examine product and process sensitivity separately

A hydraulic release near a product can create a different consequence from a release near a waterway.

Water actuation may remove a hydraulic-oil pathway, but hydraulic water is not automatically suitable for product contact. CFIA's guidance on water used in food preparation illustrates the importance of suitability for the intended use. The process boundary, hygiene requirements and any cross-connection need their own assessment. Removing one contaminant source does not establish that the replacement fluid can enter a product without consequence.

This distinction helps procurement write useful requirements. State whether the objective is to prevent lubricant release above exposed product, reduce servicing near a controlled area or meet another defined process need.

Treat heat and fire exposure with care

Oil removal may be attractive where a hydraulic release could encounter heat or ignition sources. Water as the working fluid does not make the assembled equipment fireproof or suitable for every hot process.

Molten metal is a particularly important exception to simplistic reasoning. HSE guidance warns that water contamination of molten metal can cause explosions. A proposal near such a process requires specialist assessment of potential water pathways.

The screening question is therefore comparative: which complete arrangement reduces the relevant hazards while meeting the duty?

Check whether the mechanical duty fits

Once a meaningful benefit is identified, review force, speed, travel, holding and operating frequency. The proposed water equipment must support the actual load envelope and environment. A suitable nominal pressure does not establish acceptable precision, response or lifetime.

Manufacturer information for purpose-designed water machinery, such as the Janus P3, demonstrates that supported equipment exists within defined operating ranges. It is a starting point for feasibility, not evidence that every existing oil component can be retained.

Operating pattern can create an opportunity. An intermittent gate may benefit from a supply arrangement designed around long standby, while continuous machinery needs a different energy and thermal assessment.

Include the support system in feasibility

Water supply, filtration, temperature management and maintenance capability belong in the screening. An apparently ideal oil-removal location may be difficult to support if suitable working water is unavailable or exposed branches cannot be protected against freezing. These constraints do not necessarily rule out the project, but they can change its cost and complexity substantially.

Ask who will operate and maintain the installation.

The complete support boundary should appear in the preliminary cost range. Include electrical work, controls integration, access, drainage and necessary water management.

Use renewal timing to improve the decision

Replacing an asset already due for renewal is a different economic decision from retiring functioning equipment early.

Coordinate the opportunity with planned outages and adjacent work. A technically attractive idea can still be a poor immediate priority if its transition cannot be supported safely.

Keep a future-candidate list for assets that are unsuitable for immediate change but merit review at renewal.

Compare candidates without false numerical precision

A screening matrix can organize consequence, technical fit, readiness of evidence and implementation timing. A total with several decimal places does not create certainty when the inputs are qualitative judgments.

Consider two hypothetical candidates. One is an exposed gate circuit due for replacement, with well-known loads and a manageable installation window.

Separate mandatory constraints from preferences. A candidate that cannot meet a required safety or service function should not be rescued by a high environmental score elsewhere in the matrix.

Turn the leading candidate into a bounded evaluation

Select a trial or detailed study that answers the uncertainty controlling the decision. If the main question is load response, define a representative motion test.

The DOE pumping sourcebook supports system-level assessment of operating performance. Apply that principle by comparing alternatives at the same useful duty and recording the complete measurement boundary. Keep energy, oil-exposure reduction and maintenance as distinct benefits so the proposal does not attribute all improvement to the working medium when controls or layout also change.

A strong priority list ends with concrete next actions and owners. It identifies which circuit deserves attention, why it matters, what must be confirmed and when a decision can be made.
