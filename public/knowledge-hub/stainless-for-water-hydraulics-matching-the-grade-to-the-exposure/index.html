Stainless for Water Hydraulics: Matching the Grade to the Exposure

Compare 316L, duplex and hardened stainless for corrosion, strength and wear. Choose the grade for the water chemistry, temperature, crevices and external exposure.

Components & Materials7 min read

Download the worksheet for this guide (editable text) · Open the materials requirement register (CSV)

Written by WaterQuest Solutions · Updated 2 November 2026 · About the distributor

Choose stainless steel for the water and the location

Name the alloy, then the exposure

316L is an austenitic stainless candidate for many freshwater parts. Duplex 2205 and superduplex 2507 offer stronger chloride-resistance options. Hardened martensitic or precipitation-hardening grades, including 17-4PH, may serve wear or shaft duties when approved for the exposure. There is no single chloride limit for every assembly: temperature, oxygen, crevices, stress and surface condition change the result. A manufacturer’s 2205/316L comparison.

Start by describing where the metal will be used. Is it a pressure-containing body, a moving counterface, a fastener or a support?

This is more useful than asking whether one alloy is universally better than another. A selection must satisfy the component's full duty, and unnecessary upgrading can carry cost without resolving an unsuitable joint geometry or an undocumented internal part.

Know what a grade designation does and does not tell you

A stainless grade identifies a defined material composition and related requirements under an applicable specification. Outokumpu's overview is a useful starting point for understanding these families rather than treating all stainless products as the same material.

For purchasing, a familiar designation is only the beginning. A drawing that says only “stainless” cannot reliably establish whether the delivered part matches the designer's intent, especially when an assembly contains several internal materials.

The specification should be precise without inventing requirements that do not improve the application. The objective is traceability to the approved design, not collecting certificates whose meaning nobody on the project has defined.

Separate corrosion resistance from mechanical capacity

Corrosion resistance describes how a material behaves in an environment. Mechanical design addresses stresses, deformation, fatigue and other load-related conditions. Selecting a more corrosion-resistant alloy does not automatically make an existing geometry suitable for a higher pressure. Equally, satisfying a pressure calculation does not establish acceptable behaviour in the intended water chemistry.

Consider a fitting connected to a vibrating machine. Its material may be appropriate for the fluid, while its installation introduces an unacceptable mechanical load through the pipework. The connection geometry, support and pressure duty must be reviewed together.

Moving surfaces introduce another requirement. Do not replace a documented internal grade with the nominal housing alloy solely to make the material list look uniform.

Assess local conditions that a bulk-water sample cannot show

The water measured in a reservoir represents a useful part of the exposure, but local conditions can differ at a joint or beneath a deposit. Outokumpu's corrosion guidance explains why the mechanism of attack matters to the choice of control.

A narrow instrument branch illustrates the issue. The main circuit may receive regular flow while the branch holds water during long idle periods.

This is not an argument that every crevice inevitably fails. It is an argument for describing the actual configuration before claiming suitability.

Use corrosion tables as screening evidence

Published corrosion tables can help narrow a list of candidate materials, but their test conditions are part of the result. A favourable entry is useful evidence within its boundary, not approval for every assembly containing that grade.

When reviewing a table, first ask whether the environment resembles the proposed service. A process stream containing several constituents cannot always be represented by choosing a row for just one of them.

If the comparison is uncertain, record what additional information would change the selection. That may be a better water analysis, manufacturer experience with a similar configuration or representative testing.

Preserve surface condition through manufacture and installation

Material selection is not finished when the raw stock is ordered. Fabrication and handling determine the condition of the finished surface.

Installation practices can undermine that work. Keep fluid connections protected while adjacent fabrication is occurring, prevent debris from entering open ports and use approved assembly consumables.

A repair procedure deserves the same attention. Cleaning to remove loose contamination is not necessarily the same operation as a treatment intended to restore or modify a surface.

Review dissimilar materials at the interfaces

A stainless assembly often connects to other metals or to equipment whose internal construction is different. Dissimilar-metal contact in an electrolyte can create galvanic conditions; the exposed areas and actual environment affect the severity.

Possible responses include a compatible material change or an engineered isolation arrangement. Any proposed isolation must preserve pressure integrity and the required electrical safety provisions. A modification that reduces one corrosion path while compromising bonding or creating a weak connection does not solve the system problem.

Trace the interface decision into the spare-parts list. If a specific fitting or isolating arrangement is required, make it identifiable so that a routine replacement does not restore the original exposure.

Write a material schedule that can be maintained

A useful schedule links each component or assembly to its approved wetted materials and supporting information.

The equipment supplier may provide an assembly-level approval rather than every proprietary manufacturing detail. Protecting legitimate proprietary information does not require leaving the intended service ambiguous. The buyer needs a supportable statement of suitability and a way to preserve the approved configuration.

For a water-hydraulic installation, that schedule should connect to the actual water specification. The Water Hydraulics Company's P3 documentation illustrates that construction and fluid conditions can be configuration-dependent. A product's general association with water does not establish suitability for every source or every cleaning exposure. Resolve the selected configuration during enquiry and carry it through the purchase and commissioning records.

Evaluate value over the installed service life

The lowest raw-material cost is rarely the whole purchasing question. A more suitable material or joint arrangement may reduce intervention at a difficult location, while an unnecessary upgrade may add expense without changing the governing failure mechanism.

The compelling case for stainless water-hydraulic equipment is therefore specific. The selected assembly delivers the mechanical duty, its materials suit the defined exposure, and the owner has a practical way to maintain that condition.
