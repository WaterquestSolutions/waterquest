Cylinder Losing Pressure? Measure at the Actuator, Under Load

Inlet supply, restrictions, internal bypass and binding produce similar symptoms. Compare both chamber pressures, both directions and the command signal.

Water Hydraulic Systems7 min read

Download the worksheet for this guide (editable text) · Open the cylinder requirement register (CSV)

Written by WaterQuest Solutions · Updated 2 November 2026 · About the distributor

Turn a pressure symptom into a focused check

A pressure reading becomes a force balance

For an illustrative 80/40 mm (3.15/1.57 in) cylinder, 100 bar (1,450 psi) at the cap end and 8 bar (116 psi) at the rod end give about 47.3 kN (10,630 lbf) net extension force before friction. Pump pressure alone would suggest 50.3 kN (11,300 lbf).

A water-driven cylinder that moves slowly is not necessarily suffering from low pressure. Speed depends principally on delivered flow and effective area, while force depends on the pressure balance and geometry.

This article offers diagnostic reasoning rather than a procedure for opening, blocking or adjusting a live circuit. The site's authorized methods and competent personnel must govern investigation.

Confirm what the instruments are showing

Check the identity, range and location of the pressure measurement.

Compare readings only when they represent the same state.

If an instrument reading is inconsistent with the machine's other observations, the measurement itself deserves assessment through an appropriate method. Do not assume every sensor is correct because its display is precise. Equally, do not dismiss an unexpected value merely because the machine appears to operate.

Separate pressure loss from insufficient force

During flow, pressure differences can occur across lines, fittings and control elements. The relevant difference depends on the path and operating condition.

At the cylinder, both chamber pressures contribute to the force balance. The underlying relationship is pressure acting on effective area. An unexpectedly high opposing pressure can reduce useful force even when the supply-side reading appears normal. This is one reason that one gauge does not explain every actuator problem.

The mechanical requirement also matters. If the mechanism's resistance has increased, the same hydraulic condition may no longer produce the expected movement.

Examine whether the pump is receiving suitable supply

A pump cannot deliver its supported performance if its inlet or other required connection conditions are not maintained. The Water Hydraulics Company's pump information identifies configuration-dependent supply requirements that should be checked against the installed system.

The useful observation is the condition relevant to the pump during the problematic duty. An upstream static pressure or a full-looking reservoir does not necessarily establish adequate supply during demand.

Note whether the symptom appears only at high flow, after a long run or after a service change.

Consider restrictions in the active flow path

A changed filter condition, partially unavailable path or altered control element can affect the flow reaching the actuator.

Filter differential pressure should be interpreted at a comparable flow and fluid condition. Parker's filtration guidance explains why filter selection and condition belong in hydraulic performance assessment. A changed reading can inform the investigation, but it does not by itself prove that the element is the only cause of the machine's symptom.

Review recent work and configuration changes.

Distinguish external leakage from internal bypass

Visible leakage indicates that fluid is leaving its intended containment boundary. Internal bypass can reduce useful delivery or affect holding while leaving the exterior dry.

An internal path may be within a cylinder, valve or another component. A pressure decline during holding does not uniquely identify the cylinder seal because the complete circuit and external load influence the observation. The responsible diagnostic method must distinguish those possibilities without unsafe improvised blocking or isolation.

Track make-up demand where it is meaningful to the architecture, and record whether the symptom appears in one direction or both. They do not replace equipment-specific testing, but they help ensure that the next test is selected to answer a question rather than simply to confirm a preferred guess.

Compare extension and retraction intelligently

A single-rod cylinder has different effective areas and displaced volumes on its two sides. It can therefore have different ideal forces and speeds in extension and retraction even when the supply arrangement is unchanged.

Compare actual behaviour with the intended design.

The load may also change through the stroke. A mechanism that binds near one end or passes through a poor leverage position can create a local pressure increase and slow movement.

Use temperature and time as diagnostic context

A symptom that develops as the equipment warms can suggest a different set of questions from one present immediately after start-up.

Record temperature at a relevant location and compare under similar duty.

Time also helps distinguish a transient from a sustained condition. A brief pressure dip during a command transition differs from a persistent inability to complete movement.

Review command and feedback before replacing hardware

Hydraulic performance depends on what the controls ask the equipment to do. A changed command, valve signal or sequence can produce a different pressure and movement pattern without a mechanical failure in the cylinder.

Feedback can also be misleading if a sensor, connection or interpretation has changed. A controller may respond to an apparent position or pressure condition that does not represent the mechanism correctly.

This is particularly relevant after software changes or electrical service. Recording the chronology prevents a hydraulic symptom from being investigated in isolation when it began immediately after a control modification.

Turn the observations into a focused investigation

A useful fault report states the commanded function, affected direction, position, pressure locations, temperature and timing of the symptom.

Keep the machine within its authorized operating and service arrangements during the investigation. Stored pressure and mechanical loads can remain after the motor stops; CCOHS provides the general hazardous-energy context. Do not search for leaks by exposing skin to a suspected high-pressure release or open a connection as an informal diagnostic method.

WaterQuest can assist most effectively when the symptom is described as evidence rather than as a presumed failed part.

After repair, repeat the relevant functional observation under an appropriate comparable condition. Record whether the original symptom has been resolved and whether any other behaviour changed.

Consider a hypothetical cylinder that slows only in the final part of extension.

If evidence remains inconclusive, preserve that uncertainty. The accepted equipment and mechanical limits remain constraints during diagnosis, and a symptom should not be masked by a change that creates a different unsupported condition.
