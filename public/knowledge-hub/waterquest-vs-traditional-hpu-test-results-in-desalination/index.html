WaterQuest vs Traditional HPU: Test Results in Desalination

Controlled test results comparing WaterQuest water hydraulic HPUs against traditional oil-based HPUs in desalination service conditions. Pressure stability, thermal performance, corrosion resistance, cycle durability, and environmental risk profile compared.

Desalination systems operate at the edge of mechanical tolerance. High-pressure reverse osmosis requires stable pressure delivery, continuous duty operation, corrosion resistance, energy efficiency, and long-cycle durability. Traditional hydraulic power units used in desalination environments typically rely on oil-based actuation for valve control, membrane flushing systems, and auxiliary positioning equipment. WaterQuest water hydraulic HPUs were developed specifically to align with the desalination environment itself. This article outlines performance comparisons observed during controlled testing and field simulation conditions.

The Desalination Operating Environment

Seawater reverse osmosis systems typically operate at 55 to 70 bar for seawater RO, with higher pressures in certain brackish configurations. The environment presents high chloride concentration, continuous moisture, salt spray exposure, temperature variation, and cyclic pressure loading. Oil-based HPUs in this context introduce corrosion risk at hose fittings, hydrocarbon exposure near membrane trains, and fire load in confined pump rooms. Water hydraulic HPUs eliminate hydrocarbon presence in an already water-saturated facility, aligning the actuation medium with the plant's primary process.

Test Objective and Parameters

Testing compared a traditional oil-based HPU and a WaterQuest stainless water hydraulic HPU across pressure stability, thermal behaviour, leakage rate, cycle durability, energy draw, and corrosion exposure. Testing simulated continuous valve actuation cycles, start-stop load transitions, high humidity chamber exposure, and accelerated salt spray testing to replicate actual desalination service conditions.

Pressure Stability Results

The WaterQuest HPU demonstrated immediate pressure response, minimal pressure lag, and stable holding under load. Water's near-incompressibility produced consistent actuator motion during repeated cycles. The traditional oil HPU exhibited minor pressure compliance under dynamic load, temperature-dependent viscosity variation, and slight response lag during cold-start conditions. Under steady-state operation, both systems achieved target pressure. Under transient conditions, the water system demonstrated faster response stability, which is significant for membrane flush sequencing and anti-scaling control.

Thermal Performance

Oil-based systems rely on cooling loops, reservoir thermal mass, and heat exchangers. During extended cycling, oil temperature increased gradually, viscosity shift was observed, and cooling demand rose. The WaterQuest HPU showed lower heat retention, rapid thermal dissipation, and minimal temperature-related performance variation. Water's high thermal conductivity improved temperature stability across long-duty cycles, eliminating the need for auxiliary cooling infrastructure in the test configuration.

Corrosion Exposure Testing

Salt spray chamber testing evaluated external fasteners, manifold blocks, cylinder rods, and fitting interfaces. Traditional oil HPU components with mixed alloys showed early surface corrosion at non-stainless fasteners and localized oxidation at fittings. WaterQuest HPU components, constructed with 316 stainless steel for all wetted metallic components and carbon-reinforced PEEK sliding interfaces, demonstrated no measurable pitting during the test window, stable surface integrity, and no galvanic degradation. Material uniformity reduced corrosion initiation points throughout the assembly.

Leakage and Environmental Risk

During induced hose-failure simulation, the oil system produced visible hydrocarbon discharge, surface contamination, and absorbent cleanup requirement. The WaterQuest system discharge consisted of process-compatible water, no hydrocarbon residue, and no hazardous waste handling. Mechanical failure remains undesirable in both cases. Environmental consequence differed significantly. In a facility producing potable water, this distinction is not theoretical.

Cycle Durability Testing

Both systems underwent high-cycle actuation testing with a 1M cycle target under load conditions simulating repeated full-stroke valve actuation and pressure cycling. The WaterQuest HPU maintained seal integrity, showed no varnish buildup, demonstrated no fluid degradation, and delivered stable pressure throughout. The traditional oil HPU required filtration monitoring, exhibited minor fluid darkening, and required additive condition monitoring. Water's chemical stability eliminated fluid aging variables across the full test duration.

Strategic Implication for Desalination

Desalination plants exist to produce clean water, protect marine environments, and operate efficiently under high pressure. Introducing oil into these environments adds chemical complexity that conflicts with the facility's purpose. Water hydraulic HPUs align the actuation medium with the plant's primary process fluid. In facilities built to separate salt from water, removing oil from the hydraulic system simplifies risk and supports long-term operational resilience.

Key Takeaways

  • Under transient conditions, the WaterQuest HPU demonstrated faster pressure response stability than the oil HPU due to water's near-incompressibility and temperature-independent viscosity.
  • Salt spray testing showed no measurable pitting on WaterQuest 316 stainless components while mixed-alloy oil HPU components showed early surface corrosion at non-stainless fasteners.
  • 1M cycle durability testing produced no varnish, no fluid degradation, and no additive depletion in the WaterQuest HPU. Chemical stability eliminated fluid aging as a maintenance variable.
  • Hose failure simulation in a desalination context: oil system requires hazardous cleanup near potable water infrastructure. Water system releases process-compatible fluid with no remediation requirement.
  • Water HPU footprint is smaller than oil equivalent due to elimination of cooling loops, heat exchangers, and associated auxiliary infrastructure that oil systems require in continuous-duty service.
