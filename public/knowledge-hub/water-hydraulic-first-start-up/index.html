First Start-Up on Water: Flush, Vent and Pressurize in Stages

Use the approved commissioning procedure to control debris and air, establish inlet supply and raise demand in stages. Record the baseline before handover.

Water Hydraulic Systems7 min read

Written by WaterQuest Solutions · Updated 2 November 2026 · About the distributor

Start with a clean circuit and a clear sequence

Start-up checklist for the commissioning plan

  • Confirm the exact pump, valve, seal and filter configuration.
  • Inspect construction debris controls and use the approved flushing route.
  • Confirm water supply, inlet pressure and permitted drain arrangement.
  • Vent under the equipment procedure and verify the system’s energy-control measures.
  • Raise demand in approved stages while watching leaks, temperature and response.
  • Record the accepted baseline and train the service team.

This article explains commissioning principles rather than a universal start-up sequence. The actual method must come from the selected equipment instructions, the installed circuit and the responsible commissioning team.

The Water Hydraulics Company's pump information illustrates why the configuration matters: water-service equipment still depends on defined fluid and connection conditions. A useful start-up plan translates those requirements into observations that can be verified on the actual installation. That makes the first run an engineering check rather than a hopeful demonstration.

Define what flushing is intended to remove

New assemblies can contain debris from manufacture, transport and installation. Opening a circuit during service can introduce additional contamination.

Parker's filtration handbook identifies construction and service activities among the sources of hydraulic contamination. The practical lesson is that new equipment and new fluid should not be assumed to produce a clean assembled circuit automatically. Preparation needs to address the complete installation.

Do not confuse particle removal with changing material compatibility. Flushing an oil component with water does not establish that its seals and moving contacts are suitable for water service. Likewise, a visually clear discharge does not prove that the circuit meets a defined cleanliness requirement.

Separate the flushing arrangement from normal operation

An approved flushing method may use an arrangement different from the final operating circuit. The responsible team must determine how sensitive components are protected and which flow paths are actually cleaned.

The temporary arrangement must also suit the pressure, materials and fluid involved. Authorized work arrangements must cover the temporary configuration as well as the completed installation.

Document temporary changes so they can be removed or restored deliberately. A bypass, temporary filter or disconnected instrument that remains after the intended preparation can alter operation. Pressure and mechanical loads can persist, so any investigation or adjustment must follow the site's authorized arrangements. CCOHS guidance provides the broader context for recognizing those energy sources. The familiarity of water should never be mistaken for absence of hydraulic energy.

A permanent design change needs a different record from a temporary flushing connection. Identify the revised requirement, the accepted setting or component, and who approved the change within the project.

Use the accepted water and handling route

The working fluid should match the specification for the actual equipment. Do not introduce a new mixture simply because it is used successfully in another plant system.

Handling can compromise an otherwise acceptable supply. Containers, transfer hoses and open connections should meet the preparation requirements established for the installation. The filling route should provide the agreed contamination control without creating an unsuitable pump-supply condition.

Keep fluid identity clear when several water qualities or chemicals are used at the site. A labelled connection and concise record can prevent confusion between process water, treated supply and an approved hydraulic working mixture.

Verify the pump's supporting conditions

The pump's ability to deliver flow depends on adequate conditions at its own connections. A correct outlet connection does not compensate for a supply or casing arrangement that falls outside the manufacturer's basis.

Measurements should be made at locations that represent the requirement being checked. A remote upstream pressure can differ from the condition at the pump during demand.

Record the conditions throughout the required duty, not only before the motor starts. Changing flow demand or fluid temperature may reveal an issue that a static check does not.

Manage air through the equipment-specific method

Air in a hydraulic circuit can affect stiffness, response and the interpretation of movement. It may make behaviour appear inconsistent or delay the relationship between commanded flow and actuator motion.

Do not treat opening a high-pressure connection as a generic educational bleeding technique. The commissioning team must use the provisions and authorized procedure established for the machine, with the relevant loads and energy sources controlled.

The useful learning point is to recognize air as one possible contributor to unusual start-up behaviour without assuming it explains every symptom. Record what is observed and under which state, then investigate through the approved method rather than repeatedly cycling the machine in the hope that an unexplained condition will disappear.

Introduce operating demand through agreed stages

A commissioning plan commonly progresses from basic checks toward representative duty, but the actual stages must be chosen for the machine.

Basic movement can establish direction and control response under suitable test conditions. Later checks can address the required load, timing and simultaneous functions.

Define abnormal observations that require review using the equipment limits and commissioning assessment. The response should be planned before testing so that schedule pressure does not encourage continued operation outside a condition the team understands.

Interpret pressure and motion together

Pressure alone does not describe the machine's function. A high pressure with little movement can indicate resistance, a blocked path or another circuit condition.

Record relevant actuator position or movement time together with the pressure observations.

The first start-up is a good opportunity to establish reference behaviour for later maintenance. Retain representative traces or observations from an accepted configuration.

Check thermal behaviour over a meaningful period

A short run may finish before temperature approaches its normal operating condition. Water's ability to carry heat does not remove the need for an adequate heat-rejection path.

Observe where heat is generated and where it can leave the system. The interpretation may involve the operating schedule, control losses or cooling arrangement.

Record ambient conditions and the machine state with the temperature measurements.

Finish with acceptance evidence and a clear handover

Commissioning is complete when the required installed functions and conditions have been checked and accepted by the responsible parties. ISO 4413 provides general hydraulic-system safety context, while the application and component documents establish the specific acceptance needs. Any outstanding item should have a clear status and owner.

The handover should identify the working fluid, component configuration, relevant operating observations and ongoing checks.

A concise commissioning record can distinguish preparation complete, basic function demonstrated and required duty accepted.

For an unresolved acceptance item, identify the missing evidence and the decision it prevents. Operational release remains a decision for the authorized parties; a deadline or an otherwise successful demonstration does not resolve the technical gap.
