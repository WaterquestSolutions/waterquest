Big Pump, Fine Control: Precision Lives in the Valve

Size the pump for demand and check the control valve, friction and feedback for precision. Water’s stiffness is one useful part of the motion-control design.

Specification & Design6 min read

Download the worksheet for this guide (editable text) · Open the pump requirement register (CSV)

Written by WaterQuest Solutions · Updated 2 November 2026 · About the distributor

Give the pump a clear flow demand and control target

Begin with the useful motion or process output. Identify maximum demand, minimum controlled demand and the time spent between them.

Translate the process into a flow requirement

For a cylinder, required volume follows effective area and travel. Dividing by movement time gives an ideal average flow before leakage and transient effects. The calculation must use the actual direction and geometry. The linked physics reference supports the basic pressure and area relationships underlying actuator behaviour.

An illustrative movement using 0.8 litres (0.211 US gal) in 8 seconds requires an ideal average supply of 6 litres per minute (1.59 US gpm). A second simultaneous consumer using 0.4 litres (0.106 US gal) over the same period adds 3 litres per minute (0.793 US gpm), giving 9 litres per minute (2.38 US gpm) total before allowances. A list of component sizes does not reveal when their demands overlap.

Use displacement and speed as a starting estimate

A positive-displacement pump's theoretical flow follows displacement per revolution times rotational speed. For an illustrative 4 cubic centimetres per revolution at 1,500 revolutions per minute, theoretical flow is 6 litres per minute (1.59 US gpm). The example is not a rating for a WaterQuest product.

Pressure, speed, temperature and fluid affect the delivered performance and required input. The Water Hydraulics Company's pump information includes configuration-specific performance context. Use the current curves or selection information for the proposed model rather than assuming theoretical flow is guaranteed at every pressure. It should be a documented selection decision, not an improvised adjustment after an undersized package reaches the site.

Check pressure and flow together

The pump must supply the required flow while supporting the pressure needed by the load and circuit losses. Maximum flow and maximum pressure on a data sheet may not define the same operating point for the complete offered assembly.

Hydraulic power provides a useful cross-check. At 100 bar (1,450 psi) and 9 litres per minute (2.38 US gpm), ideal hydraulic output is 1.5 kilowatts (2.01 hp) using pressure times flow divided by 600. This independently calculated example is not an equipment selection.

Low-flow control depends on the complete circuit

Precision at small movements often depends more on the valve, actuator and feedback than on maximum pump capacity. Friction, valve deadband, leakage, compliance and sensor resolution can all influence response. The design should identify the minimum controllable flow and the motion quality required under representative loads, rather than assume that capacity guarantees precision.

The Water Hydraulics Company's control-valve information distinguishes functions used to manage direction, pressure and flow. The chosen valve must operate appropriately over the required range and with the available pressure conditions. A pressure reading cannot establish position, and an actuator position may not fully represent a flexible downstream mechanism. For demanding control, pump selection is one part of an integrated motion design whose performance must be demonstrated at the installed system.

Evaluate the range between minimum and maximum demand

A machine may require fast unloaded travel, slow loaded approach and long holding periods. Selecting only for the largest flow ignores most of that operating pattern.

Do not assume that a variable-speed drive can provide every desired flow down to an arbitrarily low speed. The pump has a supported operating range, and system response may impose additional limits. This makes the tradeoff transparent to the owner and avoids a package that works well at maximum demand but behaves poorly during the precise movement that matters most to the process.

Protect the inlet at the selected flow

Higher delivered flow requires a supply arrangement that maintains the selected pump's inlet conditions. Pipework, restrictions, source level and temperature affect the pressure available while water is moving. A pump cannot compensate for inadequate inlet supply simply because its motor has unused power capacity.

Current manufacturer instructions govern the exact inlet and return configuration. The P3 information illustrates why different supply arrangements and fluid conditions need attention. For the project, obtain the applicable details and verify them at the installed equipment. A supply that supports one unit in isolation may not support the complete sequence.

Compare energy over the operating schedule

A pump's efficiency at one point does not establish annual energy consumption. The DOE's pumping-system guidance encourages examination of the complete system and demand pattern. Use that approach to identify where energy is used and which changes would affect useful service.

When comparing alternatives, hold the required process output constant and include necessary auxiliaries. Report the measurement or calculation boundary and any assumptions about cycle frequency.

Test the difficult points as well as the convenient one

Acceptance should include the states most likely to reveal a mismatch: maximum simultaneous demand, minimum controlled motion, reversals, restart and relevant loaded conditions. Record pressures, flow or movement, temperature and commands as appropriate.

Measurement uncertainty matters when judging small differences. Define instrument locations and the quantity being assessed. If the system cannot meet a point in the required envelope, identify the limiting mechanism before changing equipment.

Select a supported range rather than a headline number

A complete enquiry should identify required flow and pressure over time, minimum controllable demand, fluid, inlet conditions and available utilities.

The professional answer may combine both, provided the system is designed for the full duty. Water hydraulics offer a useful option where the required service can be delivered while removing mineral oil from the selected circuit.
