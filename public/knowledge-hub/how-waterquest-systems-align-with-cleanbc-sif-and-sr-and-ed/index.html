Funding a Water Hydraulic Project: Programme and R&D Eligibility

Connect the project scope to the right funding assessment.

A clear engineering project is easier to assess for funding or R&D support. Learn how to separate equipment purchase, development work and technical uncertainty, then organise the evidence for the relevant programme.

Article 100Economics & Funding10 min readUpdated 19 October 2026

Separate deployment from development work

Buying a proven component, integrating a package and investigating an unresolved technical question are different kinds of project work. Describe each part of your water hydraulic proposal and its costs clearly. That helps programme advisers assess the relevant funding or R&D route and gives the engineering team a useful record of what it is trying to establish.

This article concentrates on distinguishing development from deployment. Programme names in an older project discussion or web address should not be read as confirmation that a current funding stream is open or that an application qualifies. The owner should verify current criteria for the actual work and entity. A useful funding strategy follows the engineering plan; it does not invent a research narrative after an equipment purchase has already been made.

Routine adoption can be valuable without being research

Installing a supported water-hydraulic package may be new to the customer while relying on established engineering knowledge. Selecting components, checking loads and commissioning a known arrangement can require considerable skill without necessarily constituting experimental development. The distinction is not a judgement about the value of the work. It concerns the purpose and nature of the activity under the programme being considered.

A project team should therefore describe routine adoption confidently and accurately. If the business objective is to remove oil from a particular circuit, the equipment case can stand on that benefit and the required mechanical performance. There is no need to call the purchase research to make it worthwhile. Misclassifying ordinary work can undermine an otherwise sound funding enquiry and make later records difficult to defend.

Identify a technological uncertainty precisely

A possible development project needs a technical question more specific than whether the team can make the installation work. Describe the limitation in the available knowledge and the result that cannot yet be established through ordinary application of that knowledge. The uncertainty should relate to science or technology, rather than price, supplier availability, customer preference or the team's lack of familiarity with a product.

CRA's SR&ED guidance distinguishes advancement and systematic investigation from routine work. [2] An engineering team can support an assessment by recording the starting knowledge, the unresolved relationship and the proposed way to investigate it. The final eligibility conclusion belongs to the applicable review process. The purpose of the description is to make the work understandable, not to insert programme terminology into a project that lacks the underlying technical substance.

Frame a testable hypothesis

A useful hypothesis connects a proposed mechanism with an expected result. For example, a development team might investigate whether a particular interface design can meet a defined performance requirement under conditions not supported by the available knowledge. The plan should explain why that relationship is uncertain and what evidence would support or challenge the proposed explanation. It should not simply state that a prototype will be built.

The test design then follows the question. Identify the variables to control, the quantities to measure and the conditions that distinguish competing explanations. A result showing that an approach does not work can still be technically informative if the investigation produces a reasoned conclusion. Keeping that logic visible helps the team learn efficiently and gives an adviser a clearer basis for assessing the character of the work.

Separate experimental work from routine verification

A factory acceptance test often checks whether supplied equipment meets an established specification. An experiment investigates a technical uncertainty. Both involve measurement, but their purposes differ. Calling every commissioning measurement an experiment does not change the underlying activity. Conversely, a genuine investigation can include routine supporting tasks that need to be identified and assessed in relation to it.

Build the project schedule so the distinctions remain visible. Mark which tests answer a development question and which confirm ordinary installation requirements. Keep the resulting records separate enough to explain their purpose. This benefits engineering even without funding: the team can see whether it has resolved the uncertainty or merely demonstrated that the current prototype operates under one convenient condition.

Design records around the investigation

Record the initial question, the proposed explanation, the test method, observations and conclusions as the work proceeds. Preserve important design revisions and explain why they were made. A drawing change without reasoning may show what the team built but not what it learned. Failed approaches should not disappear from the record merely because they are absent from the final product.

Measurements need enough context to be interpretable. Identify configuration, load, fluid condition, temperature and relevant instrument information where they affect the question. Avoid collecting large volumes of data without a plan for analysis. The objective is a coherent technical account that connects evidence to conclusions. That account is more useful than a retrospective statement that the project was innovative because it took longer or cost more than expected.

Keep development and deployment costs distinguishable

A project may move from investigation to a commercial installation, and the cost record should show that transition. Separate experimental assemblies, routine purchased equipment, installation and normal operations according to their actual purpose. Do not assume that every invoice associated with the project belongs to the same eligible category. The applicable programme or tax rules determine treatment.

Establish a practical method for recording staff time and subcontracted work while activities occur. The method should reflect what people did rather than allocate a convenient percentage after completion. Finance and engineering should agree the work-package structure early so that technical records and expenditures can be connected. This reduces administrative reconstruction and makes the project easier to manage regardless of the eventual funding outcome.

Understand the role of NRC IRAP

NRC IRAP provides advice and, in some cases, funding for eligible businesses pursuing technology-driven innovation. Its current guidance describes business and project assessment rather than a universal equipment rebate. [1] A preliminary enquiry should therefore explain the development objective, team capability and intended commercial outcome, with clear separation between established equipment purchases and the innovation work.

An advisory discussion or invitation to submit information is not a funding commitment. Keep that status accurate in the project budget. The applicant should also understand what work can proceed before any agreement and which timing rules apply, using current programme instructions. Supplier participation can provide technical facts, but it does not replace the programme's assessment of the applicant and project.

Municipal deployment has a different logic

A municipal owner may be seeking a study, pilot or capital renewal that improves a public service. FCM's current funding resources can help identify potentially relevant routes, but the specific programme criteria must be checked. [3] A municipal demonstration is not automatically equivalent to a private company's product-development programme, even when both use similar hydraulic equipment.

The proposal should explain the service problem, alternatives, intended outcomes and evidence needed for a responsible decision. A pilot may investigate operating suitability or ownership requirements without necessarily constituting eligible research for a tax incentive. Keeping those distinctions clear helps each organisation pursue the route suited to its actual work. It also prevents a project partnership from assuming that one participant's eligibility automatically applies to every other participant and cost.

Property-based tax credits require separate screening

A development project and an equipment tax credit answer different questions. CRA's Clean Technology ITC guidance has specific property and claimant requirements. [4] [5] A hydraulic component does not qualify automatically because it uses water, removes oil or forms part of a project with an environmental objective. The actual function, ownership and applicable rules need review.

The finance model should therefore keep potential support streams separate until their treatment is assessed. Do not add a research incentive, contribution and property credit as though all apply to the same gross budget without interaction. Provide advisers with the factual work packages and assistance register. This makes the combined review more reliable and avoids an investment case that depends on several overlapping assumptions no programme has confirmed.

Let the experiment inform a real decision

A development plan should state what happens after the results are available. The outcome might justify another design iteration, a limited deployment or a decision that the proposed approach is unsuitable. Define the evidence needed for that decision in advance. Otherwise, testing can continue without a clear end while the team confuses activity with progress.

Commercial deployment should also preserve the boundary of what has been demonstrated. A successful test under one load and fluid condition is not proof of unlimited service life or suitability for every industry. The final technical report should state the supported result, remaining uncertainty and recommended operating or verification limits. This is essential for translating development knowledge into responsible product and application claims.

Build the funding case around honest technical work

A credible funding narrative explains the problem, the applicant, the activity and the evidence without exaggerating novelty. Routine equipment adoption should be presented as such. Genuine technical investigation should have a clear uncertainty, method and record. Programme screening then becomes a disciplined match to current criteria rather than an attempt to attach a fashionable label to every project cost.

WaterQuest can support the process with equipment information and a realistic description of the application questions. The applicant and its professional advisers remain responsible for eligibility and claims. The strongest result is a project whose engineering value is clear whether or not assistance is awarded: useful knowledge where development is needed, dependable machinery where deployment is appropriate and a transparent record connecting the two.

At closeout, retain a short account of what the team knew at the start, what the work established and what remains unresolved. That record helps future engineers avoid repeating unsuccessful approaches and prevents later marketing from converting a narrow test result into a broad promise. It is also a practical bridge between the development team and the people who will operate the delivered equipment.

From understanding to equipment selection

Use one technical baseline for different funding scenarios

Define the equipment duty, baseline operating costs and measurable project outcomes once. Assess each potential funding route against that documented scope, keeping unconfirmed assistance out of the base investment case.

Calculate the relevant duty

Use the lifecycle cost calculator to compare capital, recurring service, replacement and residual costs over the same study period. Use the return-on-investment calculator to test payback and net present value using your own costs, benefits and uncertainty allowance.

Connect the calculation to equipment
