Installing Water Hydraulics Aboard

Define the vessel function, keep a clean freshwater circuit inside and salt-proof materials outside, settle approval early and plan water supply for the voyage.

Testing & Adoption6 min read

Written by WaterQuest Solutions · Updated 2 November 2026 · About the distributor

Bring oil-free actuation to a defined vessel function

This guide: Installation and voyage logistics

Vessel functions · Freshwater versus seawater · Coastal exposure

Describe the practical reason for considering water. This prevents the proposal from expanding into an unsupported claim that the entire vessel becomes oil-free or environmentally benign.

It explains the information an owner, naval architect, integrator and equipment supplier need to establish a credible scope. The aim is to connect the attraction of removing a hydraulic-oil inventory with the operational reality of keeping the vessel's required machinery available.

Describe the load across its operating envelope

A marine mechanism may see different forces as its geometry changes, as the vessel moves or as the handled load varies. A catalogue maximum load is not a complete description of the mechanism's demand.

Identify simultaneous functions and any priority between them.

Distinguish normal operation from exceptional or recovery conditions. Holding a load, moving it after loss of normal power and securing it for sea may be separate functions.

Separate the working water from the marine environment

Freshwater inside a hydraulic circuit does not mean the equipment avoids salt exposure. Conversely, equipment designed for external marine exposure is not automatically supported for seawater flowing through its internal clearances.

Prepare two exposure descriptions: one for the working fluid and another for the outside of the assembly.

Outokumpu's corrosion guidance explains that stainless behaviour depends on the material and environment. Apply that principle to the complete assembly, including less visible internals. Published manufacturer information demonstrates that different fluid and construction options exist; it does not establish that every option is interchangeable.

Fit the package into the vessel's systems

Installation planning must consider available electrical power, protection arrangements, control interfaces and the effect of the package on surrounding equipment. A compact unit can still be difficult to install if the available space does not permit suitable connections or access.

Review the complete pipe and hose routes. A tidy equipment-room layout does not resolve an unsuitable route to a remote deck actuator.

Coordinate the arrangement with the vessel's existing services. Access to an essential valve or electrical enclosure should not be lost behind the new equipment.

Make the water arrangement practical for the voyage

A recirculating water circuit still needs a defined fill source, condition management and a way to deal with losses or service work. The crew must be able to obtain the accepted water and understand when its condition requires attention.

Identify whether the equipment uses freshwater, treated water or a specifically supported seawater configuration.

Consider voyage duration, remote operation and lay-up.

Establish the approval route before procurement

Marine approval depends on the vessel, equipment function and applicable framework. Transport Canada's construction and equipment information identifies requirements that vary with vessel category and circumstances. Small-vessel guidance addresses another part of that landscape. The project team should determine which framework applies instead of assuming that a supplier's general marine description resolves the question.

Classification requirements, owner standards and the role of the particular machinery may introduce additional evidence needs.

Request configuration-specific evidence. A certificate for another model, fluid, duty or manufacturing arrangement may not support the offered assembly.

Define holding and recovery without assumptions

The behaviour after loss of normal power should be a deliberate design decision. Depending on the mechanism, remaining in position, moving to a defined state or allowing controlled manual recovery may be required.

The design must identify where any required recovery energy comes from and how the load remains controlled.

Document indications as well as actions.

Stage installation around vessel availability

The installation program should account for the vessel's service commitments and the availability of the function being changed.

Where practical, resolve equipment identity, documentation and interface questions before the critical outage. Factory checks can reduce uncertainty, but they cannot prove the final vessel installation. The program still needs time for installed checks and for addressing findings without forcing an unsupported return to operation.

Agree how deviations will be handled. A different fitting, changed control arrangement or alternative fluid may appear minor during installation but alter the agreed design basis.

Test the installed function and teach the crew

Commissioning should verify the actual mechanism under the conditions required by the responsible designers and acceptance parties.

Crew familiarization should explain the parts that differ from the previous arrangement. This may include fluid identification, condition checks, filter indications and the correct response to abnormal behaviour.

Provide accessible documents and clear component identification. The crew should be able to distinguish an approved replacement from a similar-looking item and know how to obtain technical support.

Judge the marine benefit over the service pattern

The financial and environmental case should use the actual vessel duty. Consider installed cost, energy, service access, replacement logistics and the hydraulic-oil pathway removed.

Keep measured benefits separate from expectations. A short acceptance test can demonstrate function but cannot establish years of maintenance performance.

WaterQuest's strongest marine proposition is a supported water-hydraulic scope integrated into the vessel's real requirements.

A final handover review should confirm who owns each ongoing task. The equipment supplier may support components while the integrator maintains control documentation and the operator manages routine checks.

Where it fits
