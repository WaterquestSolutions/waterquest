Material Pairing for Water Hydraulic Tribology

See how the two sliding surfaces work together in water.

Friction and wear happen between two surfaces. Learn how to choose and assess material pairs for water service by considering contact load, movement, finish, alignment and the water between them.

Article 012Components & Materials10 min readUpdated 19 October 2026

Design the contact, not just the individual material

Friction and wear occur where moving surfaces meet. In water hydraulics, the material pair, finish, clearance and load determine how that contact behaves with the working water. Reviewing them together helps a pump, guide or valve maintain its intended function. This practical study of interacting surfaces is called tribology.

Two materials that are individually resistant to the water can still form an unsuitable sliding pair. Conversely, a successful pair may depend on a specific finish, geometry or fluid condition that is not obvious from the material names. The design therefore needs to describe both surfaces and the environment between them. Treating the polymer as the only active element, or treating the metal as merely a passive support, leaves the explanation incomplete.

This interface perspective is useful for both new equipment and failure investigation. It directs attention toward the conditions that changed rather than toward a preferred replacement material. If a component begins to wear after a repair, the relevant change might be alignment, surface preparation or contamination. Identifying that change can be more effective than selecting a more expensive grade without understanding the original mechanism.

Distinguish friction, wear and leakage

Friction resists relative movement and can contribute to the force or power needed to operate a component. Wear changes a surface through material removal or displacement associated with contact. Leakage is fluid movement through an unintended or controlled path. These phenomena can interact, but none is a direct substitute for measuring the others. A low-friction contact is not automatically a low-wear contact, and low measured leakage does not establish acceptable friction.

Consider a valve that becomes difficult to move. Possible explanations include increased contact force, contamination, deformation or an altered surface. Measuring leakage alone would not distinguish those possibilities. Similarly, a valve can move freely while an enlarged internal clearance reduces its ability to hold a hydraulic condition. The symptom must be connected to the function being evaluated before a remedy is selected.

A useful test plan therefore identifies separate acceptance measures. Movement force or input power can describe operating resistance. Dimensional inspection and surface examination can reveal wear. Flow measurement under defined pressure conditions can quantify leakage. The combination explains behaviour more clearly than a single claim that a material is “self-lubricating” or has a low coefficient of friction.

Describe how the surfaces are separated

Lubrication can involve a fluid film separating the principal surfaces, partial separation with some surface interaction, or conditions in which the surface contacts play a larger role. The actual regime depends on the design and operating state. An interface does not necessarily remain in one condition during starting, steady running, slowing and reversal. That is why a successful high-speed test may not describe a reciprocating component's most demanding event.

Water's relatively low viscosity makes the complete contact design important, but low viscosity alone does not prove that an interface cannot work. SKF's discussion of thin-film and mixed-media bearing applications illustrates the broader principle that materials, surface condition and operating specification must be developed together where conventional lubrication assumptions do not apply. It is background engineering guidance, not a rating for a particular water-hydraulic part. [3]

For a supplier review, ask which part of the duty governs the contact and how that conclusion was reached. A component that spends most of its time stationary may be governed by restart behaviour; another may accumulate wear during continuous running. Average speed and total operating hours are useful records, but they cannot replace a description of the actual sequence.

Define the material pair precisely

A meaningful pair description identifies the actual grades and the relevant condition of each surface. For a polymer composite, the formulation matters. For the metal counterface, the surface preparation and any treatment can matter alongside the bulk alloy. If the approved arrangement includes a coating or finish, a substitute should not be accepted merely because the underlying material is unchanged.

Victrex's tribology information is useful for understanding why performance depends on the contact system. Its properties guide provides separate data for identifiable polymer grades. Those resources support screening and analysis, while component testing establishes how a selected pair behaves in the actual geometry. A marketing label cannot combine those different evidence levels into a universal life prediction. [1] [2]

Keep the approved pairing identifiable in the parts record. If one member is replaced during maintenance, check the condition and identity of the other. Installing a new polymer insert against a damaged counterface may rapidly reproduce the problem. Equally, replacing a metal surface with an apparently similar alternative can invalidate the assumptions behind the retained polymer component. The pair is the relevant maintenance unit even when the parts are purchased separately.

Control contact geometry and alignment

The nominal load divided by a nominal area can be a useful first estimate, but real contact may not be distributed uniformly. Misalignment, deformation or an edge condition can concentrate load into a smaller region. A component that performs well in a carefully aligned test arrangement can therefore behave differently when installed on a distorted support or connected to a mechanism that imposes an unintended side force.

For a guide or bearing, establish how the load enters and how the component is supported. Identify the expected range of movement and any changes in direction. A drawing should show the features intended to maintain alignment, rather than leave the installer to infer them. Where the design permits movement through a joint or bearing, ensure that the installation does not inadvertently restrain it.

This geometry review also explains why clearance is a controlled variable. Too little can produce unwanted contact; too much can affect guidance or leakage. Temperature, manufacturing variation and exposure can change the available clearance. The designer needs a supported range that satisfies the component's functions, not a rule that a tighter fit is always better. Record the resulting limits so that repair work preserves them.

Give particles a route out of the problem

Particles can enter from the water supply, fabrication, maintenance or wear elsewhere in the circuit. Their effect depends on size, material, concentration and the vulnerable contacts they reach. A filter protects only the paths and conditions covered by its installation. Its presence does not establish that the assembled equipment was clean before startup or that contamination cannot enter downstream during a repair.

A sound plan combines compatible filtration with clean assembly and practical servicing. Protect open connections, identify approved replacement elements and provide the information needed to recognise a bypass or abnormal differential-pressure condition where applicable. Avoid changes that protect one contact while starving a pump inlet or creating another unsupported operating condition. Filtration has to fit the circuit architecture.

If wear debris appears, investigate whether it is a cause, a consequence or both. A particle can damage a contact, which then releases additional material and amplifies the problem. Replacing the visibly worn item and cleaning the local area may be insufficient if debris remains elsewhere. The recovery procedure should address the affected circuit boundary and the source that initiated the event.

Use an illustrative test matrix to expose assumptions

Imagine a proposed sliding pair for a freshwater valve. The development team needs to know whether it will move reliably after long dwell periods and continue controlling leakage after repeated operation. A useful test matrix would vary the conditions expected to influence those functions: supported water condition, temperature, representative pressure loading and the actual movement sequence. The test would also record the initial dimensions and surface state.

The purpose of varying conditions is to identify sensitivity, not to produce one attractive average. If movement resistance changes with temperature, that finding may affect actuator sizing or the permitted envelope. If leakage increases after cycling, inspect dimensions and surfaces to understand whether the result reflects wear or another change. Record exceptions instead of smoothing them into a claim of generally good performance.

The duration and sample size must suit the conclusion being drawn. A short successful trial can demonstrate initial functionality. It cannot, by itself, establish a long service life or a rare-event failure rate. State the evidence at the level it supports and describe the remaining verification work. A candid development result is more useful to a buyer than a numerical lifetime inferred from inadequate observations.

Investigate damage before changing the design

When a contact fails, preserve the relationship between the components and the operating history. Mark orientation, photograph the surfaces and identify where damage is concentrated. Compare the affected part with its counterface and with any retained baseline measurements. Information about water changes, repairs, unusual loads and idle periods can help connect the physical findings to a plausible mechanism.

Different patterns can suggest different questions, but visual appearance alone should not be treated as a final diagnosis. Seek appropriate examination where the consequence or uncertainty justifies it. The investigation should distinguish an unsuitable material pair from an assembly problem, an out-of-envelope event or contamination introduced elsewhere. Those findings lead to different corrective actions and different implications for other installed equipment.

Water hydraulics offer a valuable option where the required motion can be delivered without hydraulic oil in a sensitive location. Suitable material pairing is one of the enabling disciplines behind that option. WaterQuest's case is strongest when the proposed components have a defined operating envelope, identifiable service parts and evidence linked to the actual contact duty. The goal is predictable function supported by engineering, rather than a promise that one material combination makes friction and wear disappear.

Evaluate the counterface before choosing a polymer

Contact pressure, sliding speed, roughness and water condition act together at the interface. Request evidence for the proposed pair under the relevant duty rather than transferring a wear claim from an unrelated test.
