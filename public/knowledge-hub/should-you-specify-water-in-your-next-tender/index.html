Should You Specify Water in Your Next Tender?

Invite a water hydraulic option that can be assessed on its merits.

A tender is an opportunity to address oil exposure before the equipment is purchased. Learn when a water hydraulic option is worth including and how to write requirements that let suppliers make a meaningful offer.

Article 098Economics & Funding10 min readUpdated 19 October 2026

Give suppliers a clear route to propose water hydraulics

An upcoming tender is an opportunity to address oil exposure and fluid-servicing work while renewing equipment. State the required movement and the reason for considering water, then decide whether the tender requires it or accepts it as an alternative. Measurable performance and support requirements let suppliers respond with comparable, useful proposals.

Begin by documenting the problem. If hydraulic oil must be removed from a defined location, say why and identify the boundary. If the objective is lower energy or easier maintenance, those outcomes need their own requirements and evidence. Naming water without describing the duty can produce an incomplete offer, while a clear performance brief allows suppliers to explain the equipment and supporting services required to meet the owner's needs.

Write a duty schedule that suppliers can price

The schedule should identify each movement or function, including load, travel, speed, operating frequency and relevant environmental conditions. Describe simultaneous demands if several actuators share a supply. Separate normal operation from holding, standby and recovery. These distinctions influence equipment selection and prevent bidders from assuming different duty cycles under the same short description.

Where the load varies through travel, provide the available information or assign responsibility for establishing it. A maximum force figure alone may not describe the mechanism adequately. Include process interfaces, mounting constraints and any existing equipment intended for reuse. The more clearly the service is defined, the easier it becomes to compare a water-hydraulic offer with other arrangements without discovering major scope differences after the purchasing decision.

Define the oil-free boundary in operational terms

A useful requirement might state that the specified actuator circuit is to operate without hydraulic oil as its working medium. The scope should identify the relevant pump, reservoir, lines, controls and actuators. It should also clarify whether other lubricants in separate mechanical assemblies are outside the requirement. This avoids asking suppliers to meet an ambiguous whole-machine condition that the project has not actually defined.

The reason for the requirement should connect to the application, such as an identified contamination pathway. Avoid using oil-free as shorthand for harmless, sanitary or zero environmental impact. Canadian environmental-claims guidance reinforces the value of precise, supportable representations. [4] The tender should ask for evidence of the delivered condition and retain appropriate controls for working water and other remaining fluids.

Specify the working water and its variability

The bidder needs to know the proposed source, any treatment and the expected temperature and composition range. Identify the sampling location and date so that available analysis can be interpreted against the proposed connection. If information is missing, require the supplier to identify the data needed for confirmation. Do not let tap water become a substitute for a defined supply condition, especially where the site can change sources or where the circuit recirculates water for long periods.

The Water Hydraulics Company's pump information illustrates that supported operation depends on fluid and configuration. [2] A tender should therefore require confirmation for the offered assembly and identify responsibility for any additional treatment or supply equipment. The goal is a complete installed solution, not a compatible pump accompanied by an unpriced assumption that the owner will somehow provide the right inlet condition later.

Make reuse an evidence-based option

Retaining existing pipework, actuators or instruments can reduce cost and disruption, but physical fit is not proof of compatibility with a new medium. Ask bidders to identify every reused item and the evidence supporting it. Where an item cannot be confirmed, it should be marked for replacement or further assessment rather than silently accepted because it is already installed.

The conversion scope should also address the condition of retained equipment and residues from prior service. A tender that simply says reuse where possible leaves substantial interpretation to bidders and can make prices incomparable. A clearer approach defines the review process and the information to be provided. This allows the owner to understand whether a lower bid reflects a genuine reuse opportunity or an unresolved technical risk.

Separate control functions in the operating description

Directional movement, speed control, pressure protection, holding and isolation are different functions. The tender should describe the required behaviour without assuming that one named valve provides all of them. The operating sequence should identify commands, feedback and what occurs when the expected movement is not confirmed. This is especially important where hydraulic equipment interfaces with an existing control system.

Fault response also needs specificity. Holding position, moving to a designated position and stopping without support are not interchangeable outcomes. The responsible machinery or process assessment determines what is required. The public scope of ISO 4413 provides hydraulic-system context, but it does not establish the safety architecture of the project. [1] Assign integration responsibility so that essential behaviour is not left between the hydraulic and controls suppliers.

Ask for a complete scope matrix

List the equipment and services needed to make the installation operational: drives, pumps, actuators, valves, instruments, pipework, electrical work, supporting utilities, commissioning and training. Ask each bidder to identify included items, exclusions and owner-supplied interfaces. This makes commercial differences visible and reduces the chance that an apparent price advantage results from missing work.

The matrix should include physical access and service arrangements where relevant. Lifting points, removable panels, isolation access and space for component replacement can affect both installation and long-term cost. A compact layout is not automatically a maintainable one. Bidders should demonstrate how the offered arrangement fits the site and how routine work can be performed, rather than leaving those details to be resolved after equipment has been manufactured.

Request performance evidence at relevant conditions

A tender should identify the performance that matters and the evidence needed to support it. Motion time, useful force, input energy, leakage or other quantities must be tied to a defined operating condition and measurement boundary. A catalogue maximum or a best-efficiency point may be useful background without representing the required duty.

Distinguish guaranteed performance from estimates and reference data. If a proposed package relies on a test from a different configuration, ask the bidder to explain its relevance and limitations. System-level pumping guidance supports evaluating complete energy demand rather than only pump efficiency. [3] The purpose is not to demand an impractical volume of documentation, but to obtain evidence that can actually influence the selection and later acceptance of the equipment.

Define acceptance before bids are evaluated

Acceptance criteria should be connected to the duty schedule and operating description. Identify which checks occur before shipment and which depend on the installed mechanism or process. State how representative conditions will be established and how deviations from the design condition will be handled. This prevents later disagreement about whether a short demonstration has satisfied a broader requirement.

The tender should also identify the required records: final drawings, equipment identification, settings, service instructions, test results and training. Deferred verification needs an owner and a completion condition. A package should not be considered fully handed over merely because it moves while critical alarms, documentation or recovery provisions remain unresolved. Clear acceptance requirements protect both the owner and supplier by making completion reviewable.

Evaluate ownership information consistently

If lifecycle cost is part of selection, give bidders a common operating schedule and evaluation basis. Ask for the service tasks, consumables and supporting requirements of the offered configuration. Avoid comparing a detailed maintenance schedule from one bidder with a vague low-maintenance statement from another as though they provide equivalent information.

The owner should distinguish costs that genuinely change from shared facility expenses that remain. Uncertain repair or incident costs should be treated through transparent scenarios where reliable data are absent. Environmental benefit can also be evaluated separately where appropriate rather than forced into a speculative monetary saving. A consistent method makes the recommendation easier to explain and encourages suppliers to provide useful evidence instead of the largest unqualified percentage improvement.

Provide a route for clarifications and alternatives

A well-written tender can still contain uncertainty. The procurement process should provide an appropriate route for technical questions and any permitted alternatives, consistent with the owner's rules. Engineering responses should clarify the requirement without quietly giving one bidder a different duty. Keep the interpretation and resulting scope changes visible through the authorised process.

Where a water-hydraulic alternative is proposed, ask for a concise difference schedule showing equipment, utilities, operation and acceptance implications. That is more useful than a broad promotional comparison. It lets the review team see what changes and whether the alternative still satisfies the required service. If the technology requires a condition the site cannot provide, the issue can be resolved before contract award rather than becoming a commissioning surprise.

Specify water when the case is clear and supported

Water hydraulics belong in a tender when the proposed medium and machinery address a defined project need and the requirements can be stated and verified. In some cases, an explicit water-service requirement is justified. In others, allowing an evidence-based alternative may produce a better comparison. The responsible owner determines the procurement approach; the engineering task is to make the function, boundary and evidence clear.

WaterQuest can help translate an application into a supported equipment proposal. The strongest specification invites a concrete answer: what will be supplied, what conditions it needs, what work it performs and how the owner will know it has been delivered correctly. That gives engineers and procurement managers a better foundation than a tender built around either a technology slogan or an old equipment description copied without reconsideration.

Before release, have the duty owner, maintenance team and project integrator review the same scope matrix. Their agreement on interfaces can prevent more disruption than adding another page of generic product descriptions. The final document should leave suppliers with a common understanding of the job and reviewers with a practical way to compare their answers.
