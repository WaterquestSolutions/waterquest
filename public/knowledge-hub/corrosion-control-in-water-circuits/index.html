Corrosion Control in Water Circuits: Design It Out From the Start

Good materials and layout reduce pitting, crevice and galvanic corrosion risks. Learn how each mechanism develops and what to inspect when a deposit appears.

Components & Materials7 min read

Written by WaterQuest Solutions · Updated 2 November 2026 · About the distributor

Find the cause of corrosion before replacing the part

A brown mark may come from deposited contamination, a nearby carbon-steel component or attack of the stainless surface itself.

This guide: Design prevention

In-service investigation · Material compatibility

For water hydraulics, corrosion is both a structural and a functional concern. A pressure boundary must retain its integrity, but a valve can become unreliable long before a wall is perforated.

The practical starting point is to describe what is deteriorating, where it is happening and when it becomes noticeable. Photograph the component before cleaning it.

Define the water that will actually reach the equipment

“Runs on water” is not a complete fluid specification. A laboratory result from the supply connection also does not necessarily represent the fluid in an operating reservoir.

Separate the supply condition from the operating condition. Record the water source, the expected temperature range, how much new water enters the circuit and where samples can be collected. Chloride, pH, conductivity and particle content may all be relevant, but no single generic threshold proves that the complete assembly is suitable. A conductivity reading, for example, indicates ionic conduction; it does not identify which dissolved substances are present.

Purpose-designed water equipment provides a useful starting point because its manufacturer can define the intended medium and operating envelope. The Water Hydraulics Company's P3 information is an example of product documentation that directs users to consider water quality. It should not be read as approval for an arbitrary seawater, recycled-water or chemical-cleaning duty. Obtain confirmation for the actual fluid and configuration rather than extending a freshwater statement to a different exposure.

Understand why a stainless surface can still suffer local attack

Stainless steel owes much of its corrosion resistance to a thin passive surface film. Its behaviour depends on the alloy and the environment, rather than the word “stainless” alone. Both deserve attention in assemblies containing joints, seals and small stagnant spaces.

This changes how inspection findings should be interpreted. A broad superficial stain and a small deep defect cannot be ranked by visible area alone.

Consider a small branch used only for an occasional pressure measurement. During normal operation, the main line receives moving water while the branch may remain stagnant. The main reservoir sample describes the bulk fluid; it cannot fully describe conditions inside that branch.

Select materials using the complete service condition

A material schedule should identify actual grades and product forms. “Marine stainless” is a commercial description, not a sufficient acceptance criterion. Neither 316 nor duplex is a universal answer. The selection needs to account for the water chemistry, temperature, joints, fabrication and maintenance conditions that the component will experience.

A useful purchasing question is what evidence supports the proposed material at the most demanding condition. Laboratory results for clean, properly prepared material do not automatically describe a fabricated assembly with deposits, welds and crevices.

For a procurement manager, this distinction prevents false equivalence between quotations. Two offers may both say “stainless wetted parts” while differing in small internal components, fabrication controls and documented limits.

Trace galvanic connections across assembly boundaries

Galvanic corrosion requires an electrochemical relationship between connected materials in an electrolyte. A hydraulic skid cannot be assessed solely by checking that its principal housing is stainless. The relative exposed areas and actual environment influence the severity of the resulting attack; a simple list ranking metals cannot establish the service life of the assembled system.

The inspection task is to follow interfaces. Identify dissimilar wetted metals, determine whether they are electrically connected and consider which member would be exposed to preferential attack. The response may involve selecting a compatible replacement or providing a properly engineered isolation arrangement.

Isolation must also respect the component's other functions. A pressure connection has mechanical and sealing duties, and electrical bonding can have a separate safety purpose. An improvised fitting that interrupts one corrosion path while weakening a pressure boundary or defeating required bonding is not an improvement. Resolve the interface as a complete assembly and retain the approved arrangement in the maintenance documentation.

Keep fabrication and repair from creating a new exposure

Good material selection can be undermined by contamination, poor surface condition or unsuitable repair practices.

During a repair, distinguish the cleaning needed to remove loose contamination from treatments intended to alter a metal surface. Do not introduce a cleaner, inhibitor or biocide simply because it is familiar from another water system. Its suitability must include the elastomers, polymers, bearings and downstream destinations involved in this circuit, as well as the metal being cleaned.

Assembly records need not become an administrative burden. Record the component identity, approved consumables, unusual findings and any deviation from the intended procedure.

Plan for shutdowns as well as operating hours

A circuit that operates for a few minutes each month can present a different exposure from one that circulates throughout a production shift. Microbiologically influenced corrosion is a possible mechanism, but visible slime or a positive microbial result alone does not prove that it caused a particular metal defect.

The preservation plan should state what happens when equipment is left idle, how it is returned to service and who authorizes the transition. Draining a reservoir does not establish that every valve cavity and low point is empty. The equipment manufacturer's instructions and the actual geometry must guide the procedure.

Maintenance intervals should respond to evidence. A universal monthly or annual interval cannot account for every water source, operating schedule and access condition.

Use an inspection map to turn observations into action

Imagine a freshwater power unit operating a gate beside a treatment channel. Its reservoir, pump, distribution line and actuator each provide a different inspection opportunity.

Each observation should lead to a specific next step. New particulate contamination may justify identifying the particle source and checking affected components.

The commercial benefit of this approach is a more dependable decision. Water hydraulics can remove hydraulic-oil inventory from a sensitive location, but the owner still needs equipment that performs its duty. That is a stronger argument than promising that stainless steel can never corrode.
