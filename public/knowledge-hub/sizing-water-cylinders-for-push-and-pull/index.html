One Cylinder, Two Forces: Sizing Water Actuators for Push and Pull

A 63/36 mm cylinder gives 31 kN out and 21 kN back at 100 bar. Check both directions, the rod's buckling length and the mounting.

Match the cylinder to the whole movement

This guide: Mounting and selection

Net force ·

Water service adds a complete compatibility requirement. The body, rod, seals, guidance and internal materials must support the specified fluid and operating conditions.

Calculate both effective areas

The cap-end effective area of a conventional single-rod cylinder is based on the bore. The rod-end effective area is the bore area minus the rod area. The linked physics reference explains the ideal relationship; actual selection requires the installed conditions and appropriate design allowances.

An illustrative 63 millimetre bore with a 36 millimetre rod has a cap-end area of about 3,117 square millimetres and a rod-end area of about 2,099 square millimetres. At 100 bar (1,450 psi) with negligible opposing pressure and friction, the ideal forces are approximately 31.2 kilonewtons extending and 21.0 kilonewtons retracting. They show why a statement that a cylinder provides 31 kilonewtons is incomplete unless the direction and pressure conditions are also stated.

Include opposing pressure and circuit losses

The return chamber is not necessarily at zero gauge pressure during motion. Valves, hoses, elevation and the return arrangement can create opposing pressure that reduces useful force.

This matters particularly when a larger cylinder is selected to solve a force problem. Its greater return flow can increase losses in an unchanged return path.

Relate stroke volume to movement time

Cylinder speed depends on the flow entering the relevant effective area, subject to leakage and transient effects. The volume needed for a stroke follows from area times travel. If movement time is fixed, the pump and valves must support the corresponding flow.

Using the illustrative 63 millimetre bore and a 300 millimetre stroke, cap-end displacement is about 0.935 litres (0.247 US gal). Completing that ideal extension in 10 seconds requires about 5.61 litres per minute (1.48 US gpm). The circuit must also accommodate the return flow in each direction. These teaching calculations help define the enquiry, but the actual supplier selection must include component behaviour, load and operating conditions.

Check the rod and mounting mechanically

A rod under compression may require a buckling assessment based on length, end conditions and loading. A nominal pressure rating does not establish that a long slender rod is suitable for every pushing duty. A cylinder should not be expected to serve as the primary guide for a mechanism unless the design specifically supports that role.

The mounting loads must be transferred into a suitable structure. Pins, brackets and supports need assessment through the full stroke, including changes in angle and reaction.

Define the water and external environment separately

The hydraulic fluid may be controlled freshwater while the rod and body face washdown, salt spray or process chemicals. These are separate exposures and may govern different construction choices. A statement that the installation is marine or industrial does not provide enough information to select every material and seal.

Parker's cylinder catalogue illustrates that fluid service can influence construction and sealing options. This supports the need for an exact water-service configuration; it does not authorize substitution into an unrelated cylinder. Purpose-designed water equipment likewise uses a defined operating envelope. Request the complete approved assembly and service instructions. Do not assume that a stainless body proves compatibility of internal components or that an elastomer's general water resistance establishes suitability at the required pressure, speed and temperature.

Match the sealing and guidance to motion quality

A cylinder for occasional end-to-end movement may have different priorities from one required to move slowly and settle at intermediate positions. Friction, leakage and guidance affect that behaviour. Specify the minimum controlled speed, repeatability and holding requirement where they matter. A large maximum-force margin does not guarantee smooth movement. The control valve and feedback arrangement must be considered with the actuator rather than added after the cylinder has been selected solely for static capacity.

Long standby also deserves attention. The application may require dependable first movement after an extended period with no cycling. A rapid factory cycling demonstration does not establish that condition.

Plan the end of travel and the stopped state

Reaching the end of the stroke involves more than filling the last available volume. A moving load must decelerate, and the resulting forces must be managed.

The stopped state also needs a clear requirement. Must the mechanism hold position, move to a defined state after power loss or permit controlled manual recovery? A cylinder by itself does not establish those functions. Valves, mechanical restraints, stored energy and control logic may contribute according to the application. ISO 4413's public scope gives hydraulic-system safety context, while the project risk assessment and full applicable requirements govern the actual design. Keeping these functions explicit prevents a component purchase from being mistaken for a complete protective arrangement.

Make feedback reflect the required function

End switches, position sensors and pressure measurements answer different questions. A pressure rise does not prove that a load has moved, and a cylinder position does not always prove the final mechanism's position if a linkage can deflect or disconnect.

Define the response to a missing or inconsistent signal. The controls should distinguish a command from confirmation and provide useful fault information.

Purchase a maintainable, verifiable assembly

A complete enquiry includes load information, stroke, speed, mounting, working fluid, environment, control needs and required fault behaviour. Provide drawings or dimensions that show the actual mechanism. Ask for approved spare parts and current instructions tied to the offered configuration.

Acceptance should demonstrate the relevant loaded motion, feedback and control behaviour through an agreed plan. Retain final settings and as-installed documentation with the asset.
