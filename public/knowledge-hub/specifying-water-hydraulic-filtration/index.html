Specifying Filtration for Water Hydraulics: Beyond "10 Micron"

A β of 200 means 99.5 % capture at one stated size. Add capacity, pressure drop, location and compatibility, and clean water keeps a water system running for years.

Written by WaterQuest Solutions · Updated 2 November 2026 · About the distributor

Protect the small clearances that deliver useful performance

A real filtration specification

The Janus P3 page calls for 10 µm nominal pre-filtration (25 µm absolute) with β10 ≥ 75, and recommends return-line filtration for recirculating systems. At the stated particle size, a beta ratio of 75 corresponds mathematically to about 98.7% capture in the defined test. Check the filter’s test basis, capacity and pressure drop; do not equate the number alone with a complete filter specification. Janus P3 filtration guidance.

Begin by identifying which components require protection and where contamination can enter or be generated.

A micron number needs a performance definition

A filter described only as ten micron leaves important questions unanswered. The term may be used with nominal or absolute language that depends on the supplier's definition and test basis.

Parker's hydraulic-filtration handbook is a useful reference for filter terminology and the relationship between ratings and efficiency. For a water circuit, that background should be combined with explicit confirmation of water compatibility. A familiar oil-hydraulic filter specification cannot simply be transferred to a different medium without reviewing its element, housing, seals and operating conditions.

Beta ratio explains capture at a stated size

Beta ratio compares upstream and downstream particle counts for a stated particle-size threshold under the specified test. If the upstream count is 20,000 and the downstream count is 100, the ratio is 200. The corresponding capture efficiency is one minus the reciprocal of beta: one minus one two-hundredth equals 0.995, or 99.5 percent. This is a mathematical explanation, not a complete filter selection.

The result does not mean every particle of every size is removed. It also does not, by itself, describe dirt-holding capacity, pressure loss or performance throughout the element's life. The particle-size definition and test conditions remain part of the rating.

Efficiency and capacity answer different questions

Capture efficiency concerns the proportion of relevant particles retained under the stated conditions. Capacity concerns how much contamination the element can hold before reaching a defined limit.

The operating plan should consider both. Estimate the likely contamination burden from filling, makeup and internal generation, then select an arrangement supported for the duty.

Pressure loss changes as the element loads

Flow through a filter requires a pressure difference. The circuit must remain within the selected components' requirements as that happens, and the maintenance indication should provide useful information before the relevant limit is exceeded.

This is particularly important at a pump inlet. The Water Hydraulics Company's pump guidance illustrates why inlet arrangements and filtration requirements must be considered together for the chosen configuration.

Filter location determines what it protects

Incoming-water filtration can reduce contamination introduced during filling or makeup. Return-line filtration can address material circulating back from downstream equipment.

Do not assume one filter protects every component equally. Material generated downstream of a filter can reach another interface before returning through it. The designer must balance component requirements, pressure losses and service access, using the current instructions for the equipment rather than a universal arrangement applied to every water-hydraulic package.

Bypass behaviour belongs in the risk assessment

Some filter assemblies include a bypass arrangement, while others do not. Neither arrangement can be judged in isolation from the duty and the components being protected.

Identify the offered assembly's actual behaviour and the required response to an indication. Operators should know whether an alarm means planned attention is approaching or whether the equipment requires an immediate authorized response. The settings and procedures must come from the supported system design.

Water compatibility includes the whole assembly

The filter medium is only one part of the wetted equipment. Housing materials, seals, supports, adhesives where present and other internal construction also need compatibility with the working medium and conditions.

Saline service deserves explicit attention. The Water Hydraulics Company's desalination-pump documentation provides a dedicated filtration and flushing basis for that range. It should not be replaced by a filtration statement copied from a different freshwater product page.

Filtration does not remove every water-quality problem

Ordinary particle filtration does not establish that dissolved salts, hardness constituents or treatment chemicals are acceptable for the machinery. It also does not automatically establish microbiological suitability.

Define the purpose of each treatment stage.

Clean assembly reduces the load on installed filters

Contamination can enter through open connections, dirty tools, filling equipment and replacement components. Installed filters should not be expected to compensate for uncontrolled assembly practice.

Commissioning should establish a known fluid condition according to the responsible equipment instructions. Any cleaning or flushing method must suit the complete assembly, including materials and retained equipment. This article does not prescribe a universal procedure.

Interpret differential pressure in context

A rising pressure difference across a filter can indicate increasing restriction, but interpretation depends on flow and fluid condition.

An unexpectedly rapid change should prompt investigation of the contamination source as well as element replacement.

Make element replacement preserve the specification

A replacement that fits the housing is not necessarily equivalent. The capture rating, test basis, construction and compatibility need to match the approved requirement.

Service access should also be considered in the installation layout. The element needs to be replaced through an authorized process that controls pressure and avoids introducing new contamination. Filtration performance is therefore partly an operating-system issue: a technically correct element provides limited value if it cannot be serviced properly or if its replacement requirement is lost after handover.

Specify a complete cleanliness arrangement

A useful filtration specification identifies the protected components, required capture performance, compatible construction, location, pressure-loss limits and service indications.

WaterQuest can help owners interpret the selected equipment's requirements and assemble the relevant questions for the system designer.

Specify the water as well as the filter

Record source-water chemistry and the equipment’s filtration requirement. Do not add inhibitors, biocides or food-grade antifreeze without approval for the complete assembly; treatment changes the working medium.
