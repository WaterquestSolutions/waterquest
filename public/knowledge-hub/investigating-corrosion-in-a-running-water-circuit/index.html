Investigating Corrosion in a Running Water Circuit

Record where it is, compare against the original exposure, find the mechanism, then correct and monitor. Most issues trace to a fixable detail.

Written by WaterQuest Solutions · Updated 2 November 2026 · About the distributor

Preserve the conditions the materials were selected for

This guide: In-service investigation

Design prevention ·

This article focuses on an installed water-hydraulic circuit rather than on selecting a stainless grade from scratch. A claim that corrosion is prevented without coatings or chemicals should never be interpreted as immunity under all conditions.

Preserve the original exposure record

At commissioning, retain the selected materials, approved water condition, temperature range and relevant cleaning instructions.

The working-water description deserves particular attention. A site may switch supplies, add treatment or change a cleaning process without recognizing the effect on hydraulic machinery.

Distinguish staining from a condition assessment

A visible stain can be an early observation, but appearance alone does not establish the mechanism or remaining integrity of a pressure-containing part. The response should preserve information and obtain an appropriate assessment rather than immediately polishing away evidence or declaring the component safe from a photograph.

Record location, extent and operating context. Note whether the finding is internal or external, whether it appears under a support or deposit and whether it followed a change in service. A general educational article cannot determine whether a particular damaged component can remain in service.

Local conditions can differ from the bulk water

A sample taken from a convenient reservoir location may not represent conditions beneath a deposit or inside a stagnant crevice. Outokumpu's guidance explains the significance of corrosion mechanisms and environmental conditions in stainless applications.

For an installed circuit, this means investigation should follow the location of damage. Understanding where the mechanism operates is often the key to an effective correction that does not create unnecessary changes elsewhere in the system.

Deposits can create more than a cleanliness problem

Deposits may restrict flow, interfere with heat transfer or create local surface conditions different from the surrounding fluid.

Cleaning methods need compatibility review. A product that removes a deposit effectively may also affect seals, surfaces or other materials. The authorized procedure should identify the method, exposure and return-to-service requirements for the actual assembly.

Stagnation changes the operating story

An intermittently used circuit can spend long periods in conditions different from normal circulation. Local regions may retain water even when another part of the system is drained or refreshed.

Describe shutdown and storage conditions explicitly. Consider whether a seasonal asset remains filled, how the water condition is preserved and what is checked before restart.

External exposure can be the overlooked mechanism

The inside of a water-hydraulic assembly may operate with controlled fluid while its outside encounters washdown chemicals, salt spray, condensation or dirty standing water.

Access is important because a location that cannot be inspected or cleaned may retain a problem until it affects service. Record the external environment when ordering replacements so that a supplier does not assess only the internal hydraulic medium and miss a significant part of the service.

Dissimilar materials need context

Different metals connected within an electrolyte can create conditions relevant to galvanic corrosion, but the significance depends on the materials, geometry, exposure and electrical connection.

Maintenance can alter that arrangement through an apparently minor substitution. A fastener, fitting or replacement instrument may introduce a different material or surface condition.

Use water analysis to answer a defined question

Sampling is most useful when the investigation identifies what it needs to learn. A broad laboratory report can contain many values without resolving the question if the sample location, timing or handling does not represent the affected condition.

Compare results with the supported equipment basis and with earlier measurements where available. A changed supply or treatment stage can be more informative than a single result considered in isolation. Manufacturer water-pump guidance illustrates why the actual fluid should be identified when confirming configuration suitability. The objective is to understand the exposure reaching the machinery, not to collect paperwork that is disconnected from a practical decision.

Treat reference tables as screening tools

Corrosion tables can help identify potentially suitable materials, but they cannot represent every combination of flow, temperature, impurities, crevices and fabrication condition in a working circuit. Outokumpu explicitly discusses limitations when applying corrosion data to actual service. A favourable table entry should not be used to dismiss contrary evidence from the installed equipment.

When a problem appears, compare the test conditions behind the reference with the real exposure. The difference may explain why the initial assumption needs revision. A table alone does not choose among those corrective actions.

Close the investigation with a controlled correction

A useful investigation produces more than a replacement part. It identifies the most credible mechanism, the evidence supporting it and the action intended to prevent recurrence.

The replacement or repair must also preserve the hydraulic function. The public scope of ISO 4413 provides hydraulic-system context, but it is not a repair approval for a damaged assembly. Pressure-containing equipment should be assessed and restored through the appropriate authorized process.

Make corrosion management practical for the operating team

The strongest operating program is one people can carry out consistently. It identifies what to observe, where records are kept, which changes require review and who can assess an abnormal finding.

WaterQuest can help preserve the relationship between supported water conditions and the selected equipment. The broader lesson is that corrosion control is a maintained condition, not a one-time adjective attached to stainless steel. That supports dependable water-hydraulic service without relying on an unsupported promise that coatings, chemicals or inspection will never be needed under any circumstances.
