Renewing Municipal Hydraulics? Give Water a Fair Hearing

A defensible renewal compares credible alternatives, including water hydraulics, on service, exposure, cost and maintainability, and records why.

Use engineering judgement to improve the next municipal asset

A defensible renewal compares credible alternatives, including water hydraulics, on service, exposure, cost and maintainability, and records why.

This guide: Engineering decision record

Water hydraulics deserve consideration where they can remove a meaningful oil exposure while delivering the required municipal function. That is a credible option to investigate, not a moral test that predetermines the outcome.

The thought-provoking question is whether the project has genuinely reconsidered the inherited design. Has the working medium been assessed against the location, service and ownership needs, or has an old specification simply been repeated?

Begin with the service people depend on

Municipal machinery exists to provide a public function. The first design requirement is dependable delivery of that service under the conditions the asset must face.

Describe the required movement, load, availability and recovery needs in ordinary operational terms before selecting the technology.

An environmental improvement that undermines essential service would not be a satisfactory result. Conversely, service reliability should not become a reason to avoid examining a credible alternative indefinitely.

Examine the actual exposure around the asset

An oil-filled actuator above a channel creates a different question from one in an accessible contained equipment room.

This makes the water-hydraulic opportunity specific. A compatible water circuit can remove the selected hydraulic-oil inventory from an identified pathway.

Environmental assessment still needs to consider the proposed water release. ECCC's pollution-prevention guidance addresses substances and pathways that can affect relevant waters, rather than granting a universal exemption to anything described as water-based. The project should involve suitable expertise where needed and avoid implying that removal of oil ends every environmental responsibility associated with the installation.

Compare credible alternatives on a common basis

A fair options review begins with the same useful duty and relevant acceptance requirements. Compare complete arrangements, including power supply, controls, mechanical interfaces and service access.

Include a realistic improvement to the existing approach where appropriate.

The evaluation should identify which criteria matter and why. Reliability, exposure reduction, installed cost and maintenance capability may carry different importance at different assets.

Consider the people who will maintain the result

Operators and maintenance staff understand access, recurring problems and the practical consequences of an outage.

An unfamiliar technology needs understandable documents, correct spare parts and a credible support route. Those needs do not disqualify it. They identify work the project must complete so the municipality can own the asset confidently after the consultant and installer leave.

A design that saves a small capital amount but repeatedly consumes scarce maintenance capacity may not serve the public well. Equally, a more sophisticated package may add little value if the organization cannot support its complexity.

Treat sustainability as a whole-asset question

Removing a hydraulic-oil inventory can be a concrete improvement at a sensitive location. Energy demand, water use, materials, service life and replacement needs remain relevant.

EGBC's climate and sustainability resources support considering longer-term effects in professional practice. They do not prescribe water hydraulics as the answer. The engineer must apply judgement to the actual public function and document the reasons for the recommendation.

Where evidence is incomplete, state the uncertainty and identify whether it matters to the decision. A broad lifecycle claim without the necessary data is not an adequate substitute. The public interest is better served by a precise improvement that can be demonstrated than by an ambitious environmental statement that cannot be verified.

Make procurement open to evidence

Specifications should describe the required service clearly and explain any desired reduction in oil exposure.

Ask suppliers to identify their configuration, operating limits, service arrangement and supporting evidence. Distinguish measured results, calculations and commercial commitments. A proposal should be clear about which scope is supplied and which integration responsibilities remain with others.

Environmental representations deserve the same scrutiny as mechanical claims. Competition Bureau guidance emphasizes supportable and non-misleading claims. A candid account of a limitation should not be treated as less credible than an unsupported promise.

Use pilots where they resolve a real uncertainty

A pilot can be a responsible way to assess an unfamiliar arrangement, provided it answers a defined question.

Choose the trial boundary and acceptance criteria before operation. A brief demonstration can establish basic function but cannot by itself prove long-term reliability or approval for every similar municipal asset.

If the pilot reveals a limitation, use that information constructively. The result may justify a revised design or a different option.

Keep safety and continuity visible during renewal

Changing a hydraulic system can affect holding, emergency response and service isolation. The required behaviour of the complete mechanism must be assessed, even if the new components fit the existing connections. General hydraulic guidance such as ISO 4413 provides useful context, while the application determines the specific requirements.

Plan the transition from the old asset to the new one.

The owner should know who confirms the completed function and what evidence authorizes return to service.

Leave a decision that future teams can understand

The final record should explain the problem, alternatives, evidence and assumptions behind the recommendation. It should identify material uncertainties and the measures selected to manage them.

After installation, compare the relevant operating results with the expected benefit. Record actual duty and service experience so changes in demand are not mistaken for equipment performance.

WaterQuest's role is to contribute a credible technical option and useful evidence. It should not imply that declining its equipment is unethical.

Consider a hypothetical gate renewal above a receiving channel. The options review might find that water actuation removes an oil pathway with manageable service requirements. The technology choice alone does not reveal the quality of the professional judgement.

Cost transparency also matters to public trust. Explain which expenses are expected to change and which shared municipal costs remain.

The most useful challenge to established practice is therefore specific and answerable: what evidence would justify keeping the present arrangement, and what evidence would justify changing it? A project that asks both questions creates room for improvement while respecting the obligation to deliver dependable infrastructure with public resources.

Plan renewal by asset class

Group gates and actuators by load, movement, standby exposure and consequence of failure.
