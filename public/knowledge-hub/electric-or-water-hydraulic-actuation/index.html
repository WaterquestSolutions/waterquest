Electric or Water Hydraulic? Compare the Whole Duty

A screw turns torque into thrust much as a piston turns pressure into force. For high force, shock loads, holding and harsh environments, water hydraulics have a strong case.

Written by WaterQuest Solutions · Updated 2 November 2026 · About the distributor

Compare the complete route from power to movement

Water hydraulics are one hydraulic option, with materials and fluid management selected for the intended water duty. Their particular value can include removing mineral oil from a circuit where its presence creates a burden. The owner's decision concerns the complete installed service, not an isolated actuator displayed without the utilities and controls that make it work.

Translate the load through each mechanism

A hydraulic cylinder's ideal force follows pressure acting on effective area, adjusted for opposing pressure and mechanical resistance. An electric linear actuator often uses a screw or another transmission to convert motor torque into linear force. A large force rating does not imply that the actuator can produce that force at every speed or for every duration.

An independent ideal screw example illustrates the tradeoff. With a 10 millimetre lead, 10 newton metres of torque corresponds to about 6.28 kilonewtons of axial force if losses are ignored, using force equal to two pi times torque divided by lead. Real efficiency, friction and limits change the result. The example is not a product selection. The useful comparison must include both the required load and the speed at which it is delivered.

Check duty cycle and thermal limits

An actuator suitable for occasional isolation may not be suitable for continuous modulation or frequent reversals. Electric motors, drives and transmissions have thermal and mechanical limits that depend on the duty. A successful single stroke does not establish continuous production capability or repeated operation without adequate recovery time.

Ask for the conditions behind the offered rating. Ambient temperature, load, movement duration and frequency can influence allowable operation. A nominal motor power is not a complete duty description, and neither is a hydraulic maximum pressure.

Treat precision as an installed performance requirement

Electric actuation can offer useful direct control and feedback, while hydraulic actuation can provide effective motion control through suitable valves and sensors. Actual precision depends on backlash, compliance, friction, feedback location and controller behaviour. Define the accuracy, repeatability, minimum speed and settling behaviour the process needs.

Measure at the relevant mechanism. A motor encoder does not always establish the final driven position after a transmission or flexible linkage. This prevents the comparison from rewarding catalogue resolution that the installed mechanism cannot use or overlooking a simpler design that fully meets the required function.

Understand holding and back-driving behaviour

Some mechanical transmissions resist back-driving under certain conditions, while others can be driven by the load. Do not assume that every electric screw actuator is self-locking or that a brake automatically satisfies every protective requirement. The required stopped state must be defined for the actual load, duration and consequence of movement.

Separate ordinary position holding from a protective function. A mechanism that must remain secure during maintenance may require a different arrangement from one holding position during normal production. The risk assessment should identify what must be restrained, isolated or verified.

Plan how moving energy is absorbed

A moving load carries energy that must be managed when it slows or stops. An electric drive may dissipate or recover energy depending on its configuration and operating conditions.

An overrunning load deserves particular attention because removing drive power does not necessarily stop it. The complete control and protective arrangement must keep the motion within the required envelope. Comparing only steady force or torque misses the conditions that may govern both the equipment selection and the safety of the completed mechanism.

Compare distribution and installation space

A single remote electric actuator may be attractive because it needs suitable power and control connections without a hydraulic supply and return route.

Include access, supports, enclosures and field integration. Electric equipment needs suitable environmental protection and electrical arrangements.

Define the response to loss of normal energy

The process may require an actuator to open, close, hold or allow supervised recovery after a supply interruption. Springs, batteries, stored hydraulic energy or a separate manual arrangement may contribute, but their capacity and sequence must be verified. The presence of a backup component does not establish the complete function if the controls or mechanical path cannot use it under the initiating failure condition.

Hydraulic-system safety context is provided by ISO 4413's published scope, while the complete project must establish applicable requirements for both alternatives and their installation. Define readiness indications and the action required if the protective function is unavailable. Acceptance should address the relevant interruption through an approved test plan. A normal powered movement does not prove the loss-of-power response.

Compare energy and maintenance over the same calendar

Mechanical work is force times distance, and power relates to force times speed for a straight-line movement. The DOE's pumping guidance supports system-level energy assessment for the hydraulic option. Apply an equally complete boundary to the electric alternative rather than compare one device's input with another system's useful output.

Maintenance differs in content rather than disappearing. Electric transmissions may require inspection or service of screws, gears, brakes and seals according to their design.

Choose the arrangement that meets the complete duty

A useful comparison records the required behaviour, offered configuration, evidence and uncertainty for each option. Include installed scope, fault response and support.

WaterQuest should recommend water actuation where its hydraulic performance and removal of a defined oil inventory address the owner's needs. Electric actuation remains a credible alternative and may be the better fit for another duty.
