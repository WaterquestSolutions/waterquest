Component Spotlight: Armak Air Motors and Water Hydraulic Integration

Explore an air-motor drive for a defined water hydraulic duty.

An air motor can provide mechanical drive where compressed air is already available. Learn how its torque, speed and air demand must match the pump and the wider water hydraulic installation.

Article 070Components & Materials10 min readUpdated 19 October 2026

Connect the air supply to a useful water-pumping duty

An Armak air motor turns compressed-air energy into shaft rotation. Coupled to a suitably matched water pump, that rotation can provide a mechanical drive for a water hydraulic supply. The air motor and water circuit remain separate systems, linked by the shaft or transmission. Review their torque, speed and demand together to assess the complete package.

Armak's published range includes air motors and supporting equipment such as controls, gearing and brakes. [1] The relevant selection is the exact offered model and configuration. This article explains the integration questions; it does not claim that every Armak motor is compatible with every WaterQuest pump or that a particular combined assembly has been approved. A useful proposal must identify how the drive, coupling, pump and controls work together under the intended duty.

Draw the complete energy path

Compressed air arrives at the motor with a pressure and flow capability. The motor produces shaft torque and speed, which a coupling or gearbox transmits to the driven pump. The pump then supplies hydraulic pressure and flow to the water circuit. Each stage has losses and operating limits. A performance statement made at the motor shaft describes a different boundary from one made at the compressor's electrical input or the actuator's useful output.

This matters when comparing alternatives. An air-driven water pump may be attractive where a suitable air service already exists or where the drive arrangement solves a particular installation problem. Its total energy use still depends on generating and delivering the compressed air, operating the motor and driving the hydraulic load. Avoid treating available plant air as energy without cost. The owner should see both the local mechanical benefit and the complete utility demand so the arrangement can be compared fairly with an electric or other supported drive.

Match torque and speed to the pump duty

A pump needs sufficient input torque at the required rotational speed to deliver the intended pressure and flow. The drive selection must support the relevant operating points, including starting under the actual conditions. A motor's maximum torque and maximum speed do not necessarily occur together. Use the current performance information for the exact motor and pump rather than matching only their headline power ratings. The proposed operating point should be visible on the selection basis.

Mechanical power relates to torque and angular speed. In an independent teaching example, 10 newton metres at 1,000 revolutions per minute is approximately 1.05 kilowatts (1.41 hp) of shaft power. That arithmetic does not establish available starting torque, efficiency or a product rating. It shows why both speed and torque are needed. A drive with enough nominal power at an unsuitable speed may still require gearing or another configuration, and that transmission introduces its own limits and losses.

Consider starting and changing loads

The pump's demand changes with pressure, speed and operating conditions. Starting against a loaded circuit can differ from starting with an appropriate unloaded path. The air motor's behaviour also changes with its inlet and exhaust conditions. Define the intended sequence and obtain support for it. Do not assume that a motor's ability to stall or tolerate a changing load makes every coupled pump arrangement safe or suitable. The driven equipment still has its own operating requirements.

If the process requires controlled speed, evaluate how that speed is maintained as load and air supply vary. A simple air-flow adjustment may not provide the required regulation over every condition. Feedback or another control arrangement may be needed for the specific duty. The water circuit's required pressure and flow should be translated into the drive demand through the complete sequence. This allows the supplier to assess starts, steady operation and transitions instead of selecting a drive from one convenient point.

Evaluate the air supply at the motor

The pressure available at a compressor or remote header is not necessarily the pressure reaching the motor during demand. Distribution losses, hoses, filters, regulators and simultaneous plant use can affect the local supply. The motor's air consumption must be supported by the installation. Specify the conditions at the appropriate measurement point and the expected variation. A system that performs during an isolated demonstration may respond differently when other air users operate at the same time.

Air quality and preparation must follow the selected motor's instructions. Do not assume that all air motors share the same lubrication or moisture requirements. Armak's published accessories include air preparation and control equipment, which illustrates that the supply arrangement is part of the application. [1] Confirm the exact requirements and include the necessary supporting scope. If lubrication is required on the air side, describe it accurately rather than extending an oil-free water-circuit claim to every part of the drive system.

Include exhaust conditions and the working environment

An air motor discharges air after extracting useful energy. The exhaust arrangement can affect noise, local conditions and motor performance. Any silencer or exhaust routing must suit the selected motor and its permitted backpressure. A convenient location for the pump may not be a suitable location for untreated exhaust, especially near sensitive processes or personnel. Include the discharge path in the layout review rather than considering only the compressed-air inlet.

Environmental selection also includes ambient temperature, moisture, dust and any hazardous-area requirements. A manufacturer's range-level statement is not evidence that every assembled option is approved for a particular installation. Obtain the current documents covering the exact model, accessories and intended use. The complete coupled package may need its own assessment. This keeps useful product information from being stretched beyond its scope and gives the owner a clear record of which requirements have been addressed by the motor supplier and which remain with the integrator.

Engineer the mechanical connection

The coupling and mounting must transmit the required torque while maintaining the alignment and loads permitted by both machines. A pump shaft may have restrictions on radial or axial loading. A gearbox changes speed and torque while adding its own mounting, lubrication and service requirements. These are ordinary mechanical design questions, but they become easy to overlook when the concept is described simply as an air motor driving a water pump.

The Water Hydraulics Company's pump information includes installation considerations for mechanical drive arrangements. [3] Use current instructions for the selected model and coordinate them with the motor's requirements. Identify who supplies the adaptor, guard, coupling and support structure. Verify that the assembly can be installed and maintained without forcing alignment or placing unintended loads on either shaft. A successful integration should preserve the operating conditions of both components rather than rely on a coupling to compensate for an undefined mechanical arrangement.

Define controls across both fluid systems

The air-side control determines the drive's operation, while the water-side controls determine hydraulic pressure, flow and actuator behaviour. Stopping the air supply does not necessarily depressurise the water circuit or restrain a mechanically driven load. The combined sequence must identify how normal stop, emergency response and maintenance isolation are achieved. Avoid treating one shutoff valve as proof that every energy source in the assembly has been controlled.

ISO 4413's public scope provides context for the hydraulic side, but the complete installation also has pneumatic, mechanical and electrical considerations as applicable. [4] The responsible design and risk assessment should define the necessary measures. Control authority needs to be clear where the package connects to plant automation. Specify commands, permissions, feedback and the response to loss of each utility. This makes the integration understandable to operators and prevents an air-side event from producing an unexpected water-side state that was never considered during component selection. Identify air and water connections distinctly on the installed package. Clear labels reduce the chance of incorrect service connections and help technicians follow the two schematics without confusing a pneumatic control line with a hydraulic working line.

Compare energy at a consistent boundary

The energy required to supply compressed air depends on the generating and distribution system, including its controls and losses. A motor's local air consumption does not directly state the compressor electricity attributable to the duty. The DOE and NREL compressed-air evaluation guidance addresses system boundaries and measurement methods relevant to that assessment. [2] For an investment decision, use a consistent boundary when comparing the air-driven package with an alternative drive.

Include operating duration, idle behaviour and any change in compressor operation. A small intermittent demand and a continuous demand can have different consequences for the plant air system. Report calculated values as estimates until supported by appropriate measurements. The air-driven option may still have a useful operational advantage even if energy is not its strongest feature. State that advantage clearly, such as a supported installation arrangement or utility interface, rather than claiming efficiency from the mere fact that compressed air is already present at the site.

Verify the coupled duty before treating it as a package

A complete enquiry should provide required water pressure and flow, operating schedule, available air conditions, environmental requirements and the driven load. Ask for the exact motor, transmission, pump and control configuration. Acceptance should demonstrate the relevant operating points and transitions, with measurements on both sides where needed. A successful motor test and a successful pump test performed separately do not establish the behaviour of the coupled installation under its actual utility conditions.

WaterQuest can help assess whether an air-driven arrangement fits a particular water-hydraulic duty and identify the interfaces requiring support from the relevant manufacturers. The important distinction remains clear: the air motor supplies mechanical drive; the water pump supplies the hydraulic circuit. A persuasive proposal explains why combining them helps the application, how the complete energy and control paths work and what evidence verifies the result. That gives engineers, procurement teams and operators an informed basis for deciding whether the integration is worthwhile.

From understanding to equipment selection

Treat the air motor as a separate drive choice

If an air motor drives a water pump, match its speed and torque curve to the pump's demand and coupling. Compressed-air supply pressure and consumption belong in the drive assessment; the water motor calculator does not predict air-motor performance.

Calculate the relevant duty

Use the motor speed and torque calculator to relate water-motor displacement, flow, pressure and gearing to shaft speed and torque. Use the pump sizing calculator to estimate displacement, drive torque and shaft power from your flow, pressure and efficiency assumptions.

Connect the calculation to equipment

Review water-motor and pump operating data as separate component selections. If an air motor is the prime mover, obtain its air-service performance curve independently.

Explore water hydraulic motors and water hydraulic pumps.

What belongs in the ownership assessment of the combined drive?

Inspect the coupling and both utility supplies. Account for compressor energy and air-system servicing when comparing the complete arrangement with an electric drive.
