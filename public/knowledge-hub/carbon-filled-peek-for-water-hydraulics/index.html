Carbon-Filled PEEK: Engineering Sliding Parts for Water

Carbon-reinforced PEEK combines stiffness and low water uptake. A named grade, matched counterface and checked duty turn those properties into useful bearing performance.

Components & Materials6 min read

Written by WaterQuest Solutions · Updated 2 November 2026 · About the distributor

Build wear resistance into the moving surfaces

A named PEEK grade makes the choice concrete

VICTREX 450CA30 contains 30% carbon fibre. Its datasheet gives nominal tensile stress at break of 265 MPa (38,400 psi) at 23 °C (73 °F), and saturated water absorption of 0.30% at that temperature. These are grade-specific test values, not allowable bearing pressure or a finished pump rating. VICTREX 450CA30 datasheet.

The term describes a family of formulations rather than one universal product. The base polymer, reinforcement and other formulation choices influence the resulting properties. An engineer therefore needs a defined grade and condition, while a buyer needs to know which approved component configuration will be supplied. A catalogue reference to PEEK does not by itself answer either question.

In water hydraulics, the opportunity is to provide suitable contact behaviour without depending on a conventional oil-lubricated arrangement. That is a component-level engineering achievement, not a property that can be promised for every object made from the same material family.

Read a properties guide as a set of bounded measurements

A properties guide contains results obtained under stated test conditions. Strength, stiffness, dimensional behaviour and other values help compare candidate grades, but they do not all describe the same loading situation. A short-duration test at a stated temperature cannot automatically establish the behaviour of a continuously loaded part at another temperature.

Victrex's product guide is useful because it presents data for identified grades. If a supplier quotes one property, ask which grade and test condition it represents and why that property is relevant to the proposed component.

A good review also distinguishes material properties from component performance. A bearing's permissible duty depends on geometry and the mating surface as well as its material.

Reinforcement changes the questions, not just the numbers

Adding reinforcement can alter a polymer's stiffness and dimensional behaviour, but it also makes the actual formulation and manufacturing route important.

For a replacement part, dimensional interchangeability is therefore not enough. Confirm the approved grade, manufacturing route and finished condition where they influence function.

This distinction is especially valuable during procurement. “Equivalent PEEK” needs a defined meaning.

Treat the counterface as half of the sliding system

A sliding polymer part always interacts with another surface. That counterface has its own material, finish, hardness, geometry and condition. Victrex's tribology discussion provides context for evaluating friction and wear as an interaction rather than as a single material ranking.

A useful example is a guide operating against a rod. Similarly, an approved material pair can behave differently if the contact area becomes concentrated at one edge.

Surface specifications need to be preserved during repair. A replacement counterface that looks smoother is not automatically better for the intended contact.

Understand contact pressure and speed together

The pressure inside a hydraulic circuit is not necessarily the same as the contact pressure in a bearing or guide. Use the appropriate contact geometry rather than inserting system pressure directly into an unrelated material limit.

Speed matters because a sliding interface produces frictional heat and experiences a particular lubrication condition. Designers sometimes use a pressure-velocity quantity to screen bearing duties, but any limit belongs to a defined material, counterface and test arrangement. It is not a universal service-life prediction.

For a reciprocating component, include reversals and dwell. Average speed can conceal the conditions at the ends of travel, while an average load can conceal a short concentrated event.

Keep dimensional change in the clearance calculation

A precision component needs appropriate clearances throughout its operating range. The dimensions of the polymer and surrounding parts can change with temperature and exposure, and their changes need not be identical.

This creates a design trade-off. A small clearance may help control leakage or movement, while an insufficient allowance can produce unwanted contact under another condition.

A practical acceptance question is which conditions establish the minimum and maximum functional clearance. The answer should be traceable to dimensions and supported operating assumptions.

Distinguish wear from deformation and damage

A part that no longer holds its original shape may have lost material through wear, changed shape under load or suffered a discrete damage event.

Time under load can matter separately from the number of operating cycles. A component that carries a sustained load during a long dwell presents a different question from one that is loaded only briefly during motion.

Debris also deserves interpretation. Particles found in a filter could originate from the polymer, the counterface, another component or incoming contamination.

Verify the assembled function under a representative cycle

A useful test program measures what the component is supposed to accomplish. For a bearing or guide, that may include dimensions, friction-related behaviour and wear after an agreed cycle.

Representative testing does not mean deliberately operating equipment outside its supported limits. It means reproducing the important conditions inside the proposed duty, including starts, stops and expected idle periods where they matter.

The Water Hydraulics Company's P3 documentation is an example of water-service equipment presented with a defined construction and operating context. Its relevance is the existence of an engineered assembly, not a claim that any polymer-labelled replacement will reproduce its performance. For a purchase, obtain the approved configuration and service instructions from the supplier responsible for the equipment.

Make the material choice useful to the owner

A well-supported material selection should simplify the owner's decisions. The approved spare should be identifiable, the operating limits should be understandable and the signs requiring inspection should be documented. Otherwise, a correct original design can drift into an unsupported configuration through routine repairs.

The business benefit is not that carbon-reinforced PEEK makes maintenance disappear. It is that a suitable material pair can help deliver reliable water-service motion where removing hydraulic oil addresses a meaningful process or environmental exposure.
