Selecting Pumps for Water Hydraulic Systems

Find the pump configuration that fits your flow and pressure duty.

Choose a pump that can deliver your required flow across the pressure range you actually use. Learn how displacement, speed, inlet supply and water chemistry affect selection and reliable operation.

Article 039Components & Materials10 min readUpdated 19 October 2026

Choose the pump at the point where it must work

Your pump needs to deliver the required flow at the operating pressure, with suitable inlet conditions and working water. Compare that duty with the model’s curves, speed range and temperature limits. Selecting the whole operating envelope helps the water hydraulic supply support productive movement through starts, changing demand and sustained operation.

Start with the useful demand from the actuator or process. Identify normal operation, minimum demand, peaks and transitions. Distinguish conditions that occur together from independent maxima. Asking a supplier for maximum flow at maximum pressure can produce an oversized or unsuitable proposal if that combination never represents the real duty.

The selected pump should have a documented fit to that envelope and a practical installation arrangement. WaterQuest's role is to connect the application with supported water-service equipment. The benefit of removing hydraulic oil is strongest when the pump reliably supplies the required function rather than being chosen because it has the largest rating or smallest advertised footprint.

Translate displacement and speed into theoretical flow

For a positive-displacement pump, theoretical flow follows displacement per revolution multiplied by shaft speed. Using cubic centimetres per revolution and revolutions per minute, dividing the product by one thousand gives litres per minute. This is a useful first calculation because it connects component geometry to the required delivery.

A hypothetical six-cubic-centimetre pump turning at 1,500 revolutions per minute displaces nine litres per minute theoretically. That is not automatically the outlet flow available at the required pressure. Internal leakage and the actual operating condition affect delivered flow.

Use manufacturer performance information to move from the geometric calculation to selection. Do not assume one constant volumetric efficiency across every pressure, speed and temperature. The theoretical result is a reference that helps interpret the data, not a substitute for the data. It also does not establish that the example displacement or speed is appropriate for any particular WaterQuest model.

Match the flow curve to the useful demand

The actuator needs enough delivered flow to meet its movement requirement after relevant circuit effects are accounted for. A pump that provides the theoretical target but falls short in actual delivery may produce slower motion. Conversely, unnecessary excess flow can create a control and energy burden.

Review the performance curve at the intended conditions and identify its test basis. Note the fluid, speed and temperature where provided. If the proposed application differs materially, obtain appropriate confirmation rather than reading a convenient point from a graph and extending it beyond its stated context.

Also examine the lower end of operation. A pump selected for peak demand may spend much of its time at reduced speed or in a waiting state. The supported speed range and control arrangement should cover that use. The best selection is the one that fits the duty distribution, not only the one that reaches the peak once.

Understand the pressure boundary and drive requirement

Hydraulic output power is pressure difference multiplied by delivered volume flow. Shaft input exceeds that output because of pump losses, and electrical input also includes the motor and drive. State the boundary when comparing efficiency or sizing the supporting equipment.

For an illustrative duty of 120 bar (1,740 psi) pressure difference and eight litres per minute delivered flow, hydraulic output is 1.6 kilowatts (2.15 hp). This is teaching arithmetic using the bar-times-litres-per-minute divided-by-600 relationship. It is not a motor selection or a product performance claim.

The actual drive must be selected from supported performance and operating requirements. Starting, acceleration, duty and the permitted pump speed may matter. A nameplate power chosen from ideal hydraulic output alone can omit the losses and conditions needed for a complete package. The supplier should show how the drive and pump fit together at the required duty.

Treat inlet conditions as part of the selection

A pump depends on the fluid condition at its inlet during operation. Available source pressure, elevation and connecting losses can influence that condition. The selection should establish that the installation can maintain the required supply across the operating envelope, including relevant low-level or high-demand states.

The Water Hydraulics Company's P3 information differentiates configurations and supporting requirements. [1] The correct choice therefore includes the inlet and connection arrangement, not merely the displacement and pressure designation. Current documentation for the offered build should govern the detailed assessment.

If a booster or other supporting device is required, include it in the scope, controls and energy comparison. An apparently efficient pump selection can be misleading if its necessary auxiliaries are excluded. The owner needs to evaluate the complete arrangement that will actually deliver the accepted inlet condition at the installed location.

Specify the actual working water

Freshwater, seawater and treated process water present different material and operating questions. A general clean-water description does not identify dissolved constituents or treatment chemicals. Provide representative supply information and ask which parameters govern the offered configuration.

The manufacturer's desalination-pump literature describes a range developed for saline process duties. [2] That source should not be used to imply that every freshwater model has identical suitability. The model, material package and fluid basis need to match the proposed service.

Temperature belongs in the same assessment because it influences fluid properties and component conditions. Include normal operation and relevant extremes, together with any approved mixture or preservation arrangement. The objective is a purchase specification that describes what the pump will receive throughout ownership, rather than a fluid label that leaves the important compatibility questions unresolved.

Check the mechanical and connection interfaces

The pump must connect to a drive without imposing unsupported shaft loads or alignment conditions. Couplings, mounting arrangements and access influence the installed result. A suitable pump can perform poorly if its mechanical integration departs from the manufacturer's requirements.

Review all relevant connections, including any casing or drain paths. Their allowable conditions can differ from the pressure outlet or principal return. Combining connections without assessment may create back pressure or routing that the selected component does not permit.

The layout should also support inspection and replacement. A compact arrangement may be attractive initially but costly if routine access requires dismantling unrelated equipment. Include the people responsible for service in the design review so the final installation preserves both the technical operating basis and a practical route for maintaining it.

Evaluate control strategy and waiting-state behaviour

The pump's operating point is shaped by the circuit and controls. Flow may be required continuously, intermittently or only during particular movements. The selection should explain how the supply responds to those states and what happens to input energy when the actuator is waiting.

DOE's pumping guidance supports examining the whole system and demand pattern. [3] A small best-point efficiency advantage can be outweighed by a poor standby arrangement or unnecessary pressure loss. Compare complete alternatives at equivalent useful duty and include the relevant auxiliaries.

Do not attribute every improvement to the fluid. A control change that reduces running time is a separate engineering benefit from a pump with lower measured losses. Explaining the contributions individually makes the proposal more useful to the owner and prevents an energy claim from depending on an undefined comparison between unlike operating schedules.

Match endurance evidence to the service pattern

Continuous operation, frequent starts and occasional movement create different demands. The supplier should understand the expected schedule and any required holding or idle periods. A pump that suits one pattern should not be assumed equally suitable for another merely because the average daily running time is similar.

Ask what evidence supports the selected duty and what maintenance requirements apply. Distinguish a material or component test from installed operating experience. A short acceptance run can establish immediate performance but cannot independently prove a long service interval.

The application may also have a consequence that makes availability especially important. In that case, spares, redundancy or a recovery arrangement may influence the complete system choice. ISO 4413 provides a general hydraulic-system safety context, while the machine's function determines the specific requirements. [4] Pump selection should remain connected to those system responsibilities.

Confirm the selection with a clear acceptance basis

A useful selection record identifies the model configuration, intended operating envelope, supporting performance information and required installation conditions. It should explain any remaining confirmation item rather than leaving it hidden in a general quotation. This gives procurement a complete technical scope to compare.

Commissioning should verify the relevant installed conditions and delivered function through an agreed method. Retain the observations as a baseline for maintenance. If the site later changes the fluid, speed or demand, the record provides a basis for deciding whether the original selection remains valid.

WaterQuest's strongest pump recommendation is one that explains why the equipment fits this duty and where its limits sit. Water-service capability creates the option to remove oil from an appropriate circuit; disciplined selection makes that option dependable. The desired result is stable, documented delivery that the owner can operate and maintain with confidence.

A concise supplier enquiry can include a duty table rather than a single requested motor size. Show each important pressure and flow state, its duration and the available inlet condition. This lets the supplier identify whether one configuration covers the range or whether the architecture needs reconsideration before a detailed quotation is prepared.

When comparing offers, check that their performance boundaries match. One may include a booster or controls while another quotes only the pump. One graph may show theoretical displacement flow while another reports measured output. Resolving those differences often matters more than a small apparent numerical advantage on the first page of a brochure.

Finally, keep uncertain values labelled as such. If the site has not measured its demand or water condition, obtain that information before treating the selection as final. A targeted measurement can prevent a much larger cost later, when a physically installed pump turns out to be operating outside the conditions that justified its purchase.

From understanding to equipment selection

Select from the required delivered flow

Establish flow at the actuator or process, add relevant leakage and circuit demand, then evaluate pump displacement and speed. Check inlet conditions, drive torque and the supplied water-service configuration before comparing model sizes.

Which measurements help separate pump deterioration from circuit problems?

Record flow, pressure and input at commissioning. Declining output at the same speed warrants investigation of inlet supply, leakage and wear before increasing drive speed.
