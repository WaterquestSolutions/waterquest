Duplex Stainless at Sea: Stronger and More Pit-Resistant

Grades like 2205 out-resist 316 in chlorides, provided the grade, heat input and fabrication are specified correctly.

Components & Materials6 min read

Written by WaterQuest Solutions · Updated 2 November 2026 · About the distributor

Select an alloy for the exposure it will face

Grades like 2205 out-resist 316 in chlorides, provided the grade, heat input and fabrication are specified correctly.

Use PREN as a screening comparison

A common pitting-resistance equivalent is PREN = %Cr + 3.3 × %Mo + 16 × %N. Typical 316L is roughly 24–26, while 2205 is around 34–35; higher-alloy 2507 is above 40. Alleima’s SAF 2205 data specify a minimum PRE of 35 and compare it with about 24 for TP316L.

Start with the exposure the material must resist. Internal seawater, salt deposits, warm stagnant water and treated freshwater create different demands.

Understand why strength can be valuable

Higher material strength can offer design opportunities, but the benefit depends on which limit governs the component. A pressure-containing part may be governed by stress, stiffness, fatigue, geometry or a manufacturing requirement. A stronger alloy does not automatically permit a thinner wall or higher operating pressure. The completed design must be reassessed using the applicable properties and requirements.

For example, a valve body's minimum section may be constrained by machining, thread engagement or dimensional stability rather than a basic stress calculation. A cylinder component may need stiffness to maintain alignment even when its strength is ample.

Match corrosion resistance to the actual mechanism

Corrosion can be general or local, and different mechanisms respond to different conditions. Chloride-related pitting, crevice conditions and stress corrosion concerns are not interchangeable. Outokumpu's corrosion guidance explains the importance of environment and mechanism in stainless selection. A grade may offer improved resistance to a relevant mechanism while remaining vulnerable under a different exposure.

Temperature, deposits, stagnation and local geometry deserve particular attention in water equipment. A clean exposed surface does not experience the same condition as a narrow joint or a pocket beneath a deposit.

Specify the grade and delivered condition

Duplex grades differ in composition and intended performance. The product form and delivered condition also matter.

The completed component may contain several materials. A duplex body can still include springs, plugs, seats, seals or fasteners with different exposure limits. A complete material schedule makes this visible and gives service teams a basis for approved replacement.

Fabrication must preserve the intended properties

Material selection does not end when stock arrives. Forming, machining, welding and heat treatment can influence the delivered component.

This is a reason to assess the supplier's capability as well as the alloy's properties. Ask how critical fabrication requirements are controlled and which records accompany the part. Avoid prescribing unsupported numerical process limits from a general article; use current grade-specific technical information and the qualified procedure.

Surface condition and geometry remain important

A component's surface can be affected by fabrication residues, mechanical damage and contamination during handling. Appropriate finishing and cleaning should follow the material and equipment requirements. Do not assume that improved alloy selection makes functional surface specifications unnecessary. The component must still provide the geometry and finish needed by the seal, bearing or moving part it supports.

Design access for inspection and cleaning where deposits are expected. The ability to maintain a surface can influence lifecycle performance. A hidden crevice that cannot be inspected presents a different ownership problem from an accessible connection, even if both use the same grade.

Interpret reference data with its test conditions

Corrosion tables and laboratory results can help screen materials, but they do not directly predict the life of an assembled pump or valve. Outokumpu explicitly discusses limitations in using corrosion-table data for actual service. Treat the data as evidence for a defined comparison, then determine what additional configuration-specific support is needed.

A successful installation elsewhere also has a boundary. Ask whether it used the same grade, water, temperature, geometry and maintenance approach. A broad statement that duplex works in seawater omits too much to support a consequential purchase. This lets the owner address uncertainty systematically without either dismissing a promising material or accepting an unsupported service-life claim.

Keep dissimilar materials and replacement parts in view

A duplex component may connect to other stainless grades, copper alloys, coated parts or different fasteners. The combination can introduce corrosion and mechanical considerations that require review. Any isolation or protection measure must remain suitable for the pressure, load and service arrangement. A corrosion solution that compromises connection integrity is not a complete engineering solution.

Maintenance documentation should identify permitted replacements and any restrictions on substitution. A readily available fitting may match size and thread while changing the material combination. A clear approved-parts strategy helps preserve compatibility while making service practical, reducing the chance that a carefully selected duplex component becomes part of an unassessed mixed-material assembly.

Compare lifecycle value with realistic assumptions

Duplex may have a higher or lower total ownership cost than another supported option depending on material availability, fabrication, inspection and service consequences. Do not judge only the raw material price.

Keep expected recurring costs separate from uncertain failures. If longer service is part of the justification, state the evidence and remaining uncertainty. Include replacement availability and the local ability to perform approved repairs.

Make the material decision traceable to the application

A complete enquiry should provide internal fluid, external exposure, temperature, pressure, mechanical loads and expected operating pattern. Identify stagnant periods and cleaning or preservation methods.

WaterQuest can help define the material requirements within a supported water-hydraulic system. The persuasive argument is therefore a complete material decision that improves the application, not a claim that one stainless family eliminates corrosion or makes every water circuit suitable for seawater.
