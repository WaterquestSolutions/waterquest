Does Your Water Circuit Need an Accumulator? Name the Job First

Peak flow, pressure maintenance, emergency movement and shock damping are four different reasons. Name the job and size only what the circuit actually needs.

Specification & Design7 min read

Download the worksheet for this guide (editable text) · Open the accumulator requirement register (CSV)

Written by WaterQuest Solutions · Updated 2 November 2026 · About the distributor

Store hydraulic energy for a defined purpose

Water-hydraulic systems can use accumulators. The Water Hydraulics Company's accessories information includes water-system accumulator options, so a blanket claim that using water eliminates them is incorrect. Equally, their availability does not prove that a particular circuit needs one.

Understand how gas-separated storage works

In a gas-separated accumulator, incoming liquid compresses a gas volume through a separating element or mechanism. The gas pressure changes as its volume changes.

The gas behaviour also depends on the rate of the process and heat exchange. A slow charge and a rapid discharge need not follow the same simple thermal assumption. Engineers may use appropriate gas relationships as part of sizing, but a preliminary calculation is not a substitute for the accumulator supplier's selection method and limits.

Separate peak flow from average demand

An intermittent actuator may need more flow during a short movement than a modest pump can provide directly. Storage can supply part of that peak while the pump contributes and later recharges the accumulator. It does not create energy or guarantee lower consumption.

Consider a simplified teaching example. A movement requires 2 litres (0.528 US gal) over 10 seconds, equivalent to 12 litres per minute (3.17 US gpm) during that interval. A pump supplying 4 litres per minute (1.06 US gpm) contributes about 0.67 litres (0.177 US gal) in those 10 seconds. Storage would need to contribute about 1.33 litres (0.351 US gal) before allowances for leakage, pressure variation and other effects. The selected device must deliver the required usable volume over the permitted pressure range and have enough time to recharge before the next demand.

Check the lowest pressure during delivery

An actuator's required force does not disappear as the accumulator discharges. As stored fluid is released, the available pressure changes. The design must ensure that pressure remains adequate at the actuator after circuit losses for the full required movement.

Use the actual force and area relationships to establish the pressure requirement, including opposing chamber pressure and friction allowances as appropriate. The foundational pressure-area principle is explained in the linked physics reference, but real mechanism and circuit effects must be added by the designer. Identify the governing point in the movement and the state of the accumulator when that point occurs.

Distinguish pressure maintenance from load holding

An accumulator can replenish fluid and help maintain pressure within an operating range, but pressure maintenance is not automatically a complete load-holding function. The schematic must show how stored energy reaches the intended consumer and how other paths are controlled.

For a holding duty, define acceptable movement, duration and operating conditions. Consider what happens if pressure falls, a signal is lost or a valve changes state. An accumulator should not be described as a universal fail-safe feature merely because it stores energy. Stored energy can support a protective action, but it can also produce unexpected motion if the circuit and isolation sequence are not designed correctly.

Emergency movement needs a complete sequence

A requirement to close a valve after loss of the main supply must identify the starting state, required motion and least favourable load. Determine whether the accumulator is guaranteed to be sufficiently charged when the demand occurs. A charged vessel cannot complete a movement if the necessary valve sequence does not occur.

Define how readiness is indicated and how loss of readiness affects operation. The owner should know whether the process may continue if the stored-energy function is unavailable. Verification should address the relevant failure scenario under an approved test plan, with safe control of the mechanism. Do not infer emergency capability from a successful normal stroke while the pump is running. The acceptance evidence needs to establish the function under the conditions for which stored energy was included in the first place.

Disturbance control is a separate selection problem

An accumulator may be considered for selected pressure pulsations or transients, but a device sized for emergency volume is not automatically effective for that purpose. A general statement that an accumulator absorbs shocks does not establish performance for a particular valve closure, pump pulsation or load change.

Identify the mechanism producing the disturbance before selecting a remedy. A supply problem, abrupt control action or unsuitable piping arrangement may need correction at its source.

Confirm water compatibility throughout the assembly

The liquid-side materials, separator and connected accessories must support the specified water and temperature. A stainless exterior does not establish the compatibility of internal elements. Changes in the fluid or cleaning method can affect the selection, so the approved envelope belongs in the asset documentation.

Pressure-vessel and installation requirements must also be assessed for the jurisdiction and use. ISO 4413's public scope provides hydraulic-system context, but it is not a complete statement of every pressure-equipment requirement. Identify the documents and inspections required for the actual installation.

Design isolation around the stored energy

Stopping the pump does not empty an accumulator or remove its stored energy. Maintenance procedures must identify the stored-energy paths, control them and verify the required condition before work. CCOHS guidance emphasizes that residual energy can remain after isolation from the primary source. The procedure must be specific to the equipment and performed by authorized personnel; a general educational article cannot substitute for it.

Make the physical arrangement support that procedure. Identification, access and appropriate indication help personnel understand the state of the system. The gas side introduces its own service requirements and must be handled using the manufacturer's instructions and suitable equipment. Do not improvise charging or discharge methods. Include inspection and maintenance responsibilities in the ownership plan so that the accumulator's intended function remains available without creating an unmanaged hazard during ordinary service work or fault investigation.

Verify useful delivery over the operating cycle

Acceptance should demonstrate the actual function: peak-flow support, pressure maintenance, emergency movement or disturbance control. Record initial conditions and the complete event, including the least favourable relevant pressure. A successful first operation may not establish repeated-cycle capability if the pump cannot restore the required state in time.

WaterQuest can help determine whether storage makes a proposed water circuit more useful and how it fits the supported equipment scope. The persuasive argument is functional: the accumulator supplies a defined service that the owner needs, within a verified operating envelope.
