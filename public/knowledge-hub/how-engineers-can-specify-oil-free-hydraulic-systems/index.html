How Engineers Can Specify Oil-Free Hydraulic Systems

Give suppliers a clear duty for an oil-free proposal.

A useful specification tells suppliers what the equipment must achieve. Learn how to describe an oil-free hydraulic system through loads, movement, water quality, controls and acceptance checks that can be priced and verified.

Article 033Specification & Design10 min readUpdated 19 October 2026

Specify the result you want from an oil-free circuit

State what the equipment must move, hold or control, then explain where hydraulic oil is to be removed and why. Include the operating conditions and required response to interruptions. This gives suppliers a common brief for proposing water-service components and lets you compare how each complete arrangement will solve the operating problem.

For example, removing mineral hydraulic oil from a gate-actuation circuit is a narrower and more verifiable requirement than eliminating all lubricants from the surrounding machinery. The drive and mechanical joints may have separate lubrication needs. Identify those interfaces openly. This allows the design to meet the actual contamination or environmental objective without creating a disagreement over a phrase after equipment has been manufactured.

A performance specification should give the supplier freedom where alternatives are acceptable and firm constraints where the project needs them. Required travel time may be fixed, while pump arrangement may be open to proposal. An existing mounting envelope may be non-negotiable. Distinguishing those cases encourages useful engineering while keeping offers comparable.

Define the mechanical envelope before the hydraulic supply

Document force or torque through the relevant travel, including breakaway, running, seating and holding conditions. State the orientation, stroke or angular movement, cycle frequency and any required low-speed behaviour. Identify whether the values come from measurements, calculations or an unresolved estimate. The supplier needs to know which uncertainty could change component selection.

Hydraulic force follows pressure acting on effective area, with opposing pressures and losses accounted for. [1] A requirement for net actuator force should therefore name the operating conditions at the actuator rather than merely quote a power-unit pressure. If a linkage converts cylinder force to gate torque, provide the geometry or assign responsibility for its analysis. Otherwise, a correct cylinder calculation can still fail to establish useful machine output.

Describe exceptional states separately from continuous duty. An infrequent emergency action can govern available energy or peak demand without occurring throughout a normal shift. Conversely, a modest repetitive duty can govern heating or wear. Avoid presenting a collection of unrelated maxima as one unexplained operating point, and do not omit an infrequent but mandatory service condition because it complicates the enquiry.

Specify behaviour that can actually be verified

A statement such as fast, precise and reliable is difficult to price or test. Replace it with measurable behaviour: travel completed within an agreed interval, position maintained within a defined tolerance under a specified load, or a stated response after a named interruption. The exact values belong to the project. The educational principle is to connect every important performance adjective to a condition and a verification method.

Distinguish commanded motion from measured motion. The control system may energize a valve without the actuator completing its travel. If the process requires confirmation, specify the relevant feedback and how disagreement is handled. Likewise, a holding requirement should define the duration and loading context rather than assume that a closed control valve means zero movement indefinitely.

Make test boundaries clear. Factory evidence can establish package performance in a controlled setup. Installed performance also depends on pipework, supply, mechanism and controls. Allocate which requirements are demonstrated at the factory, which at site and which through analysis. This prevents an acceptance document from appearing complete while the condition most important to the owner was never examined.

Give the supplier a real water specification

Describe the intended source, available analysis, expected temperature and variation. Identify whether the circuit recirculates water or uses a once-through supply. Include any treatment, cleaning or preservation fluids that can reach the equipment. Where information is missing, assign a confirmation milestone before final selection rather than silently assuming a universally suitable supply.

Manufacturer information for purpose-designed equipment, such as the Janus P3, illustrates that water service includes defined component and inlet requirements. [2] Ask for the permitted conditions for the exact offered configuration, including relevant return or drain constraints. A supplier should explain how its equipment fits the supplied water data and identify any additional management the owner must provide.

Keep different water-quality questions separate. Particle filtration, dissolved chemistry and hygiene address different mechanisms. A single micron number does not establish the entire fluid specification. Likewise, potable water is not automatically within every hydraulic component's operating envelope, and hydraulic compatibility does not establish suitability for product contact. The interface schedule should show which party resolves each requirement.

Set pressure boundaries without prescribing unsupported settings

The specification should identify required operating pressures and applicable component limits, including relevant peaks. For the WaterQuest equipment scope described on this site, the stated upper boundary is 160 bar (2,321 psi) including peaks, with lower limits governing where an individual assembly requires them. Do not turn that boundary into a universal operating setpoint or assume that every component in the offer has the same rating.

Ask the supplier to account for distribution losses, opposing actuator pressure, transient behaviour and trapped volumes. The pressure at a power-unit gauge does not automatically describe every isolated section. External loads can also pressurise an actuator. These conditions need a coherent protective design rather than a checklist item saying that a relief valve is included.

Working, proof and burst figures serve different purposes. Require the offered ratings to be identified clearly and linked to the fluid, temperature and duty. A proof certificate should not be used as a substitute for an operating rating. Keeping those distinctions explicit makes technical evaluation more reliable and reduces the chance of an unsuitable substitution during purchasing.

Describe the complete interface, including responsibility

List mechanical mounting, electrical supply, command signals, feedback, communications, water supply, drainage and environmental exposure. Mark the physical package boundary on a drawing and assign responsibility for connections outside it. A hydraulic skid can be complete within its own scope while the installation still lacks essential supports, controls or water-management functions.

Separate operating authority from equipment capability. Identify which controller owns the sequence, which system supplies permissives and who defines the required response to faults. Where safety-related functions are required, assign their design and validation explicitly. A pump vendor, controls integrator and asset owner may each contribute, but the complete function needs an accountable integration process.

ISO 4413's public scope recognizes hydraulic systems as a broader design and safety discipline. [3] The project should identify applicable requirements and editions through its responsible engineering process. A general reference to a standard in a brochure does not demonstrate that the offered assembly and site interfaces have been assessed against every relevant obligation.

Request performance evidence at the decision boundary

Ask suppliers to distinguish measured data, calculations, typical catalogue information and guaranteed commitments. The distinction is especially useful for efficiency, movement quality and service life. A material test or pump curve may support one part of the selection without predicting the life or energy use of the complete machine. Require the assumptions that connect the evidence to the proposed duty.

Energy comparisons should cover the same useful output and operating schedule. Include moving, holding, standby and required auxiliaries consistently. DOE's pumping guidance supports examining the complete operating system. [4] A specification can apply that principle by requesting electrical input at named operating states and a transparent annualization method instead of demanding an unsupported universal saving percentage.

For uncertain but important behaviour, define a verification activity. A representative test can be more useful than an arbitrary service-life number that no bidder can substantiate. State the success criteria, observation period and treatment of deviations. This gives new technology a fair evidentiary route while maintaining the owner's responsibility to purchase a supported result.

Make maintenance and recovery part of the design submission

Require practical access for inspection, isolation, sampling, filter changes and removal of serviceable components. Show lifting routes and relevant clearances where equipment is heavy or confined. A package footprint should not be evaluated without its service envelope. The owner will live with those spatial decisions long after the initial installation team has left.

Identify configuration-specific spares, consumables and support arrangements. Ask for part identity and replacement requirements rather than a general assurance that parts are available. Include training and final drawings that describe the installed options. Future maintainers should be able to preserve the water compatibility and control behaviour without reconstructing the design from old correspondence.

Hydraulic and mechanical energy control remains necessary. CCOHS guidance emphasizes identifying retained and externally supplied energy before work. [5] The specification should support the site's approved maintenance arrangements through suitable design and information. It should not substitute a generic fluid label for a proper assessment of pressure, loads and unintended movement.

Evaluate deviations and preserve the accepted configuration

Require a consolidated schedule of departures from the enquiry. Each should identify the affected requirement, proposed alternative and consequence. A technically useful variation may be acceptable, but it should be evaluated openly. A statement buried in a catalogue should not be the only indication that an offered package cannot meet a mandatory operating condition.

Record the accepted design basis as the project develops. Changes in bore, valve type, supply arrangement or control settings can affect several disciplines. Update the relevant calculations, drawings and acceptance plan together. This prevents separate teams from each working correctly against different versions of the intended installation.

A strong water hydraulic specification makes the value proposition testable. It defines the oil-free boundary, the useful mechanical service, the supported fluid and pressure conditions, and the evidence required at handover. Engineers can evaluate the design, procurement can compare complete offers and the owner can maintain the result. That is how an environmental intention becomes a dependable piece of industrial infrastructure.
