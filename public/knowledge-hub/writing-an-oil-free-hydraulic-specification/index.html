Writing an Oil-Free Hydraulic Spec Bidders Can Actually Price

Replace "fast, precise, reliable" with travel times, tolerances and test methods. Define the oil-free boundary, and water suppliers can compete on equal terms.

For example, removing mineral hydraulic oil from a gate-actuation circuit is a narrower and more verifiable requirement than eliminating all lubricants from the surrounding machinery.

A performance specification should give the supplier freedom where alternatives are acceptable and firm constraints where the project needs them.

Define the mechanical envelope before the hydraulic supply

Document force or torque through the relevant travel, including breakaway, running, seating and holding conditions.

Hydraulic force follows pressure acting on effective area, with opposing pressures and losses accounted for. A requirement for net actuator force should therefore name the operating conditions at the actuator rather than merely quote a power-unit pressure.

Describe exceptional states separately from continuous duty. Avoid presenting a collection of unrelated maxima as one unexplained operating point, and do not omit an infrequent but mandatory service condition because it complicates the enquiry.

Specify behaviour that can actually be verified

A statement such as fast, precise and reliable is difficult to price or test. Replace it with measurable behaviour: travel completed within an agreed interval, position maintained within a defined tolerance under a specified load, or a stated response after a named interruption.

Distinguish commanded motion from measured motion. The control system may energize a valve without the actuator completing its travel.

Make test boundaries clear. Factory evidence can establish package performance in a controlled setup. This prevents an acceptance document from appearing complete while the condition most important to the owner was never examined.

Give the supplier a real water specification

Describe the intended source, available analysis, expected temperature and variation. Identify whether the circuit recirculates water or uses a once-through supply.

Manufacturer information for purpose-designed equipment, such as the Janus P3, illustrates that water service includes defined component and inlet requirements. Ask for the permitted conditions for the exact offered configuration, including relevant return or drain constraints. A supplier should explain how its equipment fits the supplied water data and identify any additional management the owner must provide.

Keep different water-quality questions separate. Particle filtration, dissolved chemistry and hygiene address different mechanisms. A single micron number does not establish the entire fluid specification. Likewise, potable water is not automatically within every hydraulic component's operating envelope, and hydraulic compatibility does not establish suitability for product contact.

Set pressure boundaries without prescribing unsupported settings

The specification should identify required operating pressures and applicable component limits, including relevant peaks. For the WaterQuest equipment scope described on this site, the stated upper boundary is 160 bar (2,321 psi) including peaks, with lower limits governing where an individual assembly requires them. Do not turn that boundary into a universal operating setpoint or assume that every component in the offer has the same rating.

Ask the supplier to account for distribution losses, opposing actuator pressure, transient behaviour and trapped volumes. The pressure at a power-unit gauge does not automatically describe every isolated section.

Working, proof and burst figures serve different purposes. A proof certificate should not be used as a substitute for an operating rating.

Describe the complete interface, including responsibility

List mechanical mounting, electrical supply, command signals, feedback, communications, water supply, drainage and environmental exposure.

Separate operating authority from equipment capability. Where safety-related functions are required, assign their design and validation explicitly.

ISO 4413's public scope recognizes hydraulic systems as a broader design and safety discipline. The project should identify applicable requirements and editions through its responsible engineering process. A general reference to a standard in a brochure does not demonstrate that the offered assembly and site interfaces have been assessed against every relevant obligation.

Request performance evidence at the decision boundary

Ask suppliers to distinguish measured data, calculations, typical catalogue information and guaranteed commitments.

Energy comparisons should cover the same useful output and operating schedule. DOE's pumping guidance supports examining the complete operating system. A specification can apply that principle by requesting electrical input at named operating states and a transparent annualization method instead of demanding an unsupported universal saving percentage.

For uncertain but important behaviour, define a verification activity. A representative test can be more useful than an arbitrary service-life number that no bidder can substantiate.

Make maintenance and recovery part of the design submission

Require practical access for inspection, isolation, sampling, filter changes and removal of serviceable components. Show lifting routes and relevant clearances where equipment is heavy or confined.

Identify configuration-specific spares, consumables and support arrangements.

Hydraulic and mechanical energy control remains necessary. CCOHS guidance emphasizes identifying retained and externally supplied energy before work. The specification should support the site's approved maintenance arrangements through suitable design and information.

Evaluate deviations and preserve the accepted configuration

Require a consolidated schedule of departures from the enquiry. A statement buried in a catalogue should not be the only indication that an offered package cannot meet a mandatory operating condition.

Record the accepted design basis as the project develops.

A strong water hydraulic specification makes the value proposition testable. It defines the oil-free boundary, the useful mechanical service, the supported fluid and pressure conditions, and the evidence required at handover.

Keep the specification useful after handover

State duty, interfaces, operating limits, acceptance tests and the documents supplied with the equipment. Tender comparisons should use the same scope; retain approved configuration records when staff or suppliers change.
