A Fair Test of Water vs Oil HPUs in Desalination

Measure energy per valve cycle or kWh per cubic metre over matching boundaries and intervals. A controlled comparison establishes the actual result for water and oil HPUs.

Testing & Adoption6 min read

Written by WaterQuest Solutions · Updated 2 November 2026 · About the distributor

Test like-for-like output to make the comparison useful

An oil hydraulic power unit usually supplies an oil circuit that produces mechanical work. A water power unit supplies a compatible water circuit. Its comparison boundary must include the functions it replaces, rather than treating it as an interchangeable actuator HPU.

Begin the test plan with a diagram showing the equipment, energy inputs and useful output.

Choose the correct useful output

For a desalination process comparison, the useful product is an agreed quantity and quality of permeate under defined feed conditions. DOE's overview provides the process context, while the actual membrane and plant documents establish the project-specific operating basis.

For an auxiliary hydraulic comparison, useful output may instead be completed valve movements at the required torque, speed and duty.

Neither boundary is inherently superior. They answer different ownership questions. Connecting those two views later is legitimate; mixing their measurements at the start creates a result that cannot be interpreted confidently.

Establish equivalent process conditions

A fair RO comparison holds the important process conditions sufficiently consistent or explicitly accounts for their differences. A lower energy reading achieved while producing less acceptable water is not an improvement in equivalent service.

A freshly cleaned membrane train and a fouled train do not provide identical resistance. Do not present an adjusted estimate as though both configurations were physically tested in the same condition.

Choose acceptance ranges before collecting the data.

Draw the electrical measurement boundary

A pump shaft measurement describes pump performance. A package electrical measurement includes the motor and drive. Each can be useful, but the report must name the boundary beside the result.

If an integrated recovery arrangement removes a separate booster or drive, account for that change openly. Do not compare the complete input of one option with only the principal motor of the other.

DOE's pumping guidance encourages consideration of the operating system rather than an isolated component. In a test plan, that principle becomes a practical instrument list and a marked drawing: which feeders are measured, which auxiliary loads are included, and which site loads are excluded. This also makes future retesting possible when the package or plant changes.

Use energy and volume over the same interval

Specific energy consumption for an RO process is electrical energy divided by accepted permeate volume.

For a hypothetical teaching example, a test consuming 24 kilowatt-hours while producing eight cubic metres of acceptable permeate has a specific energy consumption of three kilowatt-hours per cubic metre. These are illustrative values, not a prediction for a WaterQuest installation.

Use synchronized measurement intervals.

Capture the operating range that the owner will use

An attractive best-point result is valuable information, but it does not necessarily represent annual operation.

Describe steady operation separately from starts, stops and transitions. Likewise, part-load behaviour can outweigh a small difference at full output where demand rarely reaches the nominal maximum.

Build a duty-weighted estimate only after measuring the relevant states.

Plan uncertainty before interpreting small differences

Measurements have uncertainty. Instrument specifications alone do not describe the complete uncertainty of a test; installation, calibration, sampling and calculation methods matter.

Suppose two configurations return closely spaced results while feed conditions vary noticeably between runs. The appropriate response may be another controlled test, not a confident percentage saving.

Have the responsible test team select suitable instruments and document their locations.

Observe service behaviour without claiming lifetime proof

Efficiency is only one test outcome. Record noise, vibration observations, temperature stability, leakage, operator interventions and access difficulties.

A short trial cannot establish multi-year service life. A successful acceptance run should not become an unsupported claim that the equipment will require no maintenance or retain the same performance indefinitely.

Where reliability uncertainty controls the decision, define a follow-on monitoring period with appropriate inspection and reporting.

Translate the result into an ownership decision

The final comparison should show complete installed cost, measured performance, expected duty and the support arrangement.

Separate verified findings from estimates and unresolved questions.

WaterQuest can make a strong desalination proposal by explaining exactly which function its equipment performs and what evidence supports that scope.

Preserve the test package as an asset document. Include the marked boundary drawing, equipment identifiers, control settings, instrument record and raw data location.

An executive summary can remain concise while linking to that evidence. It should state the decision, the measured range and the assumptions most likely to change the recommendation.

Where it fits

This article is a test method. It does not report completed comparative tests or a measured efficiency advantage.
