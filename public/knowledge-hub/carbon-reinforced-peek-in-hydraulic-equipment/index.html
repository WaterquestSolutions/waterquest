Carbon-Reinforced PEEK in Hydraulic Equipment

See how an engineered polymer supports water-service wear surfaces.

Moving parts need surfaces that resist wear in water service. Discover where carbon-reinforced PEEK can support a hydraulic design and why the grade, opposing surface, load and temperature all matter.

Article 010Components & Materials10 min readUpdated 19 October 2026

Build wear resistance into the moving surfaces

Carbon-reinforced PEEK gives designers a way to create selected bearings, guides and wear surfaces for water hydraulic equipment. Its value comes from the complete contact: polymer grade, opposing surface, load, movement and water condition. Selecting those elements together helps the component manage friction and wear while water serves as the working medium.

The term describes a family of formulations rather than one universal product. The base polymer, reinforcement and other formulation choices influence the resulting properties. Manufacturing also affects the finished part. An engineer therefore needs a defined grade and condition, while a buyer needs to know which approved component configuration will be supplied. A catalogue reference to PEEK does not by itself answer either question.

In water hydraulics, the opportunity is to provide suitable contact behaviour without depending on a conventional oil-lubricated arrangement. That is a component-level engineering achievement, not a property that can be promised for every object made from the same material family. Understanding how the material is selected and verified helps separate a credible equipment proposal from a list of impressive-sounding ingredients.

Read a properties guide as a set of bounded measurements

A properties guide contains results obtained under stated test conditions. Strength, stiffness, dimensional behaviour and other values help compare candidate grades, but they do not all describe the same loading situation. A short-duration test at a stated temperature cannot automatically establish the behaviour of a continuously loaded part at another temperature. The units, specimen condition and test method are part of the information.

Victrex's product guide is useful because it presents data for identified grades. Its role in a hydraulic review is to support material screening and design inputs, not to certify an entire pump or actuator. If a supplier quotes one property, ask which grade and test condition it represents and why that property is relevant to the proposed component. [2]

A good review also distinguishes material properties from component performance. A bearing's permissible duty depends on geometry and the mating surface as well as its material. A valve's leakage depends on the assembled clearance and operating conditions. Where the decision concerns the finished machine, request evidence at that level instead of treating a favourable coupon-test result as a substitute for functional verification.

Reinforcement changes the questions, not just the numbers

Adding reinforcement can alter a polymer's stiffness and dimensional behaviour, but it also makes the actual formulation and manufacturing route important. The designer should establish whether directional behaviour matters in the part and whether the data being used represent the relevant direction. A long slender feature and a compact bearing insert may use the same named material in very different ways.

For a replacement part, dimensional interchangeability is therefore not enough. Confirm the approved grade, manufacturing route and finished condition where they influence function. An undocumented substitution may retain the external dimensions while changing the way the part deforms or interacts with its counterface. The replacement should preserve the supported component design, not merely resemble the original part when placed on a bench.

This distinction is especially valuable during procurement. “Equivalent PEEK” needs a defined meaning. Equivalence could concern chemistry, mechanical properties, processing or a tested assembly performance, and those are not interchangeable claims. The party proposing a substitution should explain which requirements remain satisfied and provide the evidence needed to assess the change. Otherwise, a small purchasing variation can become an unrecorded design change.

Treat the counterface as half of the sliding system

A sliding polymer part always interacts with another surface. That counterface has its own material, finish, hardness, geometry and condition. The interface can encounter particles, temperature changes and variations in water supply. Selecting the polymer without defining the opposing surface leaves a major part of the wear problem unspecified. Victrex's tribology discussion provides context for evaluating friction and wear as an interaction rather than as a single material ranking. [1]

A useful example is a guide operating against a rod. If the rod becomes damaged or misaligned, replacing only the guide may restore function briefly while leaving the cause of abnormal contact in place. Similarly, an approved material pair can behave differently if the contact area becomes concentrated at one edge. The investigation should inspect the pair and the mechanism that holds it in alignment.

Surface specifications need to be preserved during repair. A replacement counterface that looks smoother is not automatically better for the intended contact. Use the manufacturer's supported finish and preparation requirements rather than an appearance-based judgement. If a surface treatment is proposed, review its effect on dimensions, adhesion, wear debris and the actual water environment before treating it as an improvement.

Understand contact pressure and speed together

The pressure inside a hydraulic circuit is not necessarily the same as the contact pressure in a bearing or guide. Hydraulic pressure acts over surfaces and generates forces; the mechanical arrangement then determines how those forces are distributed at a contact. A small supporting area can experience a high local load even when the fluid pressure seems modest. Use the appropriate contact geometry rather than inserting system pressure directly into an unrelated material limit.

Speed matters because a sliding interface produces frictional heat and experiences a particular lubrication condition. Designers sometimes use a pressure-velocity quantity to screen bearing duties, but any limit belongs to a defined material, counterface and test arrangement. It is not a universal service-life prediction. A brief high-load start and prolonged steady running can place different demands on the same part.

For a reciprocating component, include reversals and dwell. Average speed can conceal the conditions at the ends of travel, while an average load can conceal a short concentrated event. The operating description should identify the parts of the cycle that could govern wear or deformation. That information allows the supplier to assess a real duty instead of an artificially simplified average.

Keep dimensional change in the clearance calculation

A precision component needs appropriate clearances throughout its operating range. The dimensions of the polymer and surrounding parts can change with temperature and exposure, and their changes need not be identical. Manufacturing tolerances also mean that nominal dimensions are not the only dimensions that can be delivered. Clearance review should consider the relevant combinations, not just the drawing's central values.

This creates a design trade-off. A small clearance may help control leakage or movement, while an insufficient allowance can produce unwanted contact under another condition. Increasing the clearance indiscriminately can create a different functional problem. The correct answer comes from the component design, the actual material data and the required performance across the operating envelope.

A practical acceptance question is which conditions establish the minimum and maximum functional clearance. The answer should be traceable to dimensions and supported operating assumptions. If the supplier manages this within a proprietary component, the buyer can request the resulting operating limits and functional evidence without demanding confidential design details. The goal is confidence in the selected assembly, not reverse engineering its internals.

Distinguish wear from deformation and damage

A part that no longer holds its original shape may have lost material through wear, changed shape under load or suffered a discrete damage event. Those mechanisms need different responses. A photograph of a worn-looking contact is not enough to assign a cause. Compare dimensions, examine the counterface and connect the findings to the operating record before choosing a replacement or changing the design.

Time under load can matter separately from the number of operating cycles. A component that carries a sustained load during a long dwell presents a different question from one that is loaded only briefly during motion. Temperature and support geometry can influence that behaviour. Where deformation is relevant, ask for the appropriate long-duration evidence rather than extrapolating from a short mechanical test.

Debris also deserves interpretation. Particles found in a filter could originate from the polymer, the counterface, another component or incoming contamination. Identifying the material and location of damage helps direct the investigation. Simply naming the polymer as the cause because it is the newest component can lead to an unnecessary redesign while leaving an alignment or cleanliness problem unresolved.

Verify the assembled function under a representative cycle

A useful test programme measures what the component is supposed to accomplish. For a bearing or guide, that may include dimensions, friction-related behaviour and wear after an agreed cycle. For a complete valve or pump, delivered flow, leakage, control behaviour and power can provide relevant functional information. The test needs a stated fluid, temperature, load and duration so that its conclusions have a clear boundary.

Representative testing does not mean deliberately operating equipment outside its supported limits. It means reproducing the important conditions inside the proposed duty, including starts, stops and expected idle periods where they matter. Record the initial condition so that later measurements describe a change rather than an unexplained final number. State any conditions that remain untested.

The Water Hydraulics Company's P3 documentation is an example of water-service equipment presented with a defined construction and operating context. Its relevance is the existence of an engineered assembly, not a claim that any polymer-labelled replacement will reproduce its performance. For a purchase, obtain the approved configuration and service instructions from the supplier responsible for the equipment. [3]

Make the material choice useful to the owner

A well-supported material selection should simplify the owner's decisions. The approved spare should be identifiable, the operating limits should be understandable and the signs requiring inspection should be documented. If the assembly requires a particular mating part or service procedure, keep that relationship visible in the parts and maintenance records. Otherwise, a correct original design can drift into an unsupported configuration through routine repairs.

The business benefit is not that carbon-reinforced PEEK makes maintenance disappear. It is that a suitable material pair can help deliver reliable water-service motion where removing hydraulic oil addresses a meaningful process or environmental exposure. WaterQuest can support that discussion by connecting the application to documented components and representative evidence. The strongest argument combines the material's useful role with a candid account of the conditions that make the finished equipment work.

From understanding to equipment selection

Specify the complete sliding pair

For a bearing or guide, identify the polymer grade, counterface material, finish, contact load and movement. Carbon reinforcement is part of the material description; it does not establish an allowable speed or service life for every geometry.

Calculate the relevant duty

Use the surface-finish calculator to convert finish units and examine an explicitly assumed Ra-to-Rz relationship. Use the lifecycle cost calculator to compare capital, recurring service, replacement and residual costs over the same study period.

Connect the calculation to equipment

Review the offered wetted materials and sliding interfaces on the pump or cylinder configuration, then provide the actual water chemistry and external exposure.

What should inspection reveal about the selected material pairing?

Retain the original wear dimensions and replacement criteria. Investigate abrasive contamination and alignment when wear changes, rather than attributing all degradation to the polymer choice.
