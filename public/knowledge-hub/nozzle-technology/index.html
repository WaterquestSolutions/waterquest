Knowledge Hub / Nozzle technology

ENGINEERING REFERENCE

Shape the spray.
Match the duty.

Water-nozzle patterns, droplet behaviour and selection inputs for fire protection, dust control, odour treatment and industrial cleaning.

Start with what the water must do at the target. Then match the nozzle, pressure, flow and supply system to that job.

WaterQuest equipment limit: 160 bar (2,321 psi), including peaks.

The selected configuration may have a lower limit. Specialist hydroblasting and cutting equipment above this pressure falls outside the equipment scope presented on this site.

START WITH THE DUTY

Pressure is one input.
The spray is the result.

01

Coverage

Match the footprint to the target. Check spray angle, nozzle spacing, overlap, obstructions and the distance over which the spray must travel.

02

Droplet behaviour

Size distribution, velocity and water flow work together. A single droplet diameter does not describe the complete spray.

03

Operating conditions

Use the selected nozzle’s data at the intended liquid properties and pressure. Check the whole circuit against its lowest applicable component limit.

NOZZLE GEOMETRY LIBRARY

Eight patterns. Different jobs.

The diagrams show the shape of each spray. Flow, droplet size, pressure rating and material options belong to the selected model’s datasheet.

PATTERN 01

Full cone

Distributes water across a filled circular footprint. Internal vane and orifice geometry shape the distribution.

Applications to assess
Area wetting, cooling, washing and selected fire-protection duties.
Confirm before selection
Distribution uniformity, free passage, flow and spray angle at the working pressure.

PATTERN 02

Hollow cone

Concentrates spray around a cone-shaped perimeter. The central region receives less direct spray.

Applications to assess
Gas cooling, process contact and selected dust-control applications.
Confirm before selection
Droplet distribution, distance to the target and whether the hollow footprint suits the duty.

PATTERN 03

Flat fan

Forms a sheet-like spray for a strip or moving surface. Adjacent patterns can overlap to cover a wider area.

Applications to assess
Conveyor washing, surface cleaning, coating and descaling.
Confirm before selection
Nozzle spacing, orientation, overlap and impact at the actual stand-off distance.

PATTERN 04

Solid stream / jet

Concentrates flow into a narrow jet. It prioritizes reach and local impact over wide spray coverage.

Applications to assess
Monitors, targeted washing and specialist jet-cleaning equipment.
Confirm before selection
Reaction force, jet reach, guarding and the pressure rating of every component.

PATTERN 05

Fog / fine mist

Breaks water into a fine spray. Small droplets offer more surface area per unit volume and respond readily to air movement.

Applications to assess
Selected water-mist protection, humidification, cooling and fine spray duties.
Confirm before selection
Measured size distribution, evaporation, ventilation and delivery to the target.

PATTERN 06

Air atomizing

Uses a separate air supply to break up the liquid. Water and air operating conditions both affect the spray.

Applications to assess
Low-flow spraying, humidification and selected fine-particle or reagent-delivery duties.
Confirm before selection
Air consumption, liquid flow, mixing arrangement and compressor requirements.

PATTERN 07

Rotating / oscillating

Sweeps one or more jets through a controlled path to cover an area or vessel interior.

Applications to assess
Tank washing, vessel cleaning, tube cleaning and specialist surface preparation.
Confirm before selection
Sweep pattern, drive requirements, reaction loads and coverage behind obstructions.

PATTERN 08

Water-mist cluster

Combines multiple orifices in one head to distribute mist in several directions. The complete head and system determine performance.

Applications to assess
Fire-protection systems where that nozzle arrangement has appropriate fire-test evidence.
Confirm before selection
Approved spacing, orientation, flow, pressure, hazard and installation limitations.

DROPLET PHYSICS

Smaller drops. More surface area.

For the same volume of water divided into equal spherical droplets, total surface area is A = 6V / d. Halving the diameter doubles the area.

That geometric relationship explains why atomization can increase contact area. It does not establish fire performance, dust capture or odour-removal efficiency.

Read the full distribution

Dv50 describes the diameter below which 50% of the liquid volume lies. Dv90 and Dv99 describe the 90% and 99% points. Keep the measurement method, pressure and sampling location with the result.

FOUR APPLICATIONS

Same working medium.
Different selection criteria.

APPLICATION 01

Fire suppression

Deliver the tested spray to the hazard.

Cooling, evaporation and interaction with the fire environment depend on the complete spray and installation. Coverage and transport are as important as droplet size.

Design inputs
Specify the protected hazard, enclosure, ventilation, obstructions, simultaneous discharge and water duration. Use the applicable fire-test evidence and the manufacturer’s installation requirements.
Selection check
Pump pressure alone does not establish suppression performance or a damage-free discharge. Confirm the approval boundary for the complete system.
Explore fire suppression ↗

APPLICATION 02

Dust suppression

Match the spray to the dust and airflow.

Nozzle arrangement affects both particle capture and the airflow induced by the spray. Enclosure and placement can change how much water reaches the dust cloud.

Design inputs
Define particle-size distribution, material flow, dust-release points, ventilation, water tolerance and drainage. Verify the result with exposure or process measurements.
Selection check
Select filtration for the nozzle’s minimum passage. Check the delivered spray under the actual operating and ventilation conditions.
Explore dust control ↗

APPLICATION 03

Odour control

Treat the source and the contact conditions.

Atomization distributes a treatment liquid. The outcome also depends on the odour compounds, selected treatment chemistry, concentration and available contact time.

Design inputs
Define the source, air movement, treatment area and compatible dosing method. Include drift, aerosol exposure, drainage and the measurement method for evaluating results.
Selection check
A finer spray or a higher pressure is not a guaranteed removal efficiency. Match the liquid and nozzle materials to the chosen treatment.
Explore Halo odour control ↗

APPLICATION 04

Industrial cleaning

Balance coverage, impact and water use.

A broad fan distributes flow over a surface; a coherent or rotating jet concentrates impact. The target material and stand-off distance determine what is useful.

Design inputs
State the soil or deposit, substrate, required finish, access, cycle time and wastewater handling. Assess guarding and reaction loads with the selected equipment.
Selection check
Keep the selected nozzle and supply circuit within their published pressure ratings, including peaks. Assess specialist hydroblasting and cutting equipment separately.

PRESSURE AND FLOW

Size for the nozzles that operate together.

Flow at the nozzle

For many fixed-orifice, single-fluid nozzles, flow varies approximately with the square root of pressure differential:

Q₂ = Q₁ × √(Δp₂ / Δp₁)

Use this relationship only where it agrees with the selected nozzle’s published data. Two-fluid atomizers and some nozzle designs require a different model.

Worked example

A nozzle rated at 6 L/min (1.59 US gpm) at 40 bar (580 psi) would deliver approximately 8.49 L/min (2.24 US gpm) at 80 bar (1,160 psi) under the square-root assumption.

Twenty nozzles operating at 6 L/min require 120 L/min (31.7 US gpm) at the nozzles. Add piping, valve, filter and elevation losses to the required supply pressure. Set any capacity allowance deliberately for the design duty.

Minimum pressure

Check the most demanding operating nozzle after losses through the supply circuit.

Maximum pressure

Check regulation, transients and blocked-flow conditions against every component limit.

Water condition

Specify water quality, filtration and maintenance access before finalizing the nozzle and pump selection.

NOZZLE SELECTION MATRIX

Six inputs for a useful enquiry.

01

Target

What must the water accomplish? Define the hazard, particle, odour source or surface and how success will be measured.

02

Geometry

Record the treatment area, throw distance, nozzle orientation, mounting, obstructions and required overlap.

03

Spray

Request the pattern, flow and measured droplet-size distribution at the actual working pressure and liquid conditions.

04

Hydraulics

Identify simultaneous nozzle demand, line losses, duty cycle, reserve supply and control sequence.

05

Materials

Provide water analysis, chemical dosing, temperature, solids and environmental exposure. Confirm the complete wetted-material and seal selection.

06

Acceptance

Agree the drawings, test evidence, approval responsibilities, commissioning checks and service information needed for the installation.

DESIGN AND PROCUREMENT

Questions before the model number.

Does higher pressure always mean better performance?

No. Pressure changes the operating point, but the useful result depends on flow, pattern, droplet distribution and delivery to the target. Use the nozzle and system data for the duty.

Can the same nozzle handle fresh water and seawater?

Confirm the body, orifice, inserts, seals and other wetted parts against the actual water chemistry and temperature. Do not assume that a stainless-steel designation alone establishes suitability.

How should filtration be specified?

Start with the nozzle manufacturer’s minimum-passage and filtration requirements. Include pressure loss, fouling allowance, monitoring, flushing and access for cleaning.

How long will a nozzle last?

Wear depends on the nozzle materials, water quality, pressure and duty. Inspect spray quality and check flow against the original operating point. Use the manufacturer’s service guidance rather than a generic life guarantee.

What should a fire-system enquiry include?

Include the protected hazard and applicable design basis, required flow and pressure, discharge duration, simultaneous zones and the approval responsibilities. Keep nozzle-supply pumping distinct from hydraulic valve actuation.

Can WaterQuest assess the nozzle and pump together?

Send the nozzle duty and available model data with the supply requirements. WaterQuest can review water-service equipment selection and the proposed integration scope within the selected equipment limits.

Bring the nozzle duty into the pump enquiry.

Share the application, selected nozzle data, simultaneous flow, pressure and working-water conditions.
