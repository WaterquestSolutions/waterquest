Evaluating a Water HPU for an Industrial Duty

See how a water HPU can serve your industrial duty.

Your power unit should support production, including starts, standby and recovery after a fault. Learn how to assess a water HPU against the complete machine cycle and the service conditions your team can maintain.

Article 061Testing & Adoption10 min readUpdated 19 October 2026

Choose a power unit that supports the production cycle

A useful HPU delivers the right pressure and flow when the machine needs them, then manages holding and standby appropriately. Water hydraulics offers that service without an oil-filled working circuit. Compare the proposed package against a schedule of starts, movements and recovery conditions so pump, drive and controls work together across the actual duty.

Begin by identifying the consumers and their required behaviour. Record which actuators operate simultaneously, which hold load and which must respond after standby or loss of a utility. Define the working water and environment. These inputs turn an equipment enquiry into an assessable problem. They also prevent two quotations with similar motor ratings from being treated as equivalent when one includes important controls, filtration or protective functions that the other leaves to the installer.

Convert actuator movements into flow demand

For a cylinder, the volume required for a movement follows from effective area and travel. Divide that volume by the required time to obtain an ideal average flow, then account for the actual circuit and component behaviour. Extension and retraction may require different flows because the cap-end and rod-end areas differ. If several consumers move together, the HPU must support the combined demand or a defined sequence that limits it. The operating logic is therefore part of the sizing basis.

A simple teaching example makes the timing visible. An actuator requiring 1.5 litres (0.396 US gal) over 15 seconds needs an ideal average supply of 6 litres per minute (1.59 US gpm) during that movement. Two identical simultaneous movements require 12 litres per minute (3.17 US gpm) before allowances. If they occur sequentially, the peak demand may remain 6 litres per minute (1.59 US gpm) but the overall sequence takes longer. These are independent volume calculations, not equipment selections. They show why a list of actuator sizes is insufficient without the timing and concurrency requirements.

Establish pressure where the load uses it

The HPU outlet pressure must support the actuator requirement plus relevant supply losses. Opposing pressure in the return chamber can reduce useful force. The calculation should therefore consider both sides of the actuator and the actual flow paths. A power unit that reaches a stated pressure with no flow may not demonstrate the loaded movement the owner needs. Evaluate pressure and flow together at the required operating points and within every component's supported limits.

The pump's current configuration-specific information is important, including the conditions behind its performance curves. The Water Hydraulics Company publishes purpose-designed pump data that can support selection, but the completed circuit determines installed performance. [2] Ask the supplier to identify the operating points used for the offer and any assumptions about temperature or supply. Where the duty includes rapid changes or significant line length, transient behaviour may also require assessment. A steady-state selection should not silently become a guarantee for every switching event in the installation.

Check the inlet and return arrangements as carefully as the outlet

An HPU cannot deliver dependable service without suitable liquid supply. Reservoir level, inlet geometry, restrictions and temperature affect the pump's operating conditions. Verify the selected pump's requirements at the relevant flow, including the least favourable credible supply condition. Do not assume that a reservoir physically above the pump proves adequate inlet performance. The complete route and configuration matter, and the manufacturer's instructions should govern the design rather than a generic rule about suction capability.

Return and case-drain conditions can be equally important. The offered assembly should identify which connections are required and their permitted conditions. A return path that is acceptable for one configuration may not suit another. Ask how the circuit manages unequal actuator volumes, make-up and any drain flow. These details are often less visible than the pressure port, yet they can affect seals, temperature and reliability. An integrated HPU evaluation follows the water through the complete package, including how it re-enters the supply or leaves the system.

Examine what happens between productive movements

Standby behaviour can dominate an intermittently used HPU's ownership. Determine whether the motor stops, unloads, maintains pressure or continues circulating flow. Each strategy affects response, energy, temperature and component cycling. A rapid restart requirement may justify one arrangement while a long idle period favours another. The offer should explain the sequence and its dependencies so that the owner can assess both readiness and consumption. A motor power rating alone does not reveal annual energy use.

The DOE's pumping-system guidance supports evaluating the operating schedule and system losses. [1] Request energy estimates or measurements for the relevant states, with a defined boundary that includes necessary auxiliaries. If the HPU needs cooling, heating or water treatment, those functions should be visible in the comparison. Do not infer installed savings from a pump efficiency at one operating point. A useful evaluation relates input energy to completed duty and time spent in each state over the expected operating calendar.

Review fluid management as an operating system

The reservoir and filtration arrangement must maintain conditions the components can tolerate. Ask how incoming water is prepared, how service contamination is controlled and how filter condition is indicated. Particles, dissolved chemistry and biological issues are different questions, so a filter rating alone does not define the fluid-management plan. Identify the approved water and treatment envelope. The owner needs to know what can be monitored on site and what changes require supplier or specialist review.

Thermal management also belongs in the package scope. Determine expected heat generation and how it will be rejected under the site's operating and ambient conditions. Water's heat capacity does not eliminate the heat balance. The Water Hydraulics Company's accessory range includes tanks, filters and heat exchangers, illustrating the supporting equipment that may form part of a complete solution. [4] The selected arrangement should be justified for the actual duty and accessible for maintenance, rather than included or omitted solely because it is a familiar standard package.

Read the control sequence before judging the control panel

A panel with many indicators is not necessarily a well-defined control system. Ask for the sequence of operation and the meaning of each relevant input and output. Identify commands, permissions, feedback and protective actions. Determine which functions reside in the HPU controls and which belong to the plant system. This division should remain clear during manual operation, automatic operation and communication loss. Unclear authority can create an integration problem even when every individual electrical component is suitable.

Evaluate the response to low water level, abnormal temperature, unavailable pressure and missing position feedback where these are relevant to the design. A protective shutdown may be appropriate for one condition, while another requires a controlled movement or maintained state. The safe response is application-specific. An emergency-stop label does not explain what happens to stored hydraulic or mechanical energy. The system risk assessment and applicable requirements must define the response, and the HPU offer should identify how its scope contributes to that function.

Make maintainability visible in the layout

Review where a technician stands, what must be isolated and how a component is removed. Filters, test points and drains should be accessible for the intended service tasks. A compact package can be poor value if a small repair requires dismantling unrelated equipment or removing the entire unit from a difficult location. Include lifting, spill collection and safe access in the layout review. These are ownership issues that can often be improved cheaply before manufacture.

Ask for component identification, approved spares and configuration-specific instructions. Determine which repairs can be performed locally and which need factory support. The electrical and hydraulic documentation should reflect the installed options and final settings. ISO 4413's public scope addresses hydraulic systems across design, installation and maintenance, but the full applicable requirements and authorised procedures still need project attention. [3] A maintainable HPU is one whose ordinary service tasks have been designed and documented, not merely one assembled from individually replaceable parts.

Test a matrix of operating states

A meaningful factory test should relate to the offered duty. Verify selected pressure-flow points, controls and protective functions under an agreed plan. Record the test conditions, instrumentation and results. Site acceptance then addresses the actual mechanism, piping, utilities and plant interfaces. The two stages have different purposes. A successful factory run cannot prove every site condition, while site commissioning should not be the first opportunity to discover that the package sequence was never clearly defined.

Include representative standby, restart and repeated-cycle behaviour. Check that the package can recover before the next demand and that temperatures remain within the supported envelope for the required duty. Approved abnormal-state tests should demonstrate the intended responses without creating uncontrolled hazards. If the acceptance case depends on a particular energy or movement result, define the measurement method before testing. This prevents a visually impressive demonstration from standing in for evidence of the performance that justified the purchase.

Compare offers through their complete scope and evidence

Create a comparison that distinguishes included equipment, owner-supplied interfaces, deviations and unresolved assumptions. A lower price may reflect a different scope rather than a better value. Conversely, additional equipment should have a clear function instead of being accepted because a larger package appears more capable. Ask each supplier to explain how the proposed HPU meets the defined operating states and what evidence supports the important claims. This makes technical and commercial evaluation part of the same decision.

WaterQuest can help translate an industrial duty into a supported water HPU arrangement with clear interfaces and service expectations. The central benefit is useful hydraulic motion with mineral oil removed from the selected circuit. That benefit becomes investable when pressure, flow, controls, fluid management and maintenance are coherent. Engineers can assess the performance, procurement can compare the offer and operations can own the result. A complete evaluation turns the power unit from a collection of components into an understandable source of plant service.
