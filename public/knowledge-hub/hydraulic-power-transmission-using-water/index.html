Hydraulic Power Transmission Using Water

Water as a hydraulic power transmission medium: power density, transmission distance, dynamic response, bulk modulus advantage, control precision, multi-actuator systems, and green hydrogen applications.

An electric motor converts electrical energy to rotational shaft power; the pump converts shaft power to fluid power; the hydraulic circuit transmits that fluid power , through pressure and flow , to the actuator, where it is converted back to mechanical work at the load. This article examines water as a hydraulic power transmission medium from a systems engineering perspective: power density, transmission distance and efficiency, dynamic response, control precision, and the application contexts where water's power transmission characteristics are not merely adequate but genuinely advantageous.

Hydraulic Power: The Fundamental Relationship

Hydraulic Power
P = Q × ΔP
P = power (W), Q = flow rate (m³/s), ΔP = pressure differential (Pa)

Practical units: P (kW) = Q (L/min) × ΔP (bar) / 600

This relationship is fluid-independent , it applies identically to water, oil, or any other hydraulic medium. A system delivering 20 L/min at 200 bar delivers 6.67 kW of hydraulic power whether the fluid is water or oil. The fluid choice affects the efficiency of generating and controlling that power, but not the fundamental power equation. There is no inherent power capacity penalty for using water as the medium , the power density of the hydraulic circuit is determined by pressure and flow, not fluid type.

Power Density: Compact Force from Small Cylinders

At 200 bar operating pressure, a 100mm bore water hydraulic cylinder generates 157 kN of force , approximately 16 tonnes , from a component with a cross-sectional area of roughly 0.008 m². No electric linear actuator of comparable physical size can approach this force output. This power density advantage is fully retained in water hydraulic systems because it is a consequence of pressure acting on area , pure physics that does not depend on the fluid's chemistry.

Water hydraulic cylinders are approximately 5 to 10% heavier than equivalent oil hydraulic cylinders in carbon steel, because stainless steel construction is marginally denser and wall thicknesses are slightly greater. In practice, this difference is not a significant factor in most industrial applications.

Transmission Distance and Line Losses

Water hydraulic systems are fully capable of long-distance power transmission. The higher piping friction losses of water compared to oil (due to turbulent flow at lower velocities) must be accounted for in the circuit design, but in correctly sized stainless steel piping, transmission losses are a small fraction of system operating pressure.

Transmission Loss Example , 50m Line
50m of 25mm ID 316SS pipe, 20 L/min at 3 m/s velocity:
Re = 998 × 3 × 0.025 / 0.001 = 74,850 (turbulent)
Darcy friction factor f ≈ 0.019 (Moody chart, smooth pipe)
ΔP = 0.019 × (50/0.025) × (998 × 3²/2) = 1.71 bar
= 0.86% of a 200 bar system pressure , entirely acceptable

Even at 50-metre transmission distances common in municipal infrastructure, pipe friction losses are a small fraction of system operating pressure in correctly sized stainless steel piping. Long-distance water hydraulic power transmission is fully practical with appropriate engineering.

Dynamic Response: Water's Stiffness Advantage

Water has a bulk modulus of approximately 2.2 GPa versus 1.5 to 1.8 GPa for mineral hydraulic oil , approximately 25 to 45% stiffer. The natural frequency of the hydraulic actuator-load system is proportional to the square root of the bulk modulus:

Hydraulic Natural Frequency
ωₙ ∝ √(B/m) | where B = effective bulk modulus, m = effective load mass

Water's higher bulk modulus increases natural frequency by factor √(2.2/1.65) ≈ 1.15
= 15% higher bandwidth potential vs oil

A 15% improvement in hydraulic natural frequency is meaningful in servo-controlled and proportional-controlled applications , it allows higher control bandwidths, faster disturbance rejection, and better tracking of dynamic command profiles. For applications requiring high dynamic performance , precision press control, fast-response gate actuation in flood control systems, servo-hydraulic testing equipment , water's bulk modulus advantage is a genuine performance benefit.

Additionally, the effective bulk modulus of oil degrades significantly with temperature and dissolved air content , a fluid containing 1% by volume of entrained air can see its effective bulk modulus reduced by 50% or more. Water is much less susceptible to these effects, making water hydraulic systems more predictably stiff across operating conditions.

Control Precision in Water Hydraulic Systems

Water hydraulic systems are fully compatible with the same proportional and servo valve control architectures used in oil hydraulic systems, with one important calibration requirement: control valves must be calibrated for water's viscosity and density.

Proportional valves used in water hydraulic systems produce approximately 15 to 25% higher flow at equivalent valve openings compared to oil service, due to water's lower viscosity. A valve specified for oil service and used without recalibration in a water system will have higher gain than intended , the system will be over-responsive and potentially unstable. Valve calibration for water service, or selection of valves with factory water calibration, is a basic commissioning requirement.

Once correctly calibrated, water hydraulic servo and proportional systems achieve positioning accuracies and dynamic performance levels comparable to oil hydraulic systems at equivalent pressure and actuator sizing.

Multi-Actuator Systems: Synchronisation and Load-Sharing

Water hydraulic systems support the same multi-actuator circuit topologies as oil systems: parallel circuits (all actuators share the same supply pressure), series circuits (actuators are daisy-chained), and individual pressure-compensated flow circuits (each actuator receives independent flow control). For applications requiring precise synchronisation , lifting systems where load skew must be minimised, gate operations where differential water pressure across the gate creates asymmetric loading , individual flow control with position feedback is the appropriate architecture in both oil and water systems.

Power Transmission in Green Hydrogen Applications

One of the most compelling emerging applications for water hydraulic power transmission is in green hydrogen production infrastructure. Electrolysis-based hydrogen production uses highly purified water as the feedstock, and operates at elevated pressures (30 to 80 bar for PEM electrolysers, up to 350 bar for high-pressure electrolysis designs) with all wetted components in 316 stainless steel or titanium.

The process fluid and the hydraulic working fluid are the same substance , ultrapure water. A water hydraulic power transmission system integrated into the electrolyser infrastructure can share the process water supply, eliminating the separate hydraulic fluid management system entirely. Valves, actuators, and control systems serve both the process control and the mechanical actuation functions, reducing component count, maintenance complexity, and system footprint.

WaterQuest Solutions, in partnership with CGIS, is developing integrated water hydraulic systems for green hydrogen production applications, including systems designed for wastewater treatment plant co-location where the treated effluent water quality is compatible with hydraulic service after appropriate conditioning. This integration represents one of the most technically elegant applications of water hydraulic power transmission , using the process medium as the power transmission medium, without the environmental liability of introducing a separate hydraulic fluid into the facility.

Key Takeaways

  • Water transmits hydraulic power through identical physics to oil , P = Q × ΔP , with identical power density at equivalent pressure and flow
  • Transmission distance is practical with correctly sized stainless steel piping; 50-metre runs at industrial flow rates produce less than 2 bar pressure drop in properly designed circuits
  • Water's higher bulk modulus (2.2 vs 1.65 GPa) provides 15% higher hydraulic natural frequency , a genuine advantage in servo and dynamic applications
  • Control precision is fully comparable to oil systems once proportional and servo valves are correctly calibrated for water's viscosity and density
  • Green hydrogen production represents the defining emerging application for water hydraulic power transmission, where process fluid and hydraulic medium integration provides both technical elegance and operational simplicity
