How Engineers Can Specify Oil-Free Hydraulic Systems

A complete practical guide for engineers specifying water hydraulic systems: operating pressure selection, fluid supply and quality, material non-negotiables, HPU and cylinder component specifications, documentation requirements, and structured commissioning procedure.

A decision made in an office, to use water rather than oil as the hydraulic working fluid, translates into components, systems, and installations that will operate for 20 to 40 years in the field. Getting the specification right means that the system delivers on the environmental, operational, and lifecycle cost promises of the water hydraulic choice. This article is written to be directly usable: the specification language, evaluation criteria, and commissioning requirements described here can be adapted directly into project documents.

Step 1: Define the System Boundary and Performance Requirements

Operating Pressure Selection

Water hydraulic systems operate most efficiently in the 150 to 250 bar range for most industrial actuation applications. The optimal pressure depends on the specific force, speed, and installation envelope requirements:

  • Municipal gate actuator applications: 150 to 200 bar provides adequate force margins for standard gate sizes without requiring the highest-specification pump and valve components
  • High-force applications (large penstock valves, flood control gate arrays): 200 to 250 bar provides better power density and component economy
  • Mining roof support and high-cycle industrial presses: 250 to 350 bar may be specified

Fluid Supply and Quality Requirements

The engineer must specify the water quality requirements for the hydraulic working fluid and the source of that water. Options include:

  • Municipal potable water supply: Generally adequate quality (low chloride, stable pH) for 316SS water hydraulic systems with minimal additional conditioning
  • Process water from the facility: Must be evaluated for chloride content, pH stability, and potential contamination from process chemicals before use as hydraulic working fluid
  • Demineralized or reverse osmosis water: The highest quality option, with very low conductivity and chloride content providing maximum corrosion protection and longest component life
  • Deionized water with inhibitor package: Standard specification for facilities without existing water treatment. A package inhibitor designed for 316SS systems is added at commissioning and maintained within concentration limits throughout service.

Freeze Protection Requirements

For outdoor installations or unheated spaces in Canadian climate conditions, the engineer must specify the freeze protection approach as part of the system design. Options are trace heating and insulation, heated enclosure, antifreeze additive (food-grade propylene glycol at 20 to 30% by volume for freeze protection to minus 8 to minus 16 degrees C), or operational drainage for seasonal installations. Freeze protection requirements drive conduit and wiring specifications, insulation materials, and power supply requirements that must be included in the civil and electrical scope.

Step 2: Specify Materials

Minimum Material Specification

All wetted components: 316L stainless steel (UNS S31603). No aluminium, no carbon steel, no cast iron, no copper alloys in any wetted or hydraulic-fluid-contact application. All dynamic seals: CF30-PEEK or filled PTFE. No NBR, no polyurethane, no standard elastomers in continuous water contact. All flexible hose: PTFE-lined inner tube, 316SS wire braid, stainless steel swaged end fittings. All fasteners in wetted assemblies: A4-70 stainless steel minimum.

The specification should also explicitly prohibit substitutions without written approval: All wetted component materials shall be as specified. No substitutions or equivalent materials shall be used without written approval from the Engineer of Record. Proposals for alternative materials must include full corrosion testing data, material certifications, and written confirmation of compatibility with the hydraulic working fluid chemistry specified for this project.

This prohibition is necessary because hydraulic component suppliers accustomed to oil hydraulic specifications may propose substitutions that are standard in oil service but entirely inappropriate for water hydraulic service.

Step 3: Component-Level Specifications

Hydraulic Power Unit (HPU)

  • Reservoir: 316L stainless steel, welded, full drain-down capability, 100-mesh inlet strainer, vented to atmosphere, pH and conductivity monitoring port, sample valve
  • Pump: Axial piston type rated for water hydraulic service (not adapted from oil design), manufacturer's written confirmation of water service suitability, volumetric efficiency above 90% at rated pressure and temperature, continuous pressure rating not less than 1.25 times maximum operating pressure
  • Motor: TEFC construction minimum IP54, direct-coupled to pump with flexible coupling, sized for pump power demand plus 25% service factor
  • Pressure relief valve: 316SS body, PEEK seat, set at 1.15 times maximum operating pressure, rated flow not less than full pump output at relief setting
  • Pressure filter: 316SS housing, 10 micron absolute glass fibre element, differential pressure indicator with electrical contact for remote monitoring
  • Instrumentation: Pressure transmitter (4 to 20 mA, 0 to 400 bar range), temperature transmitter (4 to 20 mA, 0 to 100 degrees C range), reservoir level switch, all rated IP67 minimum

Cylinders

  • Barrel: 316L SS seamless tube, honed bore Ra 0.15 to 0.40 micrometres, wall thickness designed for operating pressure with 4:1 safety factor on yield strength, calculation documentation required
  • Rod: 316 SS minimum; hard chrome on 17-4PH for applications with rod surface hardness requirement; Ra 0.10 to 0.20 micrometres finish, no spiral machining marks, passivated per ASTM A967
  • Piston seal: CF30-PEEK U-cup, rated to 1.5 times operating pressure, groove dimensions per seal manufacturer's recommendation for PEEK material at specified pressure
  • Rod seal: CF30-PEEK U-cup primary seal, PTFE composite wiper, dual-seal arrangement with intermediate drain port for systems requiring leakage monitoring
  • End caps and gland: 316SS, all threaded connections in A4 stainless fasteners with MoS2 thread compound
  • Load holding: Pilot-operated check valves on both ports, 316SS body, rated to 1.5 times operating pressure, mounted integral to cylinder or within 300mm of cylinder port

Control Valves and Piping

  • Directional control valves: 316SS body, PEEK spool bushings, rated for water service at specified pressure, MSS SP-161 certification for municipal water service applications
  • Pressure relief valves: 316SS body, PEEK seat, adjustable within 20% of set point without seal replacement
  • Check valves: 316SS body, PEEK or stainless seat, spring-loaded with cracking pressure documented
  • Tube: Seamless 316SS per ASTM A269, wall thickness per ASME B31.3 hydraulic tubing pressure rating at operating pressure with 4:1 safety factor
  • Fittings: Bite-type 316SS (Swagelok, Parker CPI, or approved equal) or orbital welded joints
  • Hose: PTFE-lined, 316SS braid, swaged stainless end fittings, rated to 1.5 times operating pressure, maximum 2-year replacement interval regardless of condition
  • Supports: 316SS clamps at maximum 300mm spacing, vibration-isolating mounting at machinery interfaces

Step 4: Testing and Certification Requirements

  1. Material Test Reports (MTRs): Chemical composition and mechanical properties for each heat of 316 stainless steel used in pressure-containing components. MTRs must confirm molybdenum content 2.0 to 3.0%.
  2. Passivation Certificates: ASTM A967 passivation verification for all machined stainless steel components.
  3. Pressure Test Certificate: Hydrostatic pressure test at 1.5 times MAWP for all completed cylinder and valve assemblies, with test pressure, duration, and inspector signature documented.
  4. Pump Performance Test: Volumetric efficiency test at rated pressure and speed, with test fluid properties (water at specified temperature) documented.
  5. Seal Material Certification: Material composition certificate confirming CF30-PEEK or filled PTFE specification for all dynamic seals. Generic polymer certifications are not acceptable.
  6. Water Quality Analysis: Pre-commissioning water sample analysis confirming pH, conductivity, chloride, dissolved oxygen, and inhibitor concentration within specification before system pressurisation.

Step 5: Commissioning Requirements

  1. System flush: Low-pressure flush (20 to 30 bar) at maximum flow rate for minimum 30 minutes, or until return fluid cleanliness meets ISO 4406 class 17/15/12, whichever is later. Flush return fluid to waste, not to reservoir.
  2. Filter change: Replace all filter elements after flush completion before proceeding to pressure testing.
  3. Pressure test: Hydrostatic test of completed system at 1.5 times MAWP for 30 minutes with all isolation valves open. No visible leakage permitted.
  4. Break-in procedure: Cycle all cylinders at 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100% operating pressure: 50 cycles at each pressure level, monitoring for leakage and abnormal motion at each stage.
  5. Water quality verification: Post-commissioning water sample analysis confirming all parameters within specification after break-in.
  6. SCADA integration test: Verify all 4 to 20 mA signals, discrete I/O, and protocol communications function correctly with plant control system before handing over.
  7. O&M documentation: Operations and maintenance manual including water quality management programme, seal replacement procedures with replacement kit part numbers, troubleshooting guide, and spare parts list delivered before commissioning sign-off.

Key Takeaways

  • Correct water hydraulic specification requires: operating pressure selection matched to application requirements (150 to 250 bar typical); fluid supply quality specification; non-negotiable material requirements (316L stainless throughout all wetted components, CF30-PEEK dynamic seals, PTFE-lined hose, A4 stainless fasteners, no aluminium/carbon steel/NBR/PU)
  • Substitution prohibition language is essential: hydraulic component suppliers accustomed to oil hydraulic specifications may propose substitutions standard in oil service that are entirely inappropriate for water hydraulic service
  • Documentation requirements including MTRs, passivation certificates, pressure test certificates, and seal material certifications must be specified before procurement commitments are made
  • The structured commissioning procedure (flush, filter change, pressure test, break-in protocol, water quality verification, SCADA integration test) must not be abbreviated: each step protects against failure modes in subsequent steps
