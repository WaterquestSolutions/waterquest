Judging a Water HPU by What It Does Between Movements

Move, hold, recover and fail safely: test all four states. A well-designed water HPU uses little energy while it waits.

Written by WaterQuest Solutions · Updated 2 November 2026 · About the distributor

Choose a power unit that supports the production cycle

Begin by identifying the consumers and their required behaviour. Record which actuators operate simultaneously, which hold load and which must respond after standby or loss of a utility.

Convert actuator movements into flow demand

For a cylinder, the volume required for a movement follows from effective area and travel. Divide that volume by the required time to obtain an ideal average flow, then account for the actual circuit and component behaviour. If several consumers move together, the HPU must support the combined demand or a defined sequence that limits it.

A simple teaching example makes the timing visible. An actuator requiring 1.5 litres (0.396 US gal) over 15 seconds needs an ideal average supply of 6 litres per minute (1.59 US gpm) during that movement. Two identical simultaneous movements require 12 litres per minute (3.17 US gpm) before allowances. If they occur sequentially, the peak demand may remain 6 litres per minute (1.59 US gpm) but the overall sequence takes longer.

Establish pressure where the load uses it

The HPU outlet pressure must support the actuator requirement plus relevant supply losses. Opposing pressure in the return chamber can reduce useful force.

The pump's current configuration-specific information is important, including the conditions behind its performance curves. The Water Hydraulics Company publishes purpose-designed pump data that can support selection, but the completed circuit determines installed performance. Ask the supplier to identify the operating points used for the offer and any assumptions about temperature or supply.

Check the inlet and return arrangements as carefully as the outlet

An HPU cannot deliver dependable service without suitable liquid supply. Reservoir level, inlet geometry, restrictions and temperature affect the pump's operating conditions. Do not assume that a reservoir physically above the pump proves adequate inlet performance.

Return and case-drain conditions can be equally important. The offered assembly should identify which connections are required and their permitted conditions.

Examine what happens between productive movements

Standby behaviour can dominate an intermittently used HPU's ownership. Determine whether the motor stops, unloads, maintains pressure or continues circulating flow. A motor power rating alone does not reveal annual energy use.

The DOE's pumping-system guidance supports evaluating the operating schedule and system losses. Request energy estimates or measurements for the relevant states, with a defined boundary that includes necessary auxiliaries. Do not infer installed savings from a pump efficiency at one operating point.

Review fluid management as an operating system

The reservoir and filtration arrangement must maintain conditions the components can tolerate. Ask how incoming water is prepared, how service contamination is controlled and how filter condition is indicated. Particles, dissolved chemistry and biological issues are different questions, so a filter rating alone does not define the fluid-management plan. Identify the approved water and treatment envelope.

Thermal management also belongs in the package scope. Water's heat capacity does not eliminate the heat balance. The Water Hydraulics Company's accessory range includes tanks, filters and heat exchangers, illustrating the supporting equipment that may form part of a complete solution. The selected arrangement should be justified for the actual duty and accessible for maintenance, rather than included or omitted solely because it is a familiar standard package.

Read the control sequence before judging the control panel

A panel with many indicators is not necessarily a well-defined control system. Ask for the sequence of operation and the meaning of each relevant input and output.

Evaluate the response to low water level, abnormal temperature, unavailable pressure and missing position feedback where these are relevant to the design. A protective shutdown may be appropriate for one condition, while another requires a controlled movement or maintained state. The safe response is application-specific. An emergency-stop label does not explain what happens to stored hydraulic or mechanical energy. The system risk assessment and applicable requirements must define the response, and the HPU offer should identify how its scope contributes to that function.

Make maintainability visible in the layout

Review where a technician stands, what must be isolated and how a component is removed. Filters, test points and drains should be accessible for the intended service tasks. Include lifting, spill collection and safe access in the layout review.

Ask for component identification, approved spares and configuration-specific instructions. ISO 4413's public scope addresses hydraulic systems across design, installation and maintenance, but the full applicable requirements and authorized procedures still need project attention. A maintainable HPU is one whose ordinary service tasks have been designed and documented, not merely one assembled from individually replaceable parts.

Test a matrix of operating states

A meaningful factory test should relate to the offered duty. Verify selected pressure-flow points, controls and protective functions under an agreed plan. A successful factory run cannot prove every site condition, while site commissioning should not be the first opportunity to discover that the package sequence was never clearly defined.

Include representative standby, restart and repeated-cycle behaviour. Check that the package can recover before the next demand and that temperatures remain within the supported envelope for the required duty. Approved abnormal-state tests should demonstrate the intended responses without creating uncontrolled hazards.

Compare offers through their complete scope and evidence

Create a comparison that distinguishes included equipment, owner-supplied interfaces, deviations and unresolved assumptions. A lower price may reflect a different scope rather than a better value.
