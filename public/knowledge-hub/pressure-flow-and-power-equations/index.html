Pressure, Flow, Power: The Three Equations Behind Every Water Circuit

Force is pressure times area, flow is area times speed, power is pressure times flow. Water obeys the same physics as oil, so every claim can be checked.

Start Here7 min read

Written by WaterQuest Solutions · Updated 2 November 2026 · About the distributor

Connect pressure to force and flow to useful movement

For a cylinder, the connection to mechanics is direct. Pressure acting on a piston area creates a force, while the volume of fluid entering the changing chamber determines movement. Those conditions depend on the load and the circuit, not just the maximum pressure printed on the power unit. This is why a hydraulic review must include both the pressure and flow required during each part of the sequence.

Water and oil hydraulics use the same fundamental relationships. Changing the medium changes component design and operating considerations, but it does not change conservation of energy or permit a cylinder to deliver an arbitrary force from a fixed pressure and area. OpenStax's treatment of Pascal's principle provides an accessible foundation for pressure transmission and force relationships.

Keep units visible through the calculation

The SI unit of pressure is the pascal, equal to one newton per square metre. A bar is 100,000 pascals (14.5 psi). An area of 50 square centimetres equals 0.005 square metres, not 0.05 square metres. At a pressure difference of 100 bar (1,450 psi), that area would produce an ideal force of 50,000 newtons.

A useful engineering calculation records the units alongside every input. This matters when equipment information combines bar, megapascals, litres per minute and millimetres.

Force also needs a clear meaning. A force in newtons is not itself a mass in kilograms, and a rated force does not establish the allowable load of a complete lifting mechanism. Gravity, geometry, acceleration and friction connect the two.

Distinguish gauge pressure, absolute pressure and pressure difference

Gauge pressure is measured relative to the surrounding atmospheric pressure. Absolute pressure is measured relative to a vacuum reference.

Instrument data should state the reference. If a supplier specifies an inlet requirement and the site records only a gauge reading, the engineer must establish how to compare them correctly. A display reading of zero gauge pressure does not mean the fluid is at zero absolute pressure.

Pressure difference is also the useful quantity for assessing a component loss. A filter inlet reading alone does not show its differential pressure; the outlet condition matters. Likewise, pump outlet pressure does not directly establish actuator pressure when connecting lines and valves consume part of the available pressure difference.

Account for both sides of a piston

A double-acting cylinder has fluid on both sides of its piston. During extension, cap-end pressure acts over the full piston area, while rod-end pressure acts over the annular area. Friction and other mechanical effects are then considered separately. Ignoring return-side pressure can overstate available force, especially where the return path is restricted or the circuit intentionally maintains back pressure.

The rod also creates a speed difference. For the same supplied volume rate, the smaller annular area produces a higher ideal piston speed than the larger full area.

A useful drawing identifies the active areas, the direction of motion and the two chamber pressures. A bore size alone is not a complete actuator specification.

Use continuity to connect flow with speed

For an ideal cylinder chamber, flow equals effective area multiplied by piston speed. If the area is 0.005 square metres and the required speed is 0.02 metres (0.0656 ft) per second, the chamber needs 0.0001 cubic metres per second, equivalent to six litres per minute. A half-metre stroke at that speed takes 25 seconds and displaces 2.5 litres (0.66 US gal) on that side.

Real supply requirements must account for leakage and the behaviour of the selected circuit. A pump's theoretical displacement multiplied by shaft speed describes theoretical delivery; actual useful flow can be lower.

The continuity relationship is especially useful in diagnosis. It does not follow that the pressure rating is too low.

Connect hydraulic power to useful work

At a defined boundary, hydraulic power is pressure difference multiplied by volume flow. A convenient equivalent is pressure in bar multiplied by flow in litres per minute, divided by 600, giving kilowatts. Thus, 100 bar (1,450 psi) at 12 litres per minute (3.17 US gpm) represents two kilowatts of hydraulic power. This is not the electrical input required by a real power unit; drive and pump losses must be included.

Mechanical output power for linear motion is force multiplied by speed. When an ideal cylinder calculation is internally consistent, the hydraulic and mechanical expressions agree.

Energy adds the dimension of time. Two kilowatts maintained for one minute corresponds to about 0.033 kilowatt-hours. System-level energy guidance is useful because it encourages assessment of the full service rather than a single pressure-flow point.

Recognize that real circuits are compliant

Liquids are often treated as incompressible in introductory calculations because that approximation is useful for many steady conditions.

This matters when pressure builds, the load changes or a valve command is reversed. Some supplied volume may initially change the compressed or expanded state of the system rather than appear immediately as piston movement.

Water-property references provide formulations within stated temperature and pressure ranges. They are useful inputs to engineering calculations, but they do not replace a model of the installed equipment. Treat fluid properties as part of the system description rather than a complete prediction of it.

Separate steady behaviour from short events

A circuit that operates below its selected steady pressure limit can still require attention to transient behaviour. A slowly updating gauge may not capture a brief event, so instrument selection must match the phenomenon being investigated.

The appropriate response is a coordinated design review of commands, flow paths, protection devices and component limits. Hydraulic-system safety guidance addresses the system as a whole; the applicable standard and project requirements need to be used for an actual design.

For an owner, the useful acceptance evidence is therefore more than a photograph of a gauge below a maximum value.

Use the physics to improve the purchasing question

A specification becomes clearer when it distinguishes the quantities that determine the task. State the required motion and load, the available time, the operating sequence and the conditions at the interfaces.

This approach makes the case for water hydraulics more persuasive where the application fits. It is neither an exception to the physics nor a reason to accept less evidence about force, movement, energy or safe operation.
