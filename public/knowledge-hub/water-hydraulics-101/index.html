Water Hydraulics 101: Full Hydraulic Force, Without the Oil

Purpose-built water pumps, valves and cylinders deliver pressure-times-area force without hydraulic oil. The working fluid is non-flammable, and oil changes and used-oil disposal leave the maintenance plan.

Written by WaterQuest Solutions · Updated 2 November 2026 · About the distributor

Picture a gate powered by water

Water carries the power; a motor, engine or other drive supplies the energy that turns the pump.

Begin with three practical questions: what must the machine do, how will the circuit deliver the movement, and where does removing oil help? Together, the answers connect the environmental benefit to equipment the owner can specify, operate and maintain.

Follow the energy through the circuit

The drive turns a pump, which supplies flow to a circuit. Pressure develops in response to the resistance encountered by that flow.

For an ideal cylinder under a simple pressure difference, force follows pressure multiplied by effective area. Flow determines how quickly fluid fills the changing chamber volume. These relationships explain why pressure and flow must be specified together. Conversely, abundant flow does not establish that the actuator can overcome the maximum load.

A larger piston can create more force at the same pressure, but it also needs more water for the same travel.

Work through a simple cylinder example

Consider an illustrative cylinder whose effective pressurized area is 0.004 square metres. Applying a pressure difference of 80 bar (1,160 psi) produces an ideal force of 32,000 newtons, because 80 bar (1,160 psi) equals eight million pascals. If the piston travels at 0.04 metres (0.131 ft) per second, the ideal chamber flow is 0.00016 cubic metres per second, or 9.6 litres per minute (2.54 US gpm). The useful mechanical power in this simplified example is force multiplied by speed: 1.28 kilowatts (1.72 hp).

The calculation is a teaching model, not a selected assembly. A real double-acting cylinder has pressures acting on both sides, and the rod reduces the effective area on one side. Friction, valve losses and acceleration must also be considered. A pressure rating cannot establish whether a slender rod is safe against buckling or whether an attachment can withstand a side load.

The example turns a movement requirement into pressure and flow that can be checked against equipment data. It also gives the project team a shared basis for comparison: the selected circuit must deliver both values together while completing the required movement.

Understand what changes when oil is removed

Conventional hydraulic oil performs several jobs: it transmits pressure, participates in lubrication, carries heat and interacts with surfaces and seals. Water can transmit pressure effectively, but it does not reproduce every other property of the oil it replaces. Filling an unsupported oil component with water is therefore a different proposition from buying equipment engineered for water service.

Low viscosity is one of the important differences. It can reduce some resistance to flow while allowing more leakage through a given clearance. A material name alone cannot establish its service life.

The Water Hydraulics Company's P3 pump is an example of commercially documented water-service equipment. It does not prove that every pump, valve or actuator attached to it is compatible. The complete circuit needs a consistent water-service specification, including the small internal components that may be invisible in a general arrangement drawing.

Choose an architecture that suits the application

In a recirculating arrangement, fluid returns for reuse after passing through the actuator or other working element. The design must manage that returning fluid, maintain an adequate supply and control contamination and temperature.

Separate motion control from process pumping. An actuator circuit uses hydraulic power to move machinery. Cylinder movement, load holding and stopping behaviour do not replace membrane-feed requirements, and a desalination pump curve does not establish actuator control performance.

This distinction becomes especially useful when a site contains several water-related services. There may be opportunities to share an approved utility or maintenance capability, but a common fluid name is not sufficient reason to connect systems.

Make fluid management part of normal operation

A suitable water source is an operating input that needs to remain suitable. Suspended particles can damage or obstruct equipment; dissolved substances may affect deposits and material compatibility; temperature changes influence both fluid properties and component limits.

Begin with the manufacturer's fluid requirements and identify how the installation will meet them. Provide appropriate sample points, access to filters and a practical method for replacing or replenishing fluid.

Make routine service straightforward for the operating team. Provide access to valves and filters, identify consumables and explain the checks that reveal changing condition.

Evaluate failure behaviour alongside normal movement

A system that performs well during a demonstration may behave differently after a hose failure, a lost command signal or an interruption to power. The correct response depends on the process and cannot be inferred from the use of water.

Stored pressure, a suspended load or mechanical spring energy can remain hazardous after the drive stops. Isolation and maintenance procedures must address those sources. Pressure limitation is another distinct function: it protects against specified overpressure conditions but does not automatically provide safe load holding, safe access or protection against every transient. Public information on ISO 4413 identifies hydraulic-system safety as a system-level subject; conformity requires the applicable requirements and evidence for the actual design.

This is why a procurement specification should request an operating sequence and failure-state description, not merely a list of components.

Build a business case around the problem being removed

Start the business case where oil removal solves a specific problem.

Energy assessment should include the actual duty cycle. Measurements from one component or one laboratory condition should not be presented as a guaranteed whole-installation saving.

Send WaterQuest the movement, load, operating schedule and reason oil exposure matters. A drawing, photograph or short operating sequence is a useful starting point.
