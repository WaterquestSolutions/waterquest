Hydrogen Propulsion, Water-Hydraulic Deck Gear: A Clean Fishing Vessel Concept

Propulsion and deck machinery are separate decisions. A hypothetical hydrogen-electric fishing boat shows how water hydraulics could take the oil off the deck.

Industry Applications6 min read

Written by WaterQuest Solutions · Updated 2 November 2026 · About the distributor

Separate the propulsion decision from the auxiliary decision

This separation prevents a misleading all-or-nothing debate. Conversely, selecting hydrogen as part of an energy strategy does not prove that every hydraulic function should use water.

Trace energy through the complete chain

In one possible concept, stored hydrogen supplies a fuel-cell system, electricity reaches a distribution system, and electric drives operate propulsion or auxiliary equipment. The vessel's required range and service schedule must be assessed using the complete architecture rather than only the efficiency of one component.

A water-hydraulic auxiliary circuit adds another energy path: an electric motor drives a pump, pressure and flow reach control equipment, and an actuator moves the load. Water does not supply free energy; it transmits energy supplied by the drive.

Define the fishing duty before choosing machinery

A fishing vessel's auxiliary demands may be intermittent, highly variable and operationally important. A hauler can experience a different load pattern from a winch, hatch or handling mechanism.

The operating crew should contribute to this definition. Their experience can reveal awkward recovery situations, deck obstructions, service constraints and practices that a simple equipment schedule misses.

Hydraulic force still follows pressure and effective area

For a linear actuator, ideal force depends on pressure acting over effective area, with opposing chamber pressure included. Changing the working medium does not change that relationship. The actual delivered force also depends on friction, mechanical geometry and operating conditions. OpenStax provides the underlying pressure-and-force explanation.

This makes water hydraulics a credible candidate for suitable force-intensive functions, but it is not proof that an existing oil-service actuator can be refilled. The Water Hydraulics Company's water-pump information illustrates that dedicated equipment has fluid and installation requirements. A concept should identify candidate equipment families and unresolved compatibility questions before presenting a compact drawing as a complete solution.

Identify the oil pathway that would actually disappear

Deck machinery can place hydraulic lines and actuators near catch, working surfaces and the surrounding water.

The boundary should be stated plainly. Converting a deck circuit does not mean the entire vessel is free of lubricants, fuels or other potential contaminants.

Fresh water and seawater are different engineering services

A vessel has ready access to seawater, but that does not make seawater automatically suitable as the hydraulic working medium. A freshwater circuit may require storage and management, while a seawater design requires explicit support for its exposure.

A recirculating arrangement also differs from a once-through arrangement. Water consumption, contamination control and the route of a leak must be evaluated for the chosen architecture.

Stored energy and suspended loads remain important

A pump stopping does not necessarily remove energy from a hydraulic system. The required behaviour after loss of power must be defined for each function. Holding, controlled lowering and moving to a safe position are different objectives, and one arrangement may not serve every condition.

The design must also consider crew access during recovery and maintenance. Manual controls should not create an unexpected movement or expose someone to a suspended load. Removing hydraulic oil can address one hazard pathway while leaving the mechanical consequences of force and stored energy essentially unchanged. A responsible concept keeps both ideas visible instead of treating a non-oil medium as a complete safety argument.

Hydrogen introduces its own integration questions

Hydrogen storage, delivery, ventilation, detection and emergency arrangements require specialist assessment within the selected vessel architecture.

An owner should ask which organization coordinates the overall integration and which approvals apply to the vessel category and intended service. Transport Canada's construction and equipment guidance illustrates the importance of determining the applicable framework rather than assuming one rule covers every vessel. Small-vessel construction guidance is separately relevant where that category applies. The cited construction-and-equipment bulletin expressly excludes fishing vessels and pleasure craft from those regulations, illustrating why category matters. These public resources orient the enquiry; they do not establish approval of the hypothetical concept described here.

Build an evidence sequence before promising outcomes

A practical development sequence begins with a duty study, proceeds through an architecture review and resolves critical uncertainties in controlled tests. Bench testing may assess a component or circuit under representative loads. Any onboard trial requires the appropriate authorized plan, responsibilities and operating limits.

For the hydraulic portion, useful evidence includes motion performance, leakage behaviour, temperature, energy consumption and maintainability over a relevant cycle. For the vessel, additional evidence is needed for stability, range, operational capability and the complete safety arrangement.

Evaluate operating capability alongside emissions

An emissions discussion needs a stated boundary. The vessel's onboard operation, the production and delivery of its energy carrier, equipment manufacture and maintenance are different accounting scopes.

Operating capability is equally important. System-level energy assessment helps connect machinery choices to actual duty rather than isolated peak-efficiency values. An arrangement that fits the owner's service pattern and support network has a stronger adoption case than one that depends on optimistic assumptions about every future operating condition.

Give the owner a decision rather than a promise

A useful concept package would show the proposed functions, energy paths, equipment boundaries, unresolved questions and staged verification plan. It would also state what is already known, what is estimated and what has not been authorized.

WaterQuest's relevant contribution is to explain where purpose-designed water hydraulics could perform a specified auxiliary function and reduce a defined hydraulic-oil exposure. The wider vessel strategy belongs to the owner and its authorized engineering partners.

Where it fits

This is a hypothetical fishing-vessel concept. Propulsion, deck machinery and vessel approvals remain separate engineering decisions.
