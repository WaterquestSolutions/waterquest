Driving a Water Pump With an Air Motor

An air motor can drive a water pump where the installation supports that arrangement. Match torque at speed, air consumption, lubrication and hazardous-area approval.

Components & Materials6 min read

Download the worksheet for this guide (editable text) · Open the motor requirement register (CSV)

Written by WaterQuest Solutions · Updated 2 November 2026 · About the distributor

Connect the air supply to a useful water-pumping duty

Armak's published range includes air motors and supporting equipment such as controls, gearing and brakes. The relevant selection is the exact offered model and configuration. This article explains the integration questions; it does not claim that every Armak motor is compatible with every WaterQuest pump or that a particular combined assembly has been approved. A useful proposal must identify how the drive, coupling, pump and controls work together under the intended duty.

Draw the complete energy path

Compressed air arrives at the motor with a pressure and flow capability. The motor produces shaft torque and speed, which a coupling or gearbox transmits to the driven pump.

This matters when comparing alternatives. An air-driven water pump may be attractive where a suitable air service already exists or where the drive arrangement solves a particular installation problem.

Match torque and speed to the pump duty

A pump needs sufficient input torque at the required rotational speed to deliver the intended pressure and flow. The drive selection must support the relevant operating points, including starting under the actual conditions. A motor's maximum torque and maximum speed do not necessarily occur together.

Mechanical power relates to torque and angular speed. In an independent teaching example, 10 newton metres at 1,000 revolutions per minute is approximately 1.05 kilowatts (1.41 hp) of shaft power. That arithmetic does not establish available starting torque, efficiency or a product rating.

Consider starting and changing loads

The pump's demand changes with pressure, speed and operating conditions. Starting against a loaded circuit can differ from starting with an appropriate unloaded path. Do not assume that a motor's ability to stall or tolerate a changing load makes every coupled pump arrangement safe or suitable.

If the process requires controlled speed, evaluate how that speed is maintained as load and air supply vary. A simple air-flow adjustment may not provide the required regulation over every condition.

Evaluate the air supply at the motor

The pressure available at a compressor or remote header is not necessarily the pressure reaching the motor during demand. Distribution losses, hoses, filters, regulators and simultaneous plant use can affect the local supply. The motor's air consumption must be supported by the installation.

Air quality and preparation must follow the selected motor's instructions. Do not assume that all air motors share the same lubrication or moisture requirements. Armak's published accessories include air preparation and control equipment, which illustrates that the supply arrangement is part of the application. Confirm the exact requirements and include the necessary supporting scope.

Include exhaust conditions and the working environment

An air motor discharges air after extracting useful energy. The exhaust arrangement can affect noise, local conditions and motor performance. Any silencer or exhaust routing must suit the selected motor and its permitted backpressure.

Environmental selection also includes ambient temperature, moisture, dust and any hazardous-area requirements. A manufacturer's range-level statement is not evidence that every assembled option is approved for a particular installation.

Engineer the mechanical connection

The coupling and mounting must transmit the required torque while maintaining the alignment and loads permitted by both machines.

The Water Hydraulics Company's pump information includes installation considerations for mechanical drive arrangements. Use current instructions for the selected model and coordinate them with the motor's requirements.

Define controls across both fluid systems

The air-side control determines the drive's operation, while the water-side controls determine hydraulic pressure, flow and actuator behaviour. Stopping the air supply does not necessarily depressurize the water circuit or restrain a mechanically driven load. The combined sequence must identify how normal stop, emergency response and maintenance isolation are achieved.

ISO 4413's public scope provides context for the hydraulic side, but the complete installation also has pneumatic, mechanical and electrical considerations as applicable. The responsible design and risk assessment should define the necessary measures. Control authority needs to be clear where the package connects to plant automation. This makes the integration understandable to operators and prevents an air-side event from producing an unexpected water-side state that was never considered during component selection.

Compare energy at a consistent boundary

The energy required to supply compressed air depends on the generating and distribution system, including its controls and losses. A motor's local air consumption does not directly state the compressor electricity attributable to the duty. The DOE and NREL compressed-air evaluation guidance addresses system boundaries and measurement methods relevant to that assessment. For an investment decision, use a consistent boundary when comparing the air-driven package with an alternative drive.

Include operating duration, idle behaviour and any change in compressor operation. A small intermittent demand and a continuous demand can have different consequences for the plant air system.

Verify the coupled duty before treating it as a package

A complete enquiry should provide required water pressure and flow, operating schedule, available air conditions, environmental requirements and the driven load. A successful motor test and a successful pump test performed separately do not establish the behaviour of the coupled installation under its actual utility conditions.
