Tap Water or Seawater? Choose Clean Freshwater Inside

A coastal site does not require seawater inside the actuator. Compare a clean freshwater circuit and external salt exposure as separate design decisions.

Choose the working water before the components

This guide: Freshwater versus seawater

Vessel functions · Coastal exposure · Installation and voyage logistics

A coastal location does not automatically require seawater inside an actuator circuit. The external environment may expose the equipment to salt spray while a recirculating circuit uses controlled freshwater. Conversely, an installation that pumps seawater as part of a process cannot avoid internal saline exposure merely by protecting its enclosure.

Define what the available water actually contains

A useful fluid description identifies source, temperature and relevant chemistry, together with particles and treatment chemicals. Provide available analyses and explain when and where samples were taken.

Physical appearance is not an adequate compatibility test. Clear water can contain dissolved salts or treatment constituents, while cloudy water may contain particles that a suitable treatment stage can remove. Filtration does not necessarily address dissolved chemistry, and a chemistry result does not establish particle cleanliness.

Consider the complete seawater supply arrangement

Seawater may be abundant at a site, but available volume is only part of the supply question. An intake, screening, treatment, storage or boost arrangement may be needed to deliver the required conditions. The supply must remain suitable during operation and the credible low-supply state.

The inlet conditions at the selected pump need configuration-specific confirmation. A static gauge reading elsewhere in the supply network does not establish that condition. The Water Hydraulics Company's pump information highlights the importance of fluid and inlet configuration when selecting purpose-designed water equipment. For a project, request current instructions for the exact model and avoid transferring details from another pump or a general web page into the final design without verification.

Understand why chloride exposure changes material selection

Stainless steel is a family of alloys, not a guarantee of immunity. Chloride-containing environments can create local corrosion concerns whose severity depends on temperature, geometry, deposits and other conditions. A stainless body label does not establish the suitability of internal springs, plugs, fasteners or mating parts.

This does not mean seawater service is impossible. It means the material package must be chosen for it.

Compare recirculating and once-through operation

A recirculating hydraulic circuit returns its working fluid for reuse. Its water condition can change through contamination, temperature, make-up and service activities.

For an illustrative once-through duty, a flow of 5 litres per minute (1.32 US gpm) operating for 12 minutes uses 60 litres (15.9 US gal) during that period. That arithmetic says nothing about whether the discharge is acceptable or whether the circuit should be once-through. The useful comparison includes all supporting equipment and the conditions it must maintain. It does not compare raw-fluid prices while ignoring the system around them.

Evaluate freshwater as an engineered alternative

Controlled freshwater can simplify some internal corrosion questions in a coastal actuator installation, but it still requires a supply and management plan.

Freshwater is not automatically the same as the component manufacturer's reference fluid. Treatment residuals, hardness, pH or other constituents may require review. This approach avoids replacing one assumption with another: seawater is not inherently unsuitable for all water equipment, and tap water is not inherently suitable for every configuration.

Include shutdown, flushing and preservation

Operating conditions change when equipment stops. Water can remain in small cavities, lines and valve passages for long periods. If a seawater circuit will be flushed with another fluid, confirm that the transition and final stored condition are supported by the components. A general instruction to flush thoroughly is incomplete unless the method can reach the relevant volumes and the operator can verify the intended result.

Do not improvise additives or cleaning chemicals because they appear compatible with the main metal body. Seals, polymers and instruments may have different limits, and residues can alter subsequent operation. The supplier should identify the approved procedure and any return-to-service checks.

Separate corrosion data from a service-life promise

Material reference data can help screen candidates, but it is not a direct prediction of assembled equipment life. Outokumpu's corrosion tables explain that such data must be interpreted in the context of actual service. Use them to inform questions and selection, then seek configuration-specific support and appropriate verification for the proposed installation.

The same principle applies to an isolated successful example. A component that has worked in one seawater duty may have had different temperature, maintenance or material options. This gives procurement a way to handle uncertainty without demanding an unsupported universal lifetime guarantee or accepting one at face value.

Make fluid management practical for the operator

A selected fluid envelope must be maintainable. Identify which conditions can be monitored on site and which require periodic sampling or supplier review.

Keep approved fluids, treatments and procedures with the asset documentation. Label fill and drain connections clearly, especially where freshwater and seawater systems coexist. Train staff to recognize that an available water connection is not automatically an approved make-up source.

Choose the complete arrangement with the best fit

The final comparison should bring together fluid availability, material package, inlet supply, treatment, recirculation, discharge and maintenance. Compare installed and recurring costs on the same duty. The preferred answer may be a freshwater actuator circuit in a saline external environment, or a specifically supported seawater configuration where the process requires it.
