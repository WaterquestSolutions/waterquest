Ten Questions Engineers Ask About Water Hydraulics — Answered

Same force as oil? What water quality? Freezing, power loss, energy? Straight answers on why water is a serious alternative.

Start Here7 min read

Download the worksheet for this guide (editable text) · Open the system requirement register (CSV)

Written by WaterQuest Solutions · Updated 2 November 2026 · About the distributor

Put water to work in a familiar hydraulic circuit

Purpose-built pumps, valves and cylinders use water to transmit force without hydraulic oil.

The phrase does not identify one universal circuit. An installation may recirculate its working water or use another defined supply arrangement. Fresh water and seawater also create different equipment requirements. Once those are clear, engineers can assess the design, procurement can identify the complete scope and operators can understand what must be supplied and maintained.

Can water produce the same force as oil?

Yes. Equal pressure on equal piston area gives equal ideal force; subtract return pressure and friction.

The ideal force relationship depends on pressure and effective area, with pressure on the opposing side included. It does not depend on choosing oil rather than water. A supported water-hydraulic actuator can therefore be assessed for a specified force requirement using the same basic physics. Actual performance also includes friction, leakage, mechanical geometry and the conditions under which the load moves.

The question becomes more useful when it includes speed, travel and duty cycle. An actuator may reach a required force briefly yet be unsuitable for continuous operation or for the necessary movement rate.

Can an existing oil system simply be filled with water?

No. Review and replace incompatible wetted components before changing the fluid.

That should not be assumed. Oil-service components may rely on the original medium for lubrication, sealing behaviour or corrosion control. A familiar pressure rating or a stainless exterior does not establish internal compatibility.

The Water Hydraulics Company's dedicated pump information illustrates that water-compatible machinery has configuration and installation requirements. A retrofit review should classify existing items as supported for reuse, requiring replacement or unresolved pending further information. Refilling without that review can create a poor test of water hydraulics because it is really testing an unsupported combination of components and fluid.

How are corrosion and wear controlled?

Match metals, polymers, seals and clearances to the water, temperature and sliding duty.

Corrosion and wear are different mechanisms and should be investigated separately. Corrosion depends on materials and their environment, including fluid chemistry, temperature and local conditions such as deposits or crevices.

Outokumpu's corrosion guidance provides useful background on the environmental factors involved in stainless selection. For purchasing, request the supported fluid range and the construction selected for the duty. For operation, preserve the approved configuration and service conditions. The answer is not that water needs no protection or that stainless steel never corrodes.

What water quality does the system need?

Use the exact equipment maker’s water-quality and filtration specification.

The answer belongs to the selected equipment and architecture. A municipal supply, treated process water, recirculated working water and seawater are not interchangeable descriptions. A clear-looking sample is not a complete specification, and potable source water does not guarantee that water remains potable after circulating through machinery.

Separate particle removal from treatment of dissolved substances or biological conditions. These are different tasks and may require different controls. The operating plan should identify the approved supply, filling procedure, inspection or monitoring requirements and the response to an unsuitable condition.

What happens in cold weather or during a power failure?

Design freeze protection and the power-loss safe state as separate requirements.

Cold exposure must be assessed through operation, standby, shutdown and maintenance. Remote tubing and small trapped volumes may be vulnerable even when the main package is sheltered. Insulation reduces heat transfer but cannot maintain temperature indefinitely without an appropriate heat source.

Power loss creates a separate question about motion and stored energy. CCOHS guidance highlights stored and residual energy within hazardous-energy control. The required response should be defined for each function: holding, controlled movement and maintenance isolation have different objectives. A professional proposal explains those responses instead of treating the working medium as a complete answer to either freezing or machinery safety.

Will the system use less electricity?

It depends on the complete duty, losses and standby controls; compare measured input at equal output.

It may, but the result depends on the complete arrangement and operating schedule. A comparison must deliver the same useful work and include the same supporting services. An isolated efficiency value at one favourable point does not establish annual savings for the installed machine.

The Department of Energy's pumping-system guidance supports examining the whole system. Ask the supplier to show active and idle states, operating hours and the basis for each consumption estimate. This approach gives finance a reviewable assumption and gives engineering a practical verification task rather than an unsupported percentage to defend.

Does removing oil eliminate contamination risk?

It removes the converted circuit’s hydraulic-oil source; hygiene and other contamination routes remain.

Removing hydraulic oil from a defined circuit removes that substance from the circuit's potential release.

Other risks remain. Working water can acquire contaminants, other machine lubricants may still be present, and a release can cause flooding or electrical damage. A water-hydraulic circuit is not automatically a hygienic process-water system or an approved discharge source.

What will maintenance and ownership cost look like?

Oil-handling tasks disappear, but water, filters, seals and access still contribute to ownership cost.

Begin with tasks rather than a universal maintenance-saving percentage. A component's purchase price does not describe the cost of restoring service when it fails in a difficult location.

Availability and logistics deserve their own review. Ask about spare identification, lead times, technician requirements and the work the owner's team can perform. A water-hydraulic project can be worthwhile through a combination of operating capability and reduced exposure even when it does not have the lowest initial price.

What should be in a serious supplier enquiry?

Give the supplier loads, travel, cycle times, water, temperatures, interfaces and acceptance criteria.

Provide the load, movement, duty cycle, environment, working-water information and required fault response.

Distinguish guarantees, relevant tests, estimates and unresolved assumptions. ISO 4413 provides hydraulic-system scope, not a certificate for a particular installation. The enquiry should also assign integration responsibilities so that essential controls or utilities do not fall between suppliers.

At acceptance, keep the measurements that define normal performance, including the relevant load, temperature and operating sequence.

A final review should include the people who will operate and maintain the machinery. Their questions about access, abnormal conditions and replacement parts often reveal practical gaps before installation and final commissioning.
