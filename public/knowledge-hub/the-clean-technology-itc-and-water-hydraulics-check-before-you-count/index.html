The Clean Technology ITC and Water Hydraulics: Check Before You Count

The 30 % federal credit covers listed clean-energy property, not hydraulic machinery by default. Here's how to tell whether your project's property qualifies.

Economics & Funding6 min read

Written by WaterQuest Solutions · Updated 2 November 2026 · About the distributor

Keep the equipment case clear while eligibility is checked

The credit’s dates and rates

The federal Clean Technology ITC is refundable and may cover up to 30% of eligible capital cost for qualifying property acquired and available for use from 28 March 2023 through 2033. The maximum rate falls to 15% for 2034.

This guide: Tax-credit eligibility

Program screening · R&D versus routine adoption

This article explains a screening workflow for engineers and buyers. It does not determine a taxpayer's entitlement or recommend a tax position. Keeping these roles clear prevents a marketing statement from becoming an unsupported financial assumption.

Check the property, claimant and timing

Screen the project using the CRA’s current property and claimant rules before putting a credit into the financial case.

Start with the property, not the desired saving

CRA's current guidance identifies defined types of clean-technology property and conditions applying to them. It does not list ordinary water-hydraulic machinery as a universal qualifying category. A component's use of water to transmit power should not be confused with equipment generating electricity from water energy.

Prepare a functional description of what the proposed asset does. An accurate classification exercise may conclude that an otherwise valuable hydraulic project does not qualify, which is a useful result before management commits to spending based on an assumed credit.

The claimant is a separate eligibility question

Property screening and claimant screening are distinct. An asset that appears technically relevant does not establish that every owner can claim the same incentive. CRA provides separate guidance on who can claim the Clean Technology ITC. The actual purchasing or owning entity and its arrangement must be assessed, rather than substituting the supplier's status or a partner's eligibility.

This matters in projects involving municipalities, related companies, partnerships or leased equipment. The entity benefiting from the machinery may not be the entity incurring every cost.

A component within a larger project needs a defined role

A water-hydraulic assembly may be supplied as part of a wider energy or industrial project. That relationship does not automatically mean the assembly inherits every tax attribute of the larger project. The relevant assessment must identify the property, its function and how the applicable rules treat it.

For example, an actuator operating an auxiliary mechanism is not necessarily classified in the same way as the primary energy-producing asset nearby.

Keep an equipment evidence package

A useful evidence package includes the quotation, technical description, drawings, selected model information and installation scope. It should identify what the equipment actually does and what is excluded.

The package should also identify assumptions still awaiting confirmation. A preliminary concept may not yet establish the final ownership, installation location or equipment function.

Separate cost categories before applying any percentage

An equipment project can contain acquisition, installation, engineering, preliminary studies, training and other costs. CRA's property guidance discusses capital-cost treatment and exclusions, which require project-specific review.

For the engineering team, the practical step is to preserve the cost breakdown and purpose of each activity. Do not combine every invoice into one clean-technology total and expect an adviser to reconstruct the distinctions later.

Other assistance can affect the calculation

A project may be considering contributions, grants or other tax measures at the same time. Their interaction must be reviewed under the applicable rules.

Keep a register of support requested, expected, awarded and received. Those are different statuses.

Timing deserves a project timeline

Engineering programs contain several dates: contract award, progress payments, delivery, installation, testing and the start of intended use.

Changes can also occur after the initial assessment. A delayed installation, revised equipment function or different ownership arrangement may require another review.

Model the project before and after potential assistance

The base case should show the engineering investment without unconfirmed support. A separate scenario can show the effect of assistance if it is established.

Use the same technical assumptions in both scenarios unless the funded project scope genuinely differs. A water-hydraulic project can have a sound business case through performance or reduced exposure even where this particular credit is unavailable; equally, a potential credit should not rescue machinery that does not meet the duty.

Do not confuse a purchase with experimental development

A team may investigate other support when the equipment does not fit a property-based credit. That does not make an ordinary purchase eligible for research assistance. NRC IRAP and SR&ED have their own purposes and requirements. The project must be described honestly, distinguishing application of established knowledge from work intended to resolve a genuine scientific or technological uncertainty.

If real development work exists, document it separately from routine procurement and installation. It also prevents the financial model from moving an unsupported assumption from one program column to another.

Make the tax review a defined decision point

Assign responsibility for obtaining the project-specific assessment and identify what information is still needed. The purchasing team should know whether the credit is confirmed for modelling purposes, still under review or excluded from the case.
