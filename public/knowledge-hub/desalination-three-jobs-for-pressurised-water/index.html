Desalination: Three Different Jobs for Pressurized Water

Membrane feed, energy recovery and valve actuation do different jobs. A worked 10 m³/h balance separates their flows, pressures and energy use.

Industry Applications7 min read

Download the worksheet for this guide (editable text) · Open the ro requirement register (CSV)

Written by WaterQuest Solutions · Updated 2 November 2026 · About the distributor

Give each desalination duty the right equipment

Start with a functional drawing that identifies each fluid stream and each mechanical duty. An oil HPU supplying an auxiliary cylinder is not performing the same job as a seawater feed pump. A valid comparison must give both options the same useful output, whether that is accepted permeate production or a specified sequence of valve movements.

DOE's desalination overview explains the role of membrane separation in producing lower-salinity water. It provides process context, while the selected membranes and complete plant design determine the actual operating requirements. The hydraulic equipment should be selected to support that process rather than being allowed to define the water-production objective by itself.

Establish a water balance before discussing recovery

For a simple steady-flow reverse-osmosis balance, feed entering the membrane system divides into permeate and concentrate. If the assumed feed is 10 m³/h (44 US gpm) and permeate production is 4 m³/h (17.6 US gpm), concentrate is approximately 6 m³/h (26.4 US gpm), neglecting other streams and changes in stored volume. The water recovery is 4 divided by 10, or 40 percent. This is an illustrative balance, not a recommended operating point.

Water recovery and energy recovery describe different things. Water recovery is a volume relationship.

Real operating accounts should identify flushing, cleaning, start-up diversion and other relevant flows separately. Product delivered to the user's storage boundary may differ from instantaneous permeate production.

Calculate pressure power with the correct flow

Hydraulic power in kilowatts is pressure difference in bar multiplied by flow in litres per minute, divided by 600. A hypothetical feed of 10 m³/h (44 US gpm) equals about 166.7 L/min (44 US gpm). At a 60 bar (870 psi) pressure difference, that flow carries approximately 16.7 kW (22.4 hp) of hydraulic power.

The pressure difference must match the chosen component boundary. If an inlet already has useful pressure, the high-pressure pump's required increase differs from its discharge gauge pressure.

Keep units explicit. A flow in cubic metres per hour cannot be inserted directly into the litres-per-minute version of the equation.

Understand the opportunity in concentrate pressure

Concentrate can leave a membrane system with pressure that retains useful energy. The theoretical energy associated with the stream is an upper bound, not a promise of recovered electrical power.

Using a separate hypothetical condition, 6 m³/h (26.4 US gpm) of concentrate passing through a usable 55 bar (798 psi) pressure difference represents approximately 9.2 kW (12.3 hp) of hydraulic power. A real recovery device cannot be assumed to return all of it as a reduction at the electrical meter.

The Water Hydraulics Company's Power Centre describes an integrated pumping and concentrate-energy arrangement. It is relevant as an example of architecture designed around the process streams.

Protect the feed-pump inlet as carefully as the discharge

A high-pressure pump needs a supported inlet condition. The available supply can change with tank level, upstream pump operation, filter loading, pipe losses and water temperature.

Locate instruments where they can establish the condition experienced by the pump. A sensor upstream of a restrictive component does not automatically describe the pressure downstream during flow. Those responses must align with the selected equipment and approved operating sequence.

Manufacturer requirements belong to the exact supplied machinery. General information about water pumps, including the P3 example, shows why fluid and inlet conditions are part of an equipment enquiry. It is not a substitute for the seawater feed pump's own documentation or a reason to apply one manufacturer's value to unrelated process equipment.

Connect materials selection to every process stream

A plant contains feed, concentrate, permeate and possibly cleaning solutions with different characteristics. Components suitable for one stream are not automatically suitable for another.

The hydraulic equipment supplier needs to understand any intended cleaning or preservation sequence that reaches its components. A membrane cleaning arrangement may require isolation or a different route through the plant. Do not assume that a pump remains compatible simply because its normal feed is acceptable.

External exposure is a separate question. A coastal installation may have corrosive atmospheric conditions even around equipment handling relatively low-salinity water.

Include auxiliaries and rejected production in energy reporting

Specific energy consumption divides electrical energy by a defined volume of useful water. Neither is inherently wrong, but the boundary must be visible.

Use matching time intervals for energy and accepted product volume. Excluding that energy while including only later productive operation may be suitable for a steady-state equipment test, but it does not describe total operating consumption.

The DOE pumping sourcebook supports evaluating supply, demand and operating patterns together. For desalination, that means testing representative production levels and including the states that influence annual service. A favourable best-point result should be retained as useful evidence while remaining distinct from a duty-weighted estimate of future plant consumption.

Keep auxiliary actuators connected to process consequences

Valves and gates can determine whether the process starts, isolates or recovers correctly. Their hydraulic supply should be designed around required force or torque, movement time, holding and fault response.

Define which system owns the operating sequence and which signals confirm valve position or readiness. A request from the plant controller and an energized hydraulic valve do not establish that the process valve completed its travel.

Where water actuation replaces an oil auxiliary circuit, identify the removed oil inventory and the remaining interfaces.

Purchase a supported operating envelope

A useful enquiry includes feed conditions, required product quality, production range, operating schedule and site constraints.

Acceptance should test the agreed function under representative conditions and explain how deviations from the design basis will be handled. A short trial cannot establish multi-year life, but it can demonstrate the operating point and reveal integration problems.

Water hydraulic technology contributes to desalination when its machinery and energy-transfer arrangements fit the process.
