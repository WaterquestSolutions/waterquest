The customer benefit

Turn the daily water requirement into a plant that can be operated and maintained.

A nameplate capacity tells only part of the story. The plant must deliver usable water through changing demand, filter changes and scheduled servicing. A modular arrangement makes the production trains and their service boundaries clear. Water-lubricated high-pressure pumps keep oil lubrication out of the pumping circuit, while the complete package connects feed treatment, membrane operation, flushing and controls to the site’s supply plan.

How water does the work

See how water does the work.

Each RO train uses a high-pressure pump to feed its membranes. A purpose-designed water-lubricated pump uses the pumped water to lubricate its internal working surfaces. Trains can be arranged to suit staged capacity and maintenance needs; shared pretreatment, electrical supply and controls are included when assessing how much water remains available during an outage.

01

Daily output is not the same as instantaneous flow.

A package may operate fewer than 24 hours per day because of maintenance, electricity scheduling or storage operation. Convert net daily delivery into running permeate flow, then use the selected recovery to establish feed and concentrate flow. Include flushing and pretreatment losses outside the simple membrane balance.

02

Isolation makes modularity useful.

Each train needs service access, safe depressurisation and controls that allow a planned outage. Shared headers, check valves, instrumentation and flushing connections should be designed so taking one module offline does not cause an unintended flow path through another. Define standby preservation and restart routines.

An illustrative calculation

Test the one-train-out case

Three illustrative trains producing 1 m³/h (4.4 US gpm) of permeate each for 20 hours per day deliver 60 m³/day (15,850 US gal/day). With one train unavailable, remaining output is 40 m³/day (10,567 US gal/day) before other losses.

Available daily output = available trains × permeate flow/train × run hours

Build the commercial case

Connect the equipment choice to the operating result.

Compare net usable output and service availability, rather than nominal capacity alone. Include shared equipment, storage, utilities, operator time, consumables and planned maintenance in the cost of supplying the site.

  1. Define the improvement. Record the present duty, service effort and operating problem. Agree the movement, output or availability the new equipment must deliver.
  2. Specify and demonstrate the package. Match components to the actual water, load and operating range. Use agreed commissioning checks or a focused trial to confirm the result.
  3. Compare the complete ownership cost. Include equipment, installation, utilities, fluid management, servicing and downtime. Use measured results where available and identify estimates clearly.

Questions customers ask

Make an informed equipment choice.

What is the benefit of water-lubricated pumping?

A purpose-designed water-lubricated pump uses the pumped water to lubricate its internal working surfaces, keeping oil lubrication out of that pumping circuit. The right model depends on water chemistry, flow, pressure and inlet conditions. WaterQuest helps match those requirements to the equipment.

Does adding a spare pump create full redundancy?

Only for the failure covered by that spare. Intake, pretreatment, electrical supply, controls and post-treatment can still interrupt all trains.

Can more modules be added later?

Only if shared services, headers, space, electrical capacity, intake and discharge arrangements allow expansion. Reserve that capacity explicitly during the first design.

Modular Scalability

Parallel operation with a defined redundancy margin

The trend towards modularity in mid-size and large SWRO plants fuels the uptake of parallel-coupled trains of positive displacement pumps with energy recovery and AC drive. This flexible architecture supports continuous capacity adjustment, redundant operation, and cost-effective expansion without replacing the entire system.

Continuous Capacity Adjustment

The capacity of the system can be adjusted continuously according to the need for water at any given time to save energy. Variable demand is addressed without oversizing a single pump or running at inefficient partial load. Each unit operates at its design point while the number of running units changes to meet production requirements.

Maintenance Redundancy

Independent Flow Control

The flow on a single pump can be adjusted without affecting the other pumps. This means that the AC drive can be sized for one pump only, while the rest of the pumps run directly online. The simple configuration with parallel-coupled positive displacement pumps saves money on the AC drive and returns significant energy savings during operation.

Modular Expansion

Additional units can be installed in parallel to expand capacity without redesigning the system architecture. A plant can start with a single unit and grow incrementally as demand increases. Each new unit plugs into the existing manifold. Capital expenditure is staged to match actual growth rather than forecast, reducing financial risk and improving return on investment.

Example Configuration: 1,500 m³/day (396,258 US gal/day) Seawater RO Plant

A regional water authority requires 1,500 m³/day (396,258 US gal/day) of potable water from seawater. The plant is designed using three P600 Power Centre units operating in parallel.

Base Configuration
  • 3 × P600 Power Centre units
  • Each unit: 21.3 m3/hr (93.8 US gpm) × 24 hr = 511 m³/day (134,992 US gal/day)
  • Total capacity: 1,533 m³/day (3% overdesign margin)
  • Operating pressure: 55 bar (798 psi) continuous
Drive & Control Strategy
  • Unit 1: Variable frequency drive (VFD) for flow control
  • Units 2 & 3: Direct online start (no VFD required)
  • Total VFD cost: One 40 kW (53.6 hp) drive vs. three 40 kW (53.6 hp) drives
  • Capital savings: ~60% on drive infrastructure
  • All three units share common feed and brine manifolds
Operating Scenarios
  • Peak demand (100%): All three units run at full speed = 1,533 m³/day (404,976 US gal/day)
  • Standard demand (67%): Two units at full speed, Unit 1 at variable speed = 1,022 m³/day (269,984 US gal/day)
  • Low demand (33%): One unit at variable speed = 511 m³/day (134,992 US gal/day) (or lower)
  • Maintenance mode: Two units maintain 1,022 m³/day (269,984 US gal/day) while one undergoes service
Energy & Cost Benefits
  • Specific energy: ~1.5 kWh/m3 per unit (with integrated energy recovery)
  • Total system power at full load: ~120 kW (vs. ~400 kW (536 hp) without energy recovery)
  • No pulsation dampers required (9-piston design inherently smooth)
  • No high-pressure booster pump or standalone ERD motors
  • System can be commissioned in hours per unit, not days

Technical Reference

Engineering Notes

Inlet conditions All PB (boosted) pumps require a minimum positive inlet pressure of 2.5 bar (36.3 psi) at the pump port. External boost pump or pressurized water main required. Feed water must be pre-filtered to 5 micron (197 µin) nominal (beta 10 = 75 or better). Pressure protection switch mandatory at inlet.
Materials of construction Duplex build: bar stock Duplex 2205 throughout all water-contacting parts. Suited to high-salinity seawater. Hybrid build: 316L stainless steel body with Duplex 2205 bearing surfaces. Suitable for brackish water and moderate salinity. Contact WaterQuest Solutions for material selection guidance.
Drive coupling Pumps must not carry induced axial or radial shaft loads. 3-part gear coupling required unless drive shaft concentricity is within 0.05 mm. B5 electric motor flange standard on P6 to P60. SAE 4-bolt flange standard on P180 and P600.
Energy figures Specific energy values are indicative at stated pressures and speeds. Actual consumption depends on system efficiency, pre-treatment, and energy recovery device integration. Power Centre packages achieve up to 70% energy savings versus non-recovery systems through integrated brine energy recovery.
Temperature range Full rated performance from 2 C to 50 C. Higher temperature operation is possible with reduced volumetric efficiency. Consult WaterQuest Solutions for elevated temperature duty specifications.
Flushing protocol Routine fresh water flushing is mandatory. During any period of non-operation, the system must be flushed with permeate to displace seawater from wetted surfaces. Weekly fresh water flushing is recommended as a minimum regardless of build specification. Failure to flush will cause accelerated corrosion even on Duplex builds.
Membrane selection Membrane model numbers are representative examples. Final selection must be confirmed with qualified membrane supplier based on site-specific feedwater analysis and target permeate quality. All systems require complete balance of plant including intake strainer, media filtration, cartridge filtration, instrumentation, pressure vessels, and brine disposal.
Power Centre commissioning Power Centre packages feature plug-and-desalt installation with only four flexible hose connections and no high-pressure welded pipework. System commissioning can be completed in hours rather than days. Recovery percentage is set at design stage and delivered to system designer specification. Flow is proportional to shaft speed, removing the need for flow instrumentation.
Parallel operation Multiple units can operate in parallel with common feed and brine manifolds. AC drive required on one unit only for variable capacity control. Remaining units run directly online. Supports unlimited capacity scaling, maintenance redundancy, and continuous capacity adjustment to match demand profiles.
ATEX compliance All pumps rated II 2GD ck T6 T85 C; compliant with Directive 94/9/EC. ISO 9001 Quality Management (FM 87247). Suitable for Zone 1 and Zone 21 atmospheres. Safe for use in potentially explosive environments.
Service intervals Standard pump packages: Routine inspection at 4,000 hours, full service at 8,000 hours. Power Centre packages: 8,000-hour service intervals with no oil changes.
