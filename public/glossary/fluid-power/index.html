Fluid power in water hydraulics

Fluid power transfers energy through a pressurised liquid or gas to create movement. Water hydraulics uses water to carry that energy from the pump to a cylinder or motor, providing hydraulic force without oil in the working circuit.

Water Hydraulics Glossary

Fluid power

Fluid power uses a pressurised liquid or gas to transmit energy and control movement. Hydraulics uses liquids, including oil or water; pneumatics uses gases, usually compressed air. Pumps or compressors supply the circuit, and actuators turn that energy into useful motion.

FundamentalsKey idea

Follow the energy: drive → pump → controls → actuator → load. Water hydraulics is one branch of fluid power.

What it means

A fluid-power system connects an energy source, a working fluid, controls and an actuator. An electric motor or engine can drive a pump; valves then route or regulate flow to a cylinder or hydraulic motor. Sensors and controls coordinate the required movement.

Pressure and flow are related but serve different roles. At a given actuator size, pressure difference influences force or torque, while flow influences speed. Losses in the circuit mean the useful output is less than the energy supplied.

Why it matters in water hydraulics

Water hydraulics applies these same principles with components designed for the selected water medium. It can be useful where the consequences of hydraulic-oil leakage are a major concern, but it still needs appropriate water quality, corrosion control, wear materials and maintenance.

Replacing the liquid in an existing oil circuit is not a complete conversion. Review the pump, valves, actuator, seals, piping and controls as a system. A material suitable for one water chemistry or temperature may be unsuitable for another.

Practical example

To open a process-water gate, a motor drives a pump, a directional valve sends water to a cylinder, and the cylinder moves the gate through its linkage. Flow controls the opening speed; available force depends on chamber pressures and piston areas.

A complete design also addresses pressure relief, load holding, position feedback and the required response to loss of power. In a recirculating circuit, returning water is collected, conditioned as required and supplied to the pump again.

Formula and design notes

Ideal linear output power: P = F × v

Hydraulic power: P = Δp × Q

In SI units, force in N multiplied by speed in m/s gives W. Pressure difference in Pa multiplied by flow in m³/s also gives W. Real input and output differ because of leakage, friction and other losses.

What to check before specifying

  • The load, movement, speed, cycle time and required safe state.
  • The working medium, environment and consequences of a leak.
  • Energy use, maintenance access and compatibility throughout the circuit.
