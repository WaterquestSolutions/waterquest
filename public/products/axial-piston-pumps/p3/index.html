Configuration Choice

Unboosted or Boosted:
Which P3 Do You Need?

Understanding the difference between 2-port unboosted and 3-port boosted determines your reservoir arrangement, pipework sizing, shaft speed ceiling, and seal life expectancy.

2-Port Unboosted

PC160-3.0W / PA160-3.0W

Standard configuration for tank-fed or low-pressure mains supply. Best choice for most HPU power pack installations. Max 1800 rpm.

Supply water comes from a local tank or low-pressure mains. Maximum inlet pressure 2.5 bar at all times.
Two ports only: P (pressure outlet) and T (tank inlet). The pump cannot lift water; inlet pressure must remain positive under all dynamic conditions.
Supply pipework must be maximum bore and minimum length. Inlet flow must be at least 20% above maximum delivery flow at the lowest tank level.
Do not place a filter between the tank and pump unless a negative pressure protection switch is installed. Filter all water entering the tank.
Ports are marked P and T. Reversing connections or wrong shaft rotation direction causes immediate pump failure. PC = clockwise, PA = anti-clockwise.
Maximum shaft speed 1800 rpm unboosted.

3-Port Boosted

PB160-3.0W

Required when inlet supply pressure is 2.5 bar or above, or when shaft speeds above 1800 rpm are needed. Extends shaft seal life significantly. Max 2000 rpm.

Three ports: pressure outlet (P), water inlet, and a separate case drain port (T). Internal leakage is routed away through the T port to a low-pressure sump or drain.
Minimum inlet supply pressure 2.5 bar at all times, from a high-pressure main, a column of water exceeding 25 metres, or an external boost pump.
Minimum positive inlet pressure must be at least 2.0 bar above T-port (case drain) pressure at all times. Maximum casing pressure at T port: 2.5 bar.
Separating leakage from the inlet prevents low-pressure cavitation inside the casing, significantly extending shaft seal life and enabling high-duty-cycle operation.
Boosted pumps rotate in either direction. Inlet and outlet ports change with shaft rotation direction. Bi-directional from a single pump body and part number.
Maximum shaft speed 2000 rpm boosted vs 1800 rpm unboosted. Higher ceiling allows a smaller motor at equivalent flow or increased flow from the same frame.

Engineering Detail

P3 Design and
Construction

Axial Piston Architecture

Multiple pistons distribute load across sliding contact surfaces simultaneously, reducing localised wear and producing genuinely smooth flow without the pulsation that a single or dual-piston arrangement would generate. Water acts as the only coolant and lubricant throughout.

Single Fixed Displacement

The P3 has a fixed displacement of 3.0 cc/rev. Flow is directly proportional to shaft speed. A VFD on the motor provides proportional flow control. For variable displacement, step up to the P60 or P180.

Material Selection for Water

Internal sliding surfaces are specified for water lubrication. Standard construction handles tap water and technical water. For seawater service a noble material internal package is specified at order time using fluid code S.

Shaft Seal and Leakage Management

In unboosted PC and PA configurations, a lip seal controls shaft leakage. In the boosted PB configuration case leakage is routed through the T port to a low-pressure drain, removing pressure differential across the shaft seal and significantly extending seal life.

Filtration Requirements

All water entering the pump must be pre-filtered to 10 micron nominal (25 micron absolute), beta-10 = 75 minimum. On tank-fed systems, filter water at the point it enters the tank rather than between the tank outlet and pump inlet.

Temperature and Fluid Compatibility

Full rated performance from 2°C to 50°C. Above 50°C volumetric efficiency reduces; consult WaterQuest. Monopropylene Glycol antifreeze available for sub-zero installations. Glycol 95/5 mixtures are compatible with clearance adjustment. Specify the exact operating fluid on all enquiries and orders.

Frequently Asked Questions

P3 Pump
Technical FAQ

Choose the PB boosted configuration when your inlet supply pressure is 2.5 bar or above, when you need the higher 2000 rpm shaft speed ceiling, or when the application runs at high duty cycle and extended shaft seal life is a priority. The boosted design routes internal leakage to a separate case drain port, eliminating back-pressure across the shaft seal. For standard HPU installations fed from a local reservoir, the unboosted PC or PA is generally the correct and simpler choice.

The T port case drain on the boosted PB must be piped to a low-pressure sump or drain at a maximum casing pressure of 2.5 bar. Keep the drain line short and large-bore to minimise back-pressure. Connecting the case drain back to the inlet reservoir at tank level is the most common arrangement. If the drain line creates pressure above 2.5 bar at the T port, premature shaft seal failure will result.

The P3 delivers a maximum of 6.0 L/min, achieved at 2000 rpm in the boosted PB configuration. At 1500 rpm the P3 produces approximately 4.95 L/min at low pressure, declining to around 3.5 L/min at 160 bar. The unboosted PC and PA versions are limited to 1800 rpm, giving a practical maximum of approximately 5.4 L/min.

Yes. Boosted pumps can rotate in either direction. The inlet and outlet pressure ports change according to shaft rotation direction, so pipework must be designed accordingly. For the unboosted versions, PC designates clockwise and PA designates anti-clockwise as viewed from the shaft end; these are not bi-directional.

The P3 reaches a peak overall efficiency of approximately 86% at 80 bar and 1500 rpm, a significant improvement over the P1 peak of around 77%. This is typical of axial piston pump scaling: larger displacement per revolution means volumetric losses represent a smaller fraction of total throughput. The P3 maintains efficiency above 80% from 40 bar through to 100 bar at 1500 rpm.
