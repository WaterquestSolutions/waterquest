WaterQuest P60

Why the P60

Maximum Fixed-Displacement Flow.
Highest Efficiency in the Range.

The P60 more than doubles the flow of the P30, reaching 146 L/min at 70cc and 1750 rpm, while simultaneously achieving peak overall efficiencies above 89% at 1500 rpm. At this displacement and body size, internal leakage as a proportion of total flow is small enough that the P60 maintains efficiency above 87% across a wide band from 60 to 140 bar at 1500 rpm, making it a genuinely high-efficiency machine for sustained industrial duty.

Engineering Detail

P60 Design and
Construction

Why Efficiency Peaks at Larger Displacement

At 70cc and 1500 rpm the P60 achieves 89% overall efficiency, significantly above the 87% peak on the P30 and the 85 to 86% peak on the compact range. This is a fundamental property of axial piston pump scaling: internal leakage, which is roughly proportional to clearance area and differential pressure, remains relatively constant regardless of displacement. As displacement increases, this fixed leakage becomes a smaller fraction of total output flow. The result is higher volumetric efficiency, and therefore higher overall efficiency, at larger displacement. The P60 is more efficient than any smaller model in the range at equivalent pressures.

T Port Step-Up to 1.5" BSPP (Unboosted)

The unboosted P60 T port is 1.5" BSPP, the largest port in the WaterQuest range. At flows up to 146 L/min the tank return line must be large enough to avoid significant back-pressure at the T port. A 1.5" pipe or larger is the minimum for the connecting return line; any reduction below 1.5" bore on the return pipework will create back-pressure that reduces volumetric efficiency and can cause seal and bearing issues. The pressure port on the unboosted PC/PA/PPC is 1" BSPP.

PPC Power Pack Variant

The PPC (Power Pack Clockwise) adds M10 x 15 deep mounting holes on a 155 mm PCD at 20° angular spacing, plus an O-ring face seal, to the standard clockwise unboosted body. This allows direct bolting onto a manifold plate or reservoir top plate, eliminating separate adaptor flanges. The 155 mm PCD and M10 thread are sized for the loads generated at P60 flow and pressure ratings. The PPC is available for the clockwise unboosted configuration only.

Shaft and Coupling Demands

The P60 shaft is 27.99/27.97 mm with an 8x7 mm key. At 42 kW maximum input power, motor and coupling selection must be checked carefully. The pump cannot accept any induced axial or radial shaft loading; all motor-to-pump connections must use a 3-part gear coupling unless shaft concentricity between motor output and pump input is verified within 0.05 mm, in which case inline direct drive is permitted. Motor frames range from 160L through 250M or above depending on displacement and operating pressure.

Filtration at P60 Flow Rates

All water entering the P60 must be pre-filtered to 10 micron nominal (25 micron absolute), beta-10 = 75 minimum. At the higher flow rates, the filter housing and element must be sized for the full pump output flow with an adequate service interval between element changes. Do not reduce filter bore below the supply pipe bore. On tank-fed systems, install filtration at the point water enters the tank, not between the tank and pump. A filter in the supply line at these flow rates creates a pressure drop risk that is difficult to manage reliably.

Temperature and Fluid Compatibility

Full rated performance from 2°C to 50°C. Above 50°C volumetric efficiency reduces; consult WaterQuest for the specific application temperature. Monopropylene Glycol antifreeze is available for sub-zero operation. The pump operates on glycol 95/5 mixtures with internal clearance adjustment. Noble material internal packages are required for seawater service. The exact operating fluid must be specified on all enquiries and orders; this is especially important at the P60 power level where any compatibility issue is expensive to address.

Frequently Asked Questions

P60 Pump
Technical FAQ

The unboosted P60 T port carries the full tank return flow, which can reach 146 L/min at maximum speed on the 70cc variant. A 1" BSPP port would create unacceptable back-pressure and line velocity on the return side at that flow rate. The 1.5" BSPP port is correctly sized to keep return line velocity and back-pressure within acceptable limits. The boosted PB version uses 1/2" BSPP on the T port because it only carries internal leakage flow, which is a small fraction of total output. Always size the connecting return pipework at least to match the 1.5" BSPP port bore; reducing bore below this on the return run will create back-pressure that affects performance and seal life.

Internal leakage in an axial piston pump is primarily a function of clearance geometry and differential pressure, and is roughly independent of displacement. As displacement increases, total output flow at any given speed increases, but the leakage remains approximately constant. This means leakage as a fraction of total flow decreases at larger displacements, which is why volumetric efficiency and overall efficiency improve as pump size increases. The P60 at 70cc and 1500 rpm reaches 89% peak efficiency, versus 87% on the P30 and 85 to 86% on the compact range. This has a direct benefit in system design: a larger pump at high efficiency can use a smaller motor than a combination of smaller pumps running at lower efficiency to produce the same flow.

The P60 body outer diameter is 200 mm, compared to 150 mm on the P30. The shaft grows from 23.993/23.980 mm to 27.99/27.97 mm; the key remains 8x7 mm. The mounting face diameter increases from the P30 to 181 mm on the P60. The port face steps up from 110 mm square on the P30 to 139 mm square on the P60. The overall body length grows from 156 mm to 199 mm. The flange diameter increases from 101.60/101.55 mm to 127.00/126.95 mm. All of these changes reflect the step up in power handling from 19.5 kW maximum on the P30 to 42 kW maximum on the P60. Bell housing and coupling specifications must be checked against the P60 geometry before reusing any P30 installation hardware.

At 160 bar and 70cc the P60 requires up to 42 kW, which typically corresponds to a 225M or 250 frame motor depending on the motor manufacturer and pole count. At 63cc and 160 bar the requirement is approximately 38 kW, and at 51cc approximately 28 kW. If the operating pressure is below 160 bar, smaller motor frames are appropriate across all three displacements. Contact WaterQuest for a specific motor frame recommendation that includes the bell housing and coupling specification for your chosen motor make and frame; at this power level the coupling and bell housing are engineered components, not standard items.

Yes. The PPC option is available on all three P60 displacements (51, 63, and 70 cc/rev) in the clockwise direction. The PPC adds M10 x 15 deep mounting holes on a 155 mm PCD at 20° angular intervals, plus an O-ring face seal on the port face. This allows the pump to bolt directly onto a manifold plate or reservoir top plate without separate flanges. The PPC is only available in the clockwise rotation direction; anti-clockwise power pack builds should use the PA variant with a separate adaptor plate. When specifying PPC, always confirm the manifold plate or reservoir top plate is drilled and tapped to the 155 mm PCD M10 pattern and is fitted with the correct O-ring groove for the face seal.
