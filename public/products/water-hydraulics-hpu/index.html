Why This Matters

Take hydraulic oil
out of the power circuit.

Less oil-handling work

Financial

Removing hydraulic oil from this circuit eliminates its oil top-ups, changes and waste-fluid handling. Compare those avoided tasks with the proposed water-management and service plan.

Remove an oil-release source

Compliance

Where a leak could reach a drain, waterway or sensitive process, changing the working fluid can remove a significant oil release source.

Make service easier to plan

Reputational

Give operators a clear maintenance plan and the information they need to manage the new circuit. Oil removal is most valuable when it supports dependable day-to-day operation.

Key Features

Built for Water.
Engineered for Performance.

316L Stainless Steel Construction

Stainless construction provides strength and corrosion resistance for suitable water service. Select the grade and seals for the actual water chemistry and temperature, including a specific review for saline duties.

Carbon-Reinforced PEEK Surfaces

Selected PEEK wear surfaces help manage the loaded contacts inside water-service components. Match the grade, opposing surface and filtration to the intended duty.

High-Efficiency Pumps and Motors

Select the pump and motor for the required flow, pressure and duty cycle. Available configurations extend up to 160 bar (2,321 psi); use the model-specific ratings in the selection table.

Accumulator Storage Options

An optional accumulator can supply a short flow demand or support a defined pressure function. Size it around the required volume and pressure range, and assess the complete control strategy when estimating energy use.

Standard and Custom Skid Layouts

Configurations range from compact modular packs built on the Pick a Pack system to full custom builds for specific site requirements and integration needs.

Water-Only Operation

Select the package for the specified working water and temperature range. Confirm water treatment, freeze protection, reservoir overflow and any required approval documentation.

The Case for Water HPUs

From operating problem to a working package.

1. Define the job

List the actuators, loads, travel times and movements that may happen together. Describe the oil-related problem or renewal need the project should address.

2. Match the supply

Use the movement schedule to select pump flow, operating pressure and drive size. Include standby, holding and any planned emergency movement.

3. Plan the water and service routine

Specify the working water, filtration, reservoir access and temperature protection. Compare the resulting routine with the oil tasks the new circuit removes.

4. Connect the installation

Coordinate mounting, pipework, electrical supply and control signals. Identify the documents and approval requirements for the supplied configuration.

5. Agree the handover

Define the commissioning checks, operator information and service arrangements before delivery. Test the package against the movement and recovery requirements you agreed.

WaterQuest Solutions water HPUs are available with 316 stainless steel reservoirs in four sizes: 5 L (1.32 US gal; S05, for P1 pumps), 25 L (6.60 US gal; S25, for P3 and P6), 55 L (14.5 US gal; S55, for P6 and P15), and 90 L (23.8 US gal; S90, for P15). The WaterQuest Solutions Pick a Pack system builds your ordering code in five steps. Step 1 selects the pump and motor (P1, P3, P6, or P15 at your required pressure). Step 2 chooses the reservoir volume (5–90 L / 1.32–23.8 US gal in 316 SS). Step 3 selects the filter and protection package (1T, 2L, 3B, 4B, or X). Step 4 selects the control valve configuration (C01 to C07). Step 5 adds accessories such as hand pump (HP), gauges (SG), heat exchanger (HE), level gauge (LG), fan cooler (FC), or additional tank return (T).
