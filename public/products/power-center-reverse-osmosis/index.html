Frequently Asked Questions

WaterQuest Solutions Power Center
Technical FAQ

The WaterQuest Solutions Power Center captures hydraulic energy from the high-pressure reject brine stream. In a conventional RO system this energy is wasted as the brine is simply depressurized. The Power Center transfers this energy back via an integrated axial piston energy recovery device that drives the booster pump function directly through a shaft coupling. No separate booster pump or motor is needed, and the recovered energy accounts for the dramatic reduction in total electricity consumption.

The WaterQuest Solutions Power Center is available in eight sizes covering production capacities from 3 cubic metres per day up to 1000 cubic metres per day from a single motor and pump assembly. For larger systems, multiple units can be run in parallel with automatic continuity if one unit goes offline. WaterQuest can assess your feedwater salinity, temperature, required recovery percentage, and flow targets to recommend the right size and configuration.

The WaterQuest Solutions Power Center is designed to be operational in hours, not the days required for conventional RO installations. The complete system connects with just four flexible hoses, a SAE flanged keyed shaft drive to the electric motor, and no high-pressure welded pipework. The integrated design removes the multiple sub-assemblies that extend conventional RO commissioning timelines.

The WaterQuest Solutions Power Center achieves service intervals of 8000 hours or more. Because the axial piston design is water-lubricated using the process fluid itself, there are no oil changes required at any interval. Parts are easily accessible for on-site servicing without specialist workshops or tools. The simplified design with fewer components also means fewer items to inspect and replace over the service life.
