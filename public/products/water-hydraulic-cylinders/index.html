Water hydraulic cylinders

What it does: Converts water pressure into linear push, pull and positioning force.

Where it’s used: Gates, valves and machine mechanisms with a defined load and stroke.

Why water: Remove hydraulic oil from the actuator circuit while retaining hydraulic force.

Materials

Material Choices for Water Service.

Cylinder Body

316 Stainless Steel

Standard 316 stainless steel construction provides the starting point for water-service material selection. Match the alloy to the water chemistry, temperature and exposure. Chloride-bearing water can still cause localized corrosion; stainless steel is not corrosion-proof.

Wear Surfaces

Engineering Polymer Seals

High-grade engineering polymer seals are part of the standard cylinder construction. Match the exact polymer and seal arrangement to the selected fluid, pressure, temperature and movement rather than treating seal selection as an afterthought.

Seal Package

Construction Matched to the Fluid

Tap-water and seawater duties require different construction decisions. The catalogue offers duplex stainless steel for seawater service and an optional ceramic coating for demanding duties. Review salinity, treatment chemicals and operating temperature before selection.

Actuator Configurations

Single and Double Acting.
Custom Mounting.

Single Acting

Unidirectional Force

Pressure produces the working stroke in one direction. A load, spring or other external force provides return motion, depending on the specified arrangement.

Select the return arrangement and its available force throughout the stroke. Confirm porting, venting and load behaviour for the particular single-acting design.

Double Acting

Bidirectional Control

Pressure can act on either side of the piston to produce extension and retraction. A single rod reduces the effective area on retraction, so push and pull forces differ at the same supply pressure.

Use the complete valve, feedback and load-control arrangement to achieve the required motion. A double-acting cylinder alone does not guarantee precise positioning or safe load holding.

What changes when the rod changes?

Illustrative calculation, not a rating for the cylinder on this page. Keep an 80 mm (3.15 in) bore and 100 bar (1,450 psi) supply pressure constant. Assume zero opposing pressure and omit friction for this comparison.

Quantity40 mm (1.57 in) rod50 mm (1.97 in) rod
Ideal extension force50.3 kN (11,300 lbf)50.3 kN (11,300 lbf)
Ideal retraction force37.7 kN (8,480 lbf)30.6 kN (6,890 lbf)
Retraction speed at 5 L/min (1.32 US gpm)22.1 mm/s (0.870 in/s)27.2 mm/s (1.07 in/s)

The larger rod reduces annular area: retraction force falls and speed rises at the same inlet flow. For the actual cylinder, include pressure at both ports, friction and the stated efficiency allowance, then check rod buckling, side load, stroke and mounting reactions.

Calculation basis

Bore area = πD²/4. Annular area = π(D² − d²)/4. These ideal results do not establish an allowable mechanical load or a safe operating speed.
