64 × 30 mm water hydraulic cylinder

What it does: Provides a 64 mm (2.52 in) bore and 30 mm (1.18 in) rod for linear actuation. At 100 bar (1,450 psi), calculated push is 31.5 kN and pull is 24.6 kN, before application-specific backpressure and mechanical checks.

Where it’s used: Compare this bore/rod combination against the load in both directions; stroke and mounting are selected for the mechanism.

Why water: Remove hydraulic oil from the actuator circuit while retaining hydraulic force.

Configuration: 64 mm (2.52 in) bore and 30 mm (1.18 in) rod; 32.17 cm² piston area and 25.10 cm² annular area. Calculation: F (kN) = pressure (bar) × effective area (cm²) × 0.98 ÷ 100. Areas and conversions use unrounded geometry. The 0.98 value is the manufacturer’s force-calculation factor, not a guaranteed overall energy efficiency. Figures assume zero return-side gauge pressure. For the actual load, assess opposing pressure on its own effective area, friction, buckling, mounting and load guidance. Calculated values do not replace a configuration-specific rating.

Calculation basis: manufacturer force factor; pressure–area formula. Confirm the selected cylinder construction and current operating limits before specifying.

What changes when the rod changes?

Illustrative calculation, not a rating for the cylinder on this page. Keep an 80 mm (3.15 in) bore and 100 bar (1,450 psi) supply pressure constant. Assume zero opposing pressure and omit friction for this comparison.

Quantity40 mm (1.57 in) rod50 mm (1.97 in) rod
Ideal extension force50.3 kN (11,300 lbf)50.3 kN (11,300 lbf)
Ideal retraction force37.7 kN (8,480 lbf)30.6 kN (6,890 lbf)
Retraction speed at 5 L/min (1.32 US gpm)22.1 mm/s (0.870 in/s)27.2 mm/s (1.07 in/s)

The larger rod reduces annular area: retraction force falls and speed rises at the same inlet flow. For the actual cylinder, include pressure at both ports, friction and the stated efficiency allowance, then check rod buckling, side load, stroke and mounting reactions.

Calculation basis

Bore area = πD²/4. Annular area = π(D² − d²)/4. These ideal results do not establish an allowable mechanical load or a safe operating speed.
