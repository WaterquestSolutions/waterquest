Project Overview

Oil removed from the actuation circuit

WaterQuest Solutions supplied a DN200 PN6 globe valve fitted with a water hydraulic actuator for use in a cruise ship bilge tank. The valve was designed for submerged service and selected as a direct replacement for an oil-actuated valve that had experienced actuator seal leakage.

The previous failure introduced hydraulic oil into the bilge. The vessel operator then had to drain the entire tank. The replacement system removes hydraulic oil from the actuation circuit and uses pressurised water to open the valve, with a spring-close fail-safe function.

ApplicationBilge, ballast, marine wastewater, drainage, and shipboard isolation duty.
Risk changeActuator leakage no longer introduces hydraulic oil into the tank.
Control methodHand-operated water hydraulic pump opens the valve locally.
Safe positionSpring force returns the valve to closed on pressure loss.
Bilge Tank Actuation Context

The operating environment is wet, restricted, and contamination-sensitive

Bilge tanks collect water, condensate, leakage, washdown water, and other shipboard drainage. Equipment installed in this space must tolerate moisture, immersion, corrosion exposure, restricted access, vibration, and periodic maintenance constraints.

Traditional oil hydraulic actuation in a bilge environment creates a direct contamination risk. A failed actuator seal can release hydraulic oil into a confined tank that may need to be pumped, cleaned, isolated, or fully drained before service can continue.

Engineering Objective

Remove oil from the actuation system without sacrificing force, reliability, or control.WaterQuest design intent

Operating Principle

Hand pump to open. Spring to close.

The actuator is vertically mounted above the bonnet and drives the valve stem axially. Pressurised water opens the valve. Spring force closes the valve when water pressure is removed or lost.

Hand pump pressurises water
Water enters G1/2 actuator port
Piston force compresses spring
Stem lifts sealing disk
DN200 flow path opens
Opening sequence
  1. The operator uses the hand pump to pressurise the actuator with water.
  2. Pressurised water enters the actuator through the G1/2 port.
  3. Hydraulic force acts on the piston area.
  4. The piston compresses the closing spring.
  5. The stem lifts the sealing disk from the seat.
  6. Flow through the DN200 valve body is permitted.
Closing sequence
  1. Water pressure is released or lost.
  2. The compressed spring extends.
  3. The actuator drives the stem and disk downward.
  4. The valve returns to the closed position.
  5. The system fails closed without hydraulic oil, compressed air, or electric motor torque at the valve.
Engineering Rationale

Same hydraulic physics. Different consequence profile.

Water is an effective hydraulic medium when the system is designed around its physical properties. The force relationship remains the same as oil hydraulics:

Force = Pressure × AreaActuator sizing principle

For valve actuation, water can generate the required linear actuator force through the same hydraulic principle used in conventional oil systems. The major engineering difference is consequence.

If an oil actuator leaks into a bilge, the result can be contamination, clean-up labour, reporting exposure, vessel delay, and possible damage to downstream treatment or discharge systems. If a water actuator leaks, the actuation medium is compatible with the bilge environment.

Where the value is strongest
  • Equipment located near water or inside containment spaces.
  • Vessel areas where leak detection and clean-up are expensive.
  • Bilge, ballast, wastewater, and shipboard drainage systems.
  • Applications where hydraulic oil release creates compliance exposure.
  • Marine machinery spaces with restricted inspection access.

WaterQuest material philosophy

For WaterQuest systems, the preferred engineering direction is to keep water-contacting and sliding interfaces in corrosion-resistant materials. Where sliding contact is present in WaterQuest water hydraulic components, the preferred contact pairing is stainless steel against carbon-reinforced PEEK. This prevents galling and avoids reliance on oil lubricity.

For shipboard service, water hydraulic components cannot be treated as oil components with the fluid changed. The system must be designed for water from the start, with correct metallurgy, polymer selection, sealing design, surface finish, and contamination control.

Fail-Safe Function

Spring-close architecture gives a predictable default state

The spring-close architecture is a major engineering advantage for bilge tank isolation. In a shipboard installation, the actuator may be exposed to immersion, limited access, long idle periods, and emergency service conditions.

A spring-return actuator gives the operator a predictable default state. For isolation duty, that default state is closed.

Maintenance and Reliability Advantages

Seal leakage no longer means oil contamination

The primary maintenance gain is removal of hydraulic oil from the actuator circuit. The same class of leakage event carries a far lower consequence when the actuation medium is water.

Oil actuation failure
Failure ModeResult
Actuator rod seal leakageOil enters bilge tank
Hose or fitting leakageOil contamination in confined marine space
Oil clean-up requirementLabour, delay, disposal, documentation
Potential discharge issueEnvironmental compliance risk
Seal failure during idle serviceHidden contamination until inspection
Recommended inspection points
  • Confirm hand pump pressure output and holding capability.
  • Inspect actuator rod and stem travel.
  • Check spring-return closure speed and seating force.
  • Inspect flange connections and bolting.
  • Confirm valve seat leakage acceptance for the vessel system.
  • Inspect actuator seals during scheduled maintenance.
  • Flush the actuation circuit with clean water after service exposure.
  • Confirm there is no binding through the full open-close stroke.
Engineering Value to Ship Operators

Risk reduction without loss of hydraulic function

The vessel still receives high-force valve actuation. The contamination profile changes completely.

Operational value

The valve can be manually opened by water hydraulic pressure and returned closed by spring force. This gives crews a dependable local operating method for service spaces where electrical actuation may be less desirable or emergency manual control is required.

Environmental value

Bilge systems are highly sensitive to oil contamination. Replacing oil actuation with water hydraulic actuation reduces the chance that actuator failure creates an oil-handling event.

Maintenance value

Water hydraulic actuation reduces oil handling, oil top-up, oil disposal, oil clean-up, and post-leak remediation work. Maintenance work shifts to normal seal, spring, stem, and pressure checks.

Safety value

A spring-close actuator gives the system a defined safe position. For isolation duty, this is a strong marine design feature.

Procurement value

This valve is not theoretical. It was designed for a cruise ship bilge tank, intended for submerged service, and selected to replace an oil-actuated valve after seal leakage caused bilge contamination.

Fleet value

The same design logic can be applied across bilge, ballast, drainage, wastewater, dockside, and other marine systems where hydraulic oil creates avoidable operational exposure.
