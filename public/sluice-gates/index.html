Sluice Gates

A sluice gate can sit motionless for months. When the water rises, failure isn't an option.

Reliable gate operation isn't simply a question of cylinder force. Water levels, gate friction, mounting geometry, operating speed and safe load holding must all work together.

How gate data becomes actuator sizing

Engineering Basis

Design starts with gate load, not actuator product range size.

A properly sized WaterQuest sluice-gate package begins with the civil and hydraulic load case: gate width and height, differential head, seal drag, guide friction, debris loading, corrosion allowance, silt, ice, required travel speed, and fail-position requirement. Those inputs determine cylinder bore, rod size, stroke, pump flow, relief setting, controls, and tubing size.

For retrofit work, WaterQuest also reviews the existing gate frame, guide wear, mounting structure, access envelope, electrical service, PLC interface, and maintenance isolation requirements. This avoids the common mistake of installing a strong actuator on a gate that binds mechanically.

Core deliverables

Water hydraulic power unit: pump, motor, clean-water reservoir, filtration, relief protection, gauges, sensors, drains, enclosure, and optional heat or freeze protection.

Gate actuator: stainless cylinder or rotary water hydraulic drive selected for gate force, stroke, mounting geometry, travel speed, holding duty, and service access.

Fluid path: 316 stainless tube, hose, fittings, manifolds, isolation valves, low-leak connections, flushing points, and drain routing.

Controls and documentation: local controls, remote I/O, limit switches, position feedback, interlocks, FAT/SAT records, drawings, O&M manual, and recommended spare parts.

Oil vs Water Risk

The biggest advantage is what happens when something goes wrong.

Water hydraulic actuation changes the fluid that could escape at the gate. Keep inspection and maintenance focused on water quality, seals, tubing and mechanical condition so the system continues to deliver the required movement.

Materials and Water Quality

Water-compatible materials are the system, not an accessory.

Water has different lubricity, corrosion behaviour, and biological risk than oil. Where two sliding or rotating surfaces contact, one is selected as 316 stainless and the other as carbon-reinforced PEEK where applicable.

316 SS

Used for fittings, manifolds, tubes, wetted hardware, and exposed components where corrosion resistance is essential.

PEEK/PTFE

Used in compatible bearing, guide, seal, and sliding interfaces depending on load and motion.

10 µm (394 µin)

Fine filtration target protects valves, pumps, actuators, and control orifices from abrasive solids.

MTR

Material certification, drawings, test records, and spare parts lists can be included for project documentation.
