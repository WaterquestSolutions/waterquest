Mining Operations

An inexpensive hydraulic leak can become a very expensive mining problem.

Lost production, contaminated water, cleanup obligations and difficult equipment access can turn a small failure into a major operating expense. Mining equipment still needs force, movement and dependable control. Evaluate where water hydraulic technology may help manage oil-related risks without compromising the underlying engineering requirements.

The customer benefit

Tackle oil exposure and dust at their source.

A leaking actuator beside a wet process creates a different problem from dust escaping a conveyor transfer. Water can serve both duties, but each needs the right equipment. Use a conditioned water hydraulic circuit to move a gate or clamp without hydraulic oil. Use a separately designed pumping and nozzle system to deliver water where dust control needs it. This gives each system a clear job and makes performance easier to measure and maintain.

How water does the work

See how water does the work.

In an actuator circuit, water under pressure pushes a piston to move the mechanism; valves control direction and speed. In a spray circuit, the pump supplies the flow and pressure needed at the nozzles. WaterQuest helps select pumps and controls around either duty, with filtration and materials chosen for the working water rather than assuming raw mine water is suitable.

01

Capture depends on contact, not pressure alone.

Droplet size, droplet velocity, nozzle location and air movement influence whether spray contacts dust. Fine droplets can follow airflow or evaporate before reaching the target; coarser droplets may wet bulk material more effectively. Select the spray for source wetting or airborne capture and validate it under operating conditions.

02

Abrasive mine water is not automatically hydraulic fluid.

Solids can damage close-clearance pumps and control valves. Specify treatment and filtration for each circuit and monitor inlet conditions. For cylinders and clamps, protect rods and seals from external grit and arrange hydraulic lines so material handling cannot strike them.

Practical applications

Practical water-powered duties around the mine.

Use these examples to connect an operating problem with a possible water-based arrangement. Bring the closest match to your enquiry, along with the site conditions that will shape the design.

01

Conveyor transfer dust

The customer’s needFalling ore entrains air and creates a dust source at a conveyor transfer.

The water-based approachEnclose the transfer where practical and position sprays for the material and airflow. Enable water only with material movement and provide a drainable, serviceable manifold outside the falling stream.

Confirm the resultCompare exposure measurements at representative production rates, together with water use, material moisture and nozzle fouling.

02

Chute-gate positioning

The customer’s needA chute gate needs repeatable positioning near a wet or environmentally sensitive process.

The water-based approachSelect a purpose-designed water hydraulic actuator against the breakaway force of a loaded gate. Protect the cylinder from abrasion and define how a blockage is isolated before intervention.

Confirm the resultDemonstrate loaded opening and closing, position feedback and pressure-limited fault response without relying on an operator to clear a live blockage.

03

Washdown-zone fixture

The customer’s needA maintenance fixture works in a regularly washed area where oil leakage would complicate housekeeping.

The water-based approachUse a separate conditioned-water actuation loop, accessible isolation and a mechanical support for maintenance. Confirm that washing chemicals are compatible with external seals and finishes.

Confirm the resultCheck holding drift, hose protection and isolation with pressure removed; document water quality and filter condition during the trial.

An illustrative calculation

Make water use visible

Eight illustrative nozzles at 0.6 L/min (0.159 US gpm) use 4.8 L/min (1.27 US gpm) while enabled. At a 40% duty cycle over an eight-hour shift, they use about 922 L (244 US gal). The same manifold running continuously uses 2,304 L (609 US gal).

Water volume = total nozzle flow × enabled time

Take the calculation into equipment selection.

This compares operating time only. It does not predict dust reduction; nozzle flow must come from the selected nozzle at its operating pressure.

Build the commercial case

Make the oil-free option a practical investment.

Select a duty with a clear production problem: actuator leakage, unreliable gate travel or poorly controlled spray delivery. Compare operating performance, maintenance, fluid handling and downtime at representative production rates.

  1. Define the improvement. Record the present duty, service effort and operating problem. Agree the movement, output or availability the new equipment must deliver.
  2. Specify and demonstrate the package. Match components to the actual water, load and operating range. Use agreed commissioning checks or a focused trial to confirm the result.
  3. Compare the complete ownership cost. Include equipment, installation, utilities, fluid management, servicing and downtime. Use measured results where available and identify estimates clearly.

Questions customers ask

Make an informed equipment choice.

Can water hydraulics provide the force this equipment needs?

Yes, when the equipment is sized for the duty. Cylinder force comes from pressure acting over the effective piston area; controlled flow sets movement speed. WaterQuest helps match the cylinder, pump and valves to the load, stroke and operating sequence, using components designed for water.

Is more pressure always better for dust control?

No. It changes the spray pattern, droplet behaviour and flow. Choose the operating point that works with the enclosure, airflow and material, then measure the result.

Can mine water supply the cylinder circuit?

Not without a water-quality assessment and component acceptance. The dust-spray supply and hydraulic working fluid may need different treatment.
