The customer benefit

Keep hydraulic oil out of the selected circuit near production.

Mills already manage large volumes of water, yet some mechanisms still depend on oil-filled hydraulic circuits beside the process. A purpose-designed water hydraulic package removes that oil source while retaining controlled force and motion. Start with a beam, valve or fixture where leakage has a clear consequence. Match the design to the positioning tolerance, load, process exposure and maintenance window the mill can support.

How water does the work

See how water does the work.

Pressure in a conditioned-water circuit creates actuator force; controlled flow determines speed. Valve selection, feedback and the mechanism’s stiffness govern positioning and holding. The working-water loop is separate from stock, showers and whitewater, allowing the hydraulic components to operate with a defined water quality.

01

Position, force and compliance matter together.

A beam-positioning mechanism may need controlled contact force rather than simply a repeatable end position. Linkage stiffness, friction, alignment and control response affect the load applied to the process. Establish the permitted force and movement with the machine supplier before selecting actuator pressure.

02

White water is not a universal hydraulic supply.

Recovered process water may contain fibres, fillers, chemicals and biological activity. Those properties can be unsuitable for close-clearance components. A segregated conditioned-water circuit can be managed to a defined specification while external surfaces are selected for the mill’s heat and chemical exposure.

Practical applications

Water hydraulic motion around the mill.

Use these examples to connect an operating problem with a possible water-based arrangement. Bring the closest match to your enquiry, along with the site conditions that will shape the design.

01

Doctor-beam positioning

The customer’s needA guarded beam needs controlled engagement and withdrawal near the wet end.

The water-based approachSelect an actuator and control arrangement with the machine supplier, accounting for beam alignment, contact force and a safe withdrawn state where appropriate.

Confirm the resultMeasure force and position through operating changes. Confirm repeatable withdrawal and the approved response to a control or pressure fault.

02

Stock-preparation valve

The customer’s needA large valve operates in an abrasive or chemically active area.

The water-based approachSelect the water hydraulic actuator for the valve torque, while treating process compatibility as a separate valve-body issue. Protect rods and sensors and provide local isolation outside the main splash zone.

Confirm the resultVerify travel and seating under representative conditions. Record pressure trends that could reveal fouling or changing valve friction.

03

Roll-handling fixture

The customer’s needA stationary fixture requires controlled positioning beside paper products.

The water-based approachUse a dedicated water hydraulic circuit to remove oil from the fixture’s actuation duty. Retain an independently engineered holding and restraint arrangement for any supported load.

Confirm the resultProve load control, position repeatability and maintenance restraint. Review whether service access fits the mill’s scheduled shutdown windows.

An illustrative calculation

Avoid paying for unnecessary throttling

An illustrative circuit passing 10 L/min (2.64 US gpm) across a 50 bar (725 psi) pressure drop dissipates about 0.83 kW (1.11 hp). If that loss persists for 6,000 hours, it represents about 5,000 kWh (17,060,708 Btu) of hydraulic energy annually.

Hydraulic power (kW) = pressure (bar) × flow (L/min) ÷ 600

This is a loss calculation, not a guaranteed saving. A complete comparison includes pump, motor, control, cooling and standby performance.

Build the commercial case

Make the oil-free option a practical investment.

Choose a mechanism where oil leakage affects product quality or production availability. Compare movement, holding stability, fluid management and maintenance with the present system, and include the cost of affected product and stoppages where records support it.

  1. Define the improvement. Record the present duty, service effort and operating problem. Agree the movement, output or availability the new equipment must deliver.
  2. Specify and demonstrate the package. Match components to the actual water, load and operating range. Use agreed commissioning checks or a focused trial to confirm the result.
  3. Compare the complete ownership cost. Include equipment, installation, utilities, fluid management, servicing and downtime. Use measured results where available and identify estimates clearly.

Questions customers ask

Make an informed equipment choice.

Can water hydraulics provide the force this equipment needs?

Yes, when the equipment is sized for the duty. Cylinder force comes from pressure acting over the effective piston area; controlled flow sets movement speed. WaterQuest helps match the cylinder, pump and valves to the load, stroke and operating sequence, using components designed for water.

Can white water feed the hydraulic circuit?

Only after treatment and explicit component acceptance. Its fibres, fillers and chemistry often make a separate conditioned loop the clearer design boundary.

Will the change reduce energy use?

That depends on the full operating profile and control architecture. Measure useful work, throttling, leakage and standby consumption before claiming a saving.
