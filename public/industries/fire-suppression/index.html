Suppression Science

How Water Atomization
Disrupts the Fire Triangle.

High-pressure water mist systems deliver superior fire suppression by atomizing water into micron-scale droplets that simultaneously attack all three elements of combustion.

The Fire Triangle and Water's Multi-Point Attack

Every fire requires three elements to sustain combustion: heat, oxygen, and fuel. Water mist systems engineered with specialized nozzles disrupt all three simultaneously through precise droplet control.

🔥

Heat Removal

Evaporative cooling absorbs latent heat at 2,257 kJ/kg. Smaller droplets maximize surface area per unit volume, accelerating heat transfer and reducing flame temperature below ignition thresholds.

💨

Oxygen Displacement

Steam expansion at 1,600:1 volume ratio displaces combustion air. Fine mist creates a vapour barrier that blocks oxygen diffusion to the fuel surface, starving the reaction zone.

🛢️

Fuel Surface Cooling

Wetting action cools fuel surfaces below pyrolysis temperature. Controlled droplet size ensures penetration without excessive runoff, preventing re-ignition of solid fuels and smothering liquid fuel fires.

Droplet Size Engineering: The Critical Parameter

Fire suppression effectiveness is directly controlled by water droplet diameter. Specialized nozzles atomize water to precise size ranges, each optimized for different suppression mechanisms and fire classifications.

Droplet Class Diameter Range Primary Mechanism Typical Application
Ultra-Fine Mist 10–50 µm Rapid evaporative cooling, oxygen displacement, complete vaporization in flame zone Enclosed machinery spaces, electrical rooms, server facilities
Fine Mist 50–200 µm Balanced cooling and oxygen blocking, minimal water damage, extended hang time Gas turbine enclosures, offshore modules, industrial kitchens
Medium Droplet 200–400 µm Direct fuel cooling, surface wetting, moderate penetration through fire plume Warehouse rack storage, manufacturing floors, vehicle bays
Spray Droplet 400–1000 µm High momentum fuel wetting, deep-seated fire penetration, Class A combustibles Timber yards, paper mills, solid fuel storage areas

Atomization Physics: Pressure, Orifice, and Flow Regime

High-pressure water mist nozzles operate at 50–200 bar (725–2,900 psi) to achieve controlled atomization. Nozzle geometry and operating pressure determine droplet size distribution through hydraulic shear and turbulent breakup mechanisms.

Nozzle Operating Parameters

  • Operating Pressure: 50–200 bar
  • Orifice Diameter: 0.3–2.0 mm
  • Flow Rate (typical): 10–100 L/min
  • Spray Cone Angle: 60°–120°
  • Droplet Velocity: 20–80 m/s

Suppression Performance Metrics

  • Surface Area per Litre: 60–600 m²
  • Evaporation Rate: 70–95%
  • Heat Absorption: 2.26 MJ/kg
  • Steam Expansion: 1,600:1
  • Knockdown Time: 30–120 sec
