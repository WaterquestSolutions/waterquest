Compare water, oil and air for hydraulic force, control and service requirements.

Three working media compared for your industry
What mattersWater hydraulicsWorking medium: waterOil hydraulicsWorking medium: hydraulic oilPneumaticsWorking medium: compressed air
Remove hydraulic oil at the sourceWater’s advantage for this application.Keep hydraulic force and controlled movement while removing hydraulic oil from the water hydraulic circuit near your product, process or waterway.Provides hydraulic force with an oil inventory that needs fluid management and leak control.Useful for suitable automation and valve duties where the available air supply meets the force and control requirements.
Working mediumWater carries the power.Water in components designed for its chemistry, lubrication and sealing requirements.Hydraulic oil selected for the pump, valves, seals and operating temperature.Compressed air supplied through suitable filtration, moisture removal and pressure regulation.
Force and motionKeep hydraulic force and control.Low liquid compressibility supports controlled movement. Pressure and actuator area determine force; flow sets speed.Also provides hydraulic force and controlled motion through pressure, flow and suitable valves.Compressible air gives a more compliant response. Precision movement is possible with suitable feedback and control.
Leaks and contaminationNo hydraulic-oil film from this circuit.Removing hydraulic oil eliminates that source of oily residue. Water leaks still need detection, drainage and repair.A leak can leave oily residue and require containment, cleaning or contaminated-material handling.Escaping air wastes supply energy. Oil carryover depends on compressor design, air treatment and actuator lubrication.
Fire behaviourThe water working medium does not burn.Remove combustible hydraulic oil from the selected circuit, an advantage around heat or fire-sensitive processes.Mineral hydraulic oil is combustible. Fire-resistant fluids have different properties and compatibility requirements.Air does not burn, but it contains oxygen. Air-powered equipment still needs assessment for its operating environment.
Routine maintenanceRemove hydraulic-oil servicing from this circuit.Maintain water quality, filters, seals and wetted materials; provide freeze protection where needed.Maintain oil condition, filters and seals; manage oil changes, storage and used-fluid handling.Maintain compressors, filters, dryers and condensate drains; inspect distribution piping and repair air leaks.
Selecting the equipmentChoose a complete water-compatible system.Match water-service pumps, valves and actuators to the load and water quality, with corrosion control and filtration designed together.Match pumps, valves, actuators and seals to the oil, temperature and duty; include leak containment where required.Match compressor capacity, air preparation and actuators to force, cycling, positioning and the response to lost air supply.

Size process-water pumping and actuation separately for water quality, flow and pressure.
