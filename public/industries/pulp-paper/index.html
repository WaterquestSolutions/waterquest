The Problem with Oil in Pulp Mills

Oil Hydraulics Create Problems That Water Hydraulics Eliminate

Pulp and paper production involves large volumes of water, fibre slurry, and chemicals that create harsh operating conditions for hydraulic equipment.

Traditional oil hydraulic systems frequently encounter problems in these environments, and the consequences go beyond mechanical failure.

⚠️

Hydraulic oil contamination of fibre streams or process water, triggering quality failures and regulatory reporting

⚠️

Oil leaks creating slip hazards on mill floors and contamination in washdown water

⚠️

Chemical degradation of seals and hydraulic components from bleaching agents and process chemicals

⚠️

Maintenance downtime caused by filtration replacement, oil analysis, and fluid disposal logistics

⚠️

Accumulation of oil residue in washdown areas and effluent treatment systems

What Water Hydraulics Change

If a leak occurs, only water is released. No hydrocarbon contamination of fibre streams, process water, or mill effluent.

316 stainless steel and PEEK materials resist the bleaching chemicals, acids, and alkalis used throughout pulp processing.

No oil analysis programme, no filtration replacement schedule, no hazardous fluid disposal contract.

Systems designed for constant washdown environments with high humidity and fibre slurry exposure.

Full hydraulic force capability at up to 160 bar, equivalent to oil-based systems without the contamination risk.

Why Water Wins in Pulp Mills

Six Advantages of Water Hydraulics in Pulp and Paper Production

🚫

No Oil Contamination

Water hydraulic systems remove petroleum fluids from production environments entirely. Every hydraulic leak event releases water, not hydrocarbons. The contamination pathway into fibre processing and mill wastewater systems is closed by design.

💧

Built for Wet Industrial Environments

Pulp mills operate with constant washdown and high ambient humidity. Water hydraulic systems are engineered specifically for environments where oil systems degrade. Stainless steel and PEEK do not corrode, swell, or fail from moisture exposure.

🧪

Resistance to Bleaching Chemicals

Bleaching chemicals, acids, and alkaline solutions used throughout pulp processing degrade conventional hydraulic seals and components. 316 stainless steel and engineered PEEK polymers provide strong resistance to the chemicals common in kraft and sulphite processes.

⚙️

Reliable Motion Control for Heavy Equipment

Gates, conveyors, presses, and process valves throughout a pulp mill require precise, repeatable actuation at continuous duty cycles. Water hydraulic cylinders and motors operating at up to 160 bar deliver the same force capability as oil-based equivalents.

🔧

Reduced Maintenance Requirements

Water hydraulic systems eliminate oil filtration equipment, fluid replacement schedules, oil analysis programmes, and hazardous fluid disposal requirements. The maintenance overhead of managing a petroleum hydraulic fluid inventory across a large mill is removed entirely.

🦺

Improved Workplace Safety

Removing hydraulic oil reduces slip hazards on mill floors, eliminates fire risk from pressurized oil mist near hot surfaces, and removes the chemical handling requirements associated with petroleum fluids. A safer mill is also a more productive one.

Self-lubricating bearing and seal material that performs without oil lubrication. Chemically inert to the acids, alkalis, and process chemicals used in kraft and sulphite pulp processing.

Polymer Sealing Components

PTFE and ultra-high molecular weight polyethylene for sealing surfaces requiring extreme chemical inertness or very low static friction. Compatible with all chemicals encountered in pulp and paper production.
