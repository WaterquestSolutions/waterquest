The customer benefit

Remove an oil source where timber is handled.

A leaking hydraulic circuit can spread oil onto timber, work areas or nearby water. For a suitable handling duty, water hydraulics removes that oil inventory while retaining the force needed to grip, position or divert material. A sheltered mill station often provides a practical starting point: the load is well defined, service access is available and operating conditions can be controlled. Mobile equipment needs its own assessment of shock loads, temperature and available support.

How water does the work

See how water does the work.

A water hydraulic cylinder produces force from pressure on its piston. The linkage turns that force into grip or gate movement, and control valves manage direction and speed. Purpose-designed water components, protected rods and filtration support reliable operation around bark and grit. Freezing protection is planned for the complete circuit, including shutdown and storage.

01

Clamping force comes through the linkage.

Cylinder force is transformed by the clamp geometry, and its useful component changes through the travel. Timber diameter, friction, impact and asymmetric loading affect secure handling. Size the mechanism to retain the load without excessive pressure or damage to the material, and assess what happens when supply pressure is lost.

02

Debris and freezing shape reliability.

Bark, grit and fibres can damage exposed rods or enter poorly protected systems. Water can freeze in low points and trapped cavities. Rod protection, filtration, service access and a documented cold-weather strategy need to be designed together rather than added after installation.

Practical applications

Start with the timber-handling job that fits.

Use these examples to connect an operating problem with a possible water-based arrangement. Bring the closest match to your enquiry, along with the site conditions that will shape the design.

01

Sawmill log clamp

The customer’s needA stationary handling station needs repeatable grip beside timber and washdown areas.

The water-based approachSelect a guarded water hydraulic cylinder and clamp linkage with accessible hoses and suitable rod protection. Define force limits and a holding arrangement for the actual log-size range.

Confirm the resultTest grip, repeatability and controlled release across representative timber sizes, including the documented loss-of-pressure case.

02

Mill sorting gate

The customer’s needA gate diverts material without needing the full hydraulic capability of a mobile machine.

The water-based approachUse a dedicated water hydraulic actuator with position confirmation and a protected supply. Coordinate movement with material tracking and inhibit motion when an obstruction is detected.

Confirm the resultVerify cycle time, position accuracy and jam recovery; inspect seals and guards after representative debris exposure.

03

Waterside timber-handling fixture

The customer’s needA shore-side fixture operates where leaked oil could enter receiving water.

The water-based approachSpecify a purpose-designed water circuit for the fixture while keeping the lifting and structural safety assessment independent. Protect services against abrasion and environmental exposure.

Confirm the resultDemonstrate loaded holding and recovery, review external corrosion and quantify maintenance effort before expanding the application.

An illustrative calculation

Speed requires flow

An illustrative 50 mm (1.97 in) bore cylinder has an area of about 1,963 mm² (3.04 in²). A 300 mm (11.8 in) extension in two seconds requires about 17.7 L/min (4.68 US gpm) before leakage allowance.

Flow = piston area × stroke ÷ travel time

Take the calculation into equipment selection.

Retraction differs because the rod reduces the effective area. Check simultaneous demands, acceleration and the clamp’s load before selecting the pump.

Build the commercial case

Make the oil-free option a practical investment.

Use a defined mill duty to compare grip, cycle time, servicing and oil-handling work. Include protection against debris and freezing in the installed cost, then use the operating results to identify the next suitable mechanism.

  1. Define the improvement. Record the present duty, service effort and operating problem. Agree the movement, output or availability the new equipment must deliver.
  2. Specify and demonstrate the package. Match components to the actual water, load and operating range. Use agreed commissioning checks or a focused trial to confirm the result.
  3. Compare the complete ownership cost. Include equipment, installation, utilities, fluid management, servicing and downtime. Use measured results where available and identify estimates clearly.

Questions customers ask

Make an informed equipment choice.

Can water hydraulics provide the force this equipment needs?

Yes, when the equipment is sized for the duty. Cylinder force comes from pressure acting over the effective piston area; controlled flow sets movement speed. WaterQuest helps match the cylinder, pump and valves to the load, stroke and operating sequence, using components designed for water.

Where is a sensible first trial?

A protected stationary mechanism with a clear load envelope and convenient maintenance access usually provides a more manageable test boundary than a whole mobile machine.

Can antifreeze simply be added?

Only with the component supplier’s acceptance for the exact fluid mixture. Additives can change lubrication, viscosity, seal behaviour and environmental characteristics.
