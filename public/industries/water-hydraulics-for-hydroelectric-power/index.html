Hydroelectric Power

Millions of litres of water. One leaking hydraulic actuator. Is the risk worth accepting?

At hydroelectric facilities, gate actuation must overcome substantial loads, maintain controlled movement and remain dependable through long service intervals. Using water as the hydraulic medium removes the petroleum-based fluid from the actuation circuit, without removing the need for precise force calculations, secure load holding and proper control.

The customer benefit

An oil-free working circuit belongs beside the reservoir.

A gate actuator works where a hydraulic-oil release can reach the intake, tailrace or reservoir. Water hydraulics removes that oil inventory from the selected circuit while retaining hydraulic force and controllable movement. For a new gate or refurbishment, it is an opportunity to address leakage exposure, worn mechanisms and difficult service access together. Start with the gate load and operating sequence, then build the power package around them.

How water does the work

See how water does the work.

The pump supplies pressurised water to the gate cylinder through control valves. A vertical cylinder connected to the gate converts that pressure into lifting force; controlled flow sets how quickly the gate travels. Holding and emergency functions are designed into the valve, control and mechanical arrangement. The actuator’s conditioned-water loop remains separate from the reservoir water.

01

Static head is only the beginning.

Gate force depends on differential head, pressure distribution, seals, friction and gate geometry. Flow-induced loading and vibration can matter during partially open operation. The actuator and structure must be checked across the full operating envelope, not only at fully open and fully closed positions.

02

Closure time interacts with the waterway.

Changing flow velocity changes pressure in a confined water column. Penstock length, wave speed, surge provision and the closure law influence the transient. A rapid actuator is not automatically a safer actuator. Derive the permitted motion from the hydraulic transient study and verify it at commissioning.

Practical applications

Water hydraulic power for intake, spillway and isolation duties.

Use these examples to connect an operating problem with a possible water-based arrangement. Bring the closest match to your enquiry, along with the site conditions that will shape the design.

01

Intake isolation gate

The customer’s needThe plant needs a gate that can isolate a waterway for a defined emergency or maintenance case.

The water-based approachSize the cylinder and its mountings for the full load envelope, with a controlled closure profile and a documented reserve-energy strategy where required. Provide a means of proving isolation before entry.

Confirm the resultRecord position and pressure during agreed tests. Demonstrate emergency movement under the approved test conditions and verify isolation procedures.

02

Spillway gate operation

The customer’s needA gate must regulate reservoir release over changing head and weather conditions.

The water-based approachUse feedback-controlled motion with redundant information where the risk assessment requires it. Assess unequal loading and synchronisation for multi-actuator gates.

Confirm the resultTest position agreement, holding drift and communications failure. Confirm the response to a failed sensor without causing an unintended release.

03

Auxiliary valve actuation

The customer’s needA cooling or service-water valve requires reliable movement near turbine equipment.

The water-based approachUse a dedicated water hydraulic package for this auxiliary duty, keeping it distinct from a turbine governor retrofit. Match the valve’s torque, speed and safe position to the plant sequence.

Confirm the resultProve local and remote operation, permissives and restoration after power loss. Record the valve’s actual travel time under representative pressure.

An illustrative calculation

Pressure surges deserve their own calculation

For an illustrative rapid velocity change of 0.5 m/s (1.64 ft/s) in water with density 1,000 kg/m³ (62.4 lb/ft³) and a pressure-wave speed of 1,000 m/s (3,281 ft/s), the Joukowsky estimate is 500 kPa (72.5 psi), or 5 bar (72.5 psi).

Δp = ρ × a × Δv

Take the calculation into equipment selection.

This simplified rapid-transient estimate is not a penstock design calculation. Actual closure timing, reflections, air and surge devices require a system study.

Questions customers ask

Make an informed equipment choice.

Can water hydraulics provide the force this equipment needs?

Yes, when the equipment is sized for the duty. Cylinder force comes from pressure acting over the effective piston area; controlled flow sets movement speed. WaterQuest helps match the cylinder, pump and valves to the load, stroke and operating sequence, using components designed for water.

Can water hydraulics replace an existing turbine governor?

That is a separate control-system engineering project. This page describes candidate gate and auxiliary duties; dynamic response and protection need application-specific evidence.

Can an accumulator provide emergency closure?

It can form part of a design, but available volume, pressure, leakage, monitoring and the complete required stroke must be calculated and proven.
