From liquid droplets to water vapour

Atomisation increases surface area. Evaporation creates expansion.

A nozzle divides liquid water into fine droplets. This exposes more surface for heat transfer. When those droplets absorb enough heat to become vapour, the same mass of water occupies a much larger volume. The roughly 1,600× comparison describes the liquid-to-vapour change, not atomisation alone.

The comparison behind the number

At about 100°C (212°F) and 1.014 bar absolute (14.7 psia), saturated water vapour occupies roughly 1,600 times the volume of the same mass of saturated liquid water. This is a volume ratio; the water mass stays the same.

Why this matters to fire suppression

Evaporation absorbs heat. Vapour can also dilute oxygen locally, depending on ventilation and enclosure conditions. Actual vapour volume depends on temperature, pressure and how much water evaporates.

Suppression Science

How water mist acts on the fire triangle.

Water mist breaks water into small droplets that absorb heat. Depending on the fire and enclosure, evaporation can also reduce oxygen concentration locally, while droplets reaching fuel surfaces can cool them.

Heat, Oxygen and Fuel: Where Water Acts

Water can cool flames and fuel surfaces, while water vapour can dilute oxygen in the fire zone. The contribution of each mechanism depends on the hazard, spray delivery, and ventilation.

🔥

Heat Removal

Water absorbs heat as it warms and evaporates. Smaller droplets provide more surface area for the same water volume, helping heat transfer when the spray reaches hot gases and flames.

💨

Oxygen Displacement

As droplets evaporate, water vapour can reduce oxygen concentration locally. This contribution depends on enclosure conditions, ventilation, and how the mist mixes with the fire gases.

🛢️

Fuel Surface Cooling

Droplets that reach a fuel surface can cool and wet it, reducing heat available to sustain burning. Whether they reach the surface depends on spray momentum, obstructions, and the rising fire plume.

Droplet Size Engineering: Understanding the Trade-Off

A nozzle produces a distribution of droplet sizes. Smaller droplets offer more surface area per water volume; larger droplets carry more momentum at the same speed. The complete spray pattern and operating conditions determine how effectively water reaches and cools the fire.

Atomisation Physics: Pressure, Orifice, and Flow Regime

Match nozzle geometry and pressure to the fire-tested system design. The WaterQuest equipment ceiling stated on this site is 160 bar (2,321 psi), including peaks; the selected configuration may have a lower limit.

Nozzle selection inputs

  • Pressure: within the selected system and component limits
  • Orifice: selected for the required spray distribution
  • Flow: based on nozzle demand and simultaneous discharge
  • Spray pattern: matched to coverage and obstructions
  • Droplet momentum: assessed with the complete spray pattern

What establishes suppression performance

  • Droplet distribution and surface area
  • Heat transfer and the fraction of water that evaporates
  • Cooling under the tested fire conditions
  • Vapour formation at the local temperature and pressure
  • Fire-test results for the protected hazard

Six Reasons

Where water can
strengthen the design.

🔥

Non-flammable working fluid

Water itself does not burn. Replacing a suitable oil hydraulic circuit removes that combustible working-fluid inventory; electrical and other ignition sources still need control.

✅

Remove the hydraulic-oil source

An oil-free working circuit removes one potential source of contamination. Specify working-water quality and the hygiene or discharge requirements for the installation.

🌡️

Design for temperature

Select the equipment for the expected operating and emergency temperatures. Include freezing, standby periods and loss of power in the protection strategy.

⚙️

Materials matched to the exposure

Select metals, seals and wear surfaces for the actual water chemistry and environment. Provide access for inspection, flushing and service.

🔧

A defined maintenance programme

The converted circuit no longer needs hydraulic-oil changes. Plan water-quality checks, filtration, seal inspection and functional tests to maintain readiness.

📉

A measurable lifecycle case

Compare installed cost, energy, inspection, consumables and service over the same duty and operating life. Record the oil-handling tasks and exposure removed.
