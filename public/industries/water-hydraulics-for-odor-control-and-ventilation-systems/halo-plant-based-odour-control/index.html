Chemistry needs contact

Bring the treatment to the odour-bearing air.

Plant-based odour treatment uses interactions between the formulation and odorous compounds. Delivery determines whether that contact can occur.

  1. Create the contact zone

    Distribute the selected mixture where it can intercept the release. Coverage, airflow and residence time influence contact.

  2. Transfer into the liquid

    Some odorous compounds can dissolve into treatment droplets. Their affinity for the liquid and the available contact area affect this transfer.

  3. Match the chemistry

    Formulation-specific interactions, including neutralization for suitable compounds, can reduce odour. Confirm the applicable mechanism for the selected Halo product.

WaterQuest application engineering

Design the delivery. Control the contact.

Specify the product, water supply, atomization and operating logic as one application. Match the pump, nozzles and dose to the actual duty.

Supply

Water quality, available flow, pressure, power and filtration.

Coverage

Air movement, nozzle location, drift and acceptable wetting.

Compatibility

Wetted materials, product concentration and cleaning access.

Control

Operating schedule, level indication, faults and usage records. Specify remote access or alerts where required.

Start with the duty

Different sources. A focused application.

Use the source and release pattern to shape the Halo assessment. Suitability depends on the compounds, conditions and selected formulation.

01 / Wastewater & biosolids

Receiving, storage and sludge handling

Map tanks, vents and dewatering areas. Distinguish a captured air stream from an open surface, and allow for changing process loads.

02 / Waste & organics

Transfer, storage and active handling

Trace releases during tipping, turning and movement. Relate the application zone to operating periods, wind and nearby receptors.

03 / Industrial processing

Production vents and process areas

Characterize the stream, temperature and operating cycle. Check access, materials compatibility and interaction with existing air treatment.

Evidence before expansion

Define success. Then demonstrate it.

An effective trial tests the complete application under representative operating conditions, with agreed measurement points and a clear decision at the end.

  1. Baseline

    Record the existing condition, process activity and weather. Choose representative observation or sampling points.

  2. Trial

    Apply defined settings. Track the application zone, operating periods and product consumption.

  3. Compare

    Review equivalent periods using consistent odour assessment and, where relevant, compound-specific sampling.

  4. Refine

    Adjust, accept or reassess. Include resource use, servicing and system reliability in the decision.
