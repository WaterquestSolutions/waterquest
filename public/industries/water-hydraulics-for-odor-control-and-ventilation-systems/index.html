The customer benefit

Give the treatment process dependable water and movement.

Uneven spray, a sticking damper or a valve in the wrong position can undermine an odour-control process. A coordinated water supply and actuation package helps keep those functions under control. Water hydraulic actuators remove oil from the selected working circuit; water-lubricated pumps serve suitable clean-water duties. The treatment designer still selects the chemistry, contact conditions and air-handling arrangement that address the actual odour compounds.

How water does the work

See how water does the work.

For damper or valve movement, a conditioned-water circuit delivers pressure to an actuator and uses valves to control its travel. For spray supply, the pump delivers water to the selected nozzles. Keep these duties distinct from circulating chemically aggressive scrubber liquor: the working fluid and every wetted component must be matched to their service.

01

Water contact must suit the chemistry.

A wet scrubber transfers suitable compounds from air into a liquid. Solubility, chemical reaction, contact area and residence time affect removal. Plain water will not remove every odorous compound effectively. Chemical dosing requires compatible equipment, managed blowdown and a treatment process designed for that reagent.

02

The fan and the liquid system have different duties.

The fan overcomes air-path resistance while the liquid pump supplies distribution or sprays. A high-pressure mist pump is not automatically suitable for a low-pressure recirculating scrubber. Choose each pump from the required flow, pressure, fluid chemistry and solids loading, then coordinate the controls.

Practical applications

Water supply and actuation across the treatment train.

Use these examples to connect an operating problem with a possible water-based arrangement. Bring the closest match to your enquiry, along with the site conditions that will shape the design.

01

Sludge-building wet scrubber

The customer’s needOdorous air from sludge handling can escape through openings before treatment.

The water-based approachReview enclosure and extraction first. For suitable compounds, use an engineered scrubber with an appropriate circulation pump, distribution system and monitored reagent chemistry where required.

Confirm the resultVerify capture airflow and treatment performance at representative loading; record pressure drop, liquid conditions and blowdown requirements.

02

Biofilter conditioning

The customer’s needA biological odour-treatment bed needs a controlled moisture condition to support operation.

The water-based approachProvide distributed irrigation or humidification that suits the media and treatment supplier’s limits. Prevent dry zones and avoid saturating the bed or flooding air paths.

Confirm the resultMap moisture conditions, pressure drop and outlet performance through changing weather and contaminant loads.

03

Ventilation damper positioning

The customer’s needA corrosive wet environment makes some damper locations difficult to maintain.

The water-based approachSelect a protected water hydraulic actuator for the damper torque and desired position on a fault. Keep the clean actuation circuit separate from scrubber liquor and provide accessible isolation.

Confirm the resultCheck full travel, position feedback and the loss-of-power response; verify the resulting ventilation condition rather than only the actuator movement.

An illustrative calculation

Air-contact volume is a design input

For an illustrative treatment air flow of 3,600 m³/h (15,850 US gpm), the flow is 1 m³/s (35.3 ft³/s). A nominal three-second empty-bed residence time corresponds to 3 m³ (106 ft³) of bed volume.

Nominal bed volume = air flow × residence time

Take the calculation into equipment selection.

This is arithmetic only. The treatment supplier determines the required contact time, media, packing, distribution and removal performance for the actual compounds.

Questions customers ask

Make an informed equipment choice.

What is the benefit of water-lubricated pumping?

A purpose-designed water-lubricated pump uses the pumped water to lubricate its internal working surfaces, keeping oil lubrication out of that pumping circuit. The right model depends on water chemistry, flow, pressure and inlet conditions. WaterQuest helps match those requirements to the equipment.

Can an outdoor mist ring solve a building odour issue?

It may fail to intercept the emissions and can mask rather than remove the source. Start with capture and compound-specific treatment.

Can one pump serve scrubber liquor and hydraulic actuators?

Not as an assumed shared circuit. Chemical liquor and precision hydraulic components have different compatibility and cleanliness requirements.
