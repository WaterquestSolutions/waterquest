The customer benefit

Keep the water experience free of an avoidable oil source.

An oil leak near pool water can disrupt operation and create a difficult clean-up. A dedicated water hydraulic loop removes that source of oil while providing the force to operate suitable gates and mechanisms. Build the equipment around the real cycle pattern, dynamic loads and access for maintenance. Guest protection comes from the complete attraction design, including guarding, interlocks and the response to a fault.

How water does the work

See how water does the work.

The pump sends pressurised water through control valves to the actuator. Pressure provides force and flow controls speed; sensors coordinate the mechanism with the operating sequence. The hydraulic working water can be held in a separate conditioned loop, so disinfected pool water does not have to pass through the pump, seals and precision valves.

01

Repeated motion makes timing and fatigue important.

Wave equipment may cycle frequently for long operating periods. Acceleration, deceleration, end cushioning and coordinated movement affect loads on the actuator, pins and supporting structure. Count the expected cycles and include the worst operating combination rather than sizing only for a single slow stroke.

02

Pool chemistry is not a universal working fluid.

Disinfectant concentration, chlorides, temperature and cleaning chemicals can affect materials and seals. A segregated conditioned-water loop allows the actuator’s fluid specification to be managed independently. External splash exposure still needs to be addressed through material selection, drainage and inspection.

Practical applications

Repeatable movement for wet-area equipment.

Use these examples to connect an operating problem with a possible water-based arrangement. Bring the closest match to your enquiry, along with the site conditions that will shape the design.

01

Wave-system gate mechanism

The customer’s needAn air-driven wave installation needs repeatable gate or valve movement in a restricted service area.

The water-based approachSelect a water hydraulic actuator against the attraction designer’s required timing and fatigue duty. Guard the mechanism and integrate position confirmation with the existing safety architecture.

Confirm the resultVerify timing over a representative cycle count and test agreed fault responses with the attraction closed to visitors.

02

Adjustable play feature

The customer’s needA movable feature changes position between approved operating modes.

The water-based approachKeep movement outside guest access or protect it through the attraction’s engineered safeguards. Define a held position and prevent an automatic restart after an interruption.

Confirm the resultDemonstrate position limits, guarding, emergency stop and supervised reset under the approved acceptance procedure.

03

Plant-room isolation valve

The customer’s needA large service valve operates near the pool-water system.

The water-based approachUse a water hydraulic actuator sized for valve torque, with clear local indication and a controlled closure rate. Do not let actuation-water connections create a cross-connection with the treated bathing-water circuit.

Confirm the resultProve isolation and valve travel without creating an unacceptable transient or interrupting required water-treatment functions.

An illustrative calculation

Count the lifetime movements

A mechanism completing one cycle every 12 seconds accumulates 300 cycles per hour. At six operating hours per day and 200 days per year, that is 360,000 cycles annually.

Annual cycles = cycles/hour × operating hours/day × days/year

Build the commercial case

Make the oil-free option a practical investment.

Focus on a mechanism where oil leakage could interrupt operation or affect pool water. Assess cycle consistency, service access, inspection time and the effect of planned maintenance on attraction availability.

  1. Define the improvement. Record the present duty, service effort and operating problem. Agree the movement, output or availability the new equipment must deliver.
  2. Specify and demonstrate the package. Match components to the actual water, load and operating range. Use agreed commissioning checks or a focused trial to confirm the result.
  3. Compare the complete ownership cost. Include equipment, installation, utilities, fluid management, servicing and downtime. Use measured results where available and identify estimates clearly.

Questions customers ask

Make an informed equipment choice.

Can water hydraulics provide the force this equipment needs?

Yes, when the equipment is sized for the duty. Cylinder force comes from pressure acting over the effective piston area; controlled flow sets movement speed. WaterQuest helps match the cylinder, pump and valves to the load, stroke and operating sequence, using components designed for water.

Can pool water power the cylinders?

Only if the complete circuit is expressly designed for that chemistry and the cross-connection assessment accepts it. Segregation is often an important design choice.

Will the mechanism restart after a power cut?

Restart behaviour must follow the attraction’s safety design. Restoration of electrical power alone should not be treated as permission for movement.
