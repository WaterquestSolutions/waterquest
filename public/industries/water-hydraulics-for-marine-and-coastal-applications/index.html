The customer benefit

Keep hydraulic oil away from the harbour and the deck.

Equipment beside open water has little separation between a leaking hose and the environment. Replacing a suitable oil hydraulic circuit with water hydraulics removes that source of oil while retaining compact, controllable actuation. Design the package around the full movement and holding duty, then give the crew accessible isolation, inspection points and a clear recovery procedure. The result is an oil-free working circuit built for the actual marine task.

How water does the work

See how water does the work.

Pressurised water carries power from the pump to the actuator. The cylinder converts pressure into force, while controlled flow governs movement. A dedicated treated-freshwater loop keeps the working fluid separate from the sea; a seawater-fed arrangement requires components selected specifically for that chemistry. External salt exposure and internal working-water quality are separate design considerations.

01

Geometry changes the cylinder load.

Ramp weight, live load and centre of gravity create a moment around the hinge. The actuator’s perpendicular moment arm changes through the travel, so the largest cylinder force may occur near the beginning or end of motion. Include vessel acceleration and imposed loads in the structural design basis.

02

Salt exposure is more than a material label.

External splash, immersion, crevices, dissimilar metals and warm stagnant water create different corrosion conditions. A 316L description alone is not seawater approval. Check rods, fasteners, bearings, seals and hose fittings, with flushing and inspection access designed into the installation.

Practical applications

Water-powered movement at the water’s edge.

Use these examples to connect an operating problem with a possible water-based arrangement. Bring the closest match to your enquiry, along with the site conditions that will shape the design.

01

Harbour boarding ramp

The customer’s needA shore-to-vessel ramp needs controlled movement close to open water.

The water-based approachUse a dedicated water hydraulic cylinder arrangement with guarded hoses, position feedback and an independently assessed holding system. Keep operator sightlines and the vessel’s changing position in the control design.

Confirm the resultDemonstrate movement and holding across the load and geometry envelope, then test controlled recovery after a power or line-pressure loss.

02

Deck hatch handling

The customer’s needA heavy hatch must clear seals, move without racking and remain secure during service.

The water-based approachSize the actuator for seal breakaway and the least favourable leverage. Coordinate paired cylinders where used, and provide a mechanical securing method appropriate to the hatch design.

Confirm the resultMeasure alignment, closing force and position agreement. Prove maintenance restraint with hydraulic pressure removed.

03

Coastal intake gate

The customer’s needAn intake gate must isolate a seawater channel without exposing the actuator internals to untreated seawater.

The water-based approachPlace the clean-water supply in a sheltered service area and select the externally exposed cylinder and fittings for the actual splash or immersion conditions. Allow inspection without obstructing the intake walkway.

Confirm the resultTest full differential-head operation, position indication and a representative isolation sequence. Inspect accessible crevice and seal areas after the trial.

An illustrative calculation

Leverage before pressure

An illustrative 30 kN (6,744 lbf)·m ramp moment acting through a 0.30 m (0.984 ft) perpendicular actuator arm requires 100 kN (22,481 lbf) of cylinder force. If that arm reduces to 0.15 m (0.492 ft), the required force doubles to 200 kN (44,962 lbf).

Actuator force = hinge moment ÷ perpendicular lever arm

Take the calculation into equipment selection.

The example excludes dynamic effects and margins. Evaluate the complete travel before choosing cylinder bore or system pressure.

Build the commercial case

Make the oil-free option a practical investment.

Choose a mechanism where removing hydraulic oil has a clear operational value. Include deck cleaning, fluid handling, hose access, inspection and the consequence of a release when comparing the installation over its service life.

  1. Define the improvement. Record the present duty, service effort and operating problem. Agree the movement, output or availability the new equipment must deliver.
  2. Specify and demonstrate the package. Match components to the actual water, load and operating range. Use agreed commissioning checks or a focused trial to confirm the result.
  3. Compare the complete ownership cost. Include equipment, installation, utilities, fluid management, servicing and downtime. Use measured results where available and identify estimates clearly.

Questions customers ask

Make an informed equipment choice.

Can water hydraulics provide the force this equipment needs?

Yes, when the equipment is sized for the duty. Cylinder force comes from pressure acting over the effective piston area; controlled flow sets movement speed. WaterQuest helps match the cylinder, pump and valves to the load, stroke and operating sequence, using components designed for water.

Can the circuit run directly on seawater?

Only if the selected pump, valves, actuators and seals are rated for its chemistry and temperature. A treated freshwater circuit is a separate design choice.

Will a water circuit eliminate maintenance?

No. Filtration, corrosion inspection, seals, hose condition and reliable load holding remain essential, especially after long idle periods.
