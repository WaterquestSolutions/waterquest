Materials Engineering

Oil and water play by different rules. Good engineering respects the difference.

Oil and water can both transmit hydraulic power, but a system designed for oil cannot simply be expected to run reliably on water. Water demands a different approach to lubrication, wear and, especially, material compatibility.

The concern goes beyond ordinary rust. When dissimilar metals—such as carbon steel and stainless steel—are electrically connected and exposed to water containing dissolved salts or minerals, they can behave like a small battery. The water carries ions between them, allowing an electrochemical reaction that accelerates corrosion of the more active metal. This is galvanic corrosion. A small carbon steel fitting connected to a much larger stainless steel assembly can be particularly vulnerable, concentrating the damage in that single component. Technical reference: ASSDA ↗

That is why material selection must extend through the entire water circuit. A carefully engineered pump is only part of the solution: the valves, fittings and connections must also be compatible with the water and with one another. Our approach centres on 316 stainless steel and carbon-reinforced PEEK, with materials selected for their role and operating conditions.

That principle is why we created WQS Stainless: to supply the 316 stainless steel components that help carry sound engineering through every part of a water hydraulic system. Because reliable water hydraulics begins with designing for water from the start.

Our WQS Stainless division makes it possible to offer complete turnkey water hydraulic systems from a single source. From the hydraulic equipment to the stainless steel fittings and connections, you benefit from a coordinated package, consistent material selection and one point of contact for the complete system.
