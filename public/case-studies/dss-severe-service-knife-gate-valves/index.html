The challenge

Handle difficult media. Rethink the actuation fluid.

Abrasive slurries, suspended solids, fibres and corrosive process conditions can place heavy demands on an isolation valve. Selection must account for the media, pressure differential, materials and the force needed to move the gate.

Where conventional oil hydraulics operate near a process, waterway or sensitive work area, a leak from the actuation circuit adds a separate contamination concern. The valve and its actuator therefore address two connected parts of the same duty.

The paired solution

A severe-service valve with water hydraulic power.

The DSS valve provides the process-isolation element. Its gate, body, seating arrangement and materials are selected for the service. The water hydraulic equipment supplies the motion needed to open and close it.

Water replaces hydraulic oil in the selected actuation circuit. Purpose-designed water-service components, suitable filtration and an appropriate control arrangement complete the equipment selection.

Why the combination matters

Address the process duty and the oil-leak concern.

Severe-service isolation

The document identifies demanding solids-handling and corrosive duties as the reason to select a severe-service knife gate valve. Valve configuration and wetted materials must match the actual process.

An oil-free actuation circuit

Using water removes hydraulic oil from this circuit and the associated oil-spill cleanup requirement. Working-water quality, any additives and site discharge requirements still form part of the design.

A non-flammable working medium

Water itself does not burn. Replacing hydraulic oil removes that combustible fluid inventory from the selected circuit.

A practical ownership review

The potential value includes avoiding oil purchase, oil handling and oil-spill cleanup for the replaced circuit. Compare these with water treatment, filtration, energy, maintenance and installation costs for the actual duty.
