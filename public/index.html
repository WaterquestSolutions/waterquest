Three practical reasons to choose water

Protect your process. Simplify fluid servicing. Put energy to work.

01 / Environmental benefit

Remove an oil-contamination source.

Replacing hydraulic oil in the selected working circuit removes that source of oil leakage near your product, soil or waterway. It also removes the used oil that circuit would otherwise generate.

02 / Maintenance benefit

Reduce oil-related service tasks.

Eliminate hydraulic-oil changes, oil-condition checks and used-oil disposal from that circuit. Servicing focuses on water quality, filters, seals and component condition.

03 / Efficiency potential

Design for useful output.

Water’s low viscosity can reduce flow resistance. Purpose-built pumps, precision clearances and well-matched controls determine how that potential translates into energy use at your operating duty.

Water also does not burn, an advantage around heat or fire-sensitive processes. Compare complete systems at the same duty to establish the energy and service savings for your application.

How water hydraulics works

Pressure creates force. Flow creates movement.

A driven pump sends water through control valves to a cylinder or motor. The water transmits power; the pump’s drive supplies the energy.

01 / Pump

Supply the flow.

The pump moves water through the circuit. Resistance from the load develops pressure.

02 / Valves

Control the response.

Valves direct and regulate flow so equipment starts, changes speed, reverses and stops as required.

03 / Actuator

Do the work.

Cylinders lift, push and pull. Motors turn. A recirculating circuit collects the water for reuse.

The same pressure acting on the same effective piston area produces the same ideal force with water or oil. Actual output also accounts for friction, opposing pressure and component ratings.

What makes the system reliable

Match the water, materials and duty.

01 / Start with the water

Protect the components.

Match the equipment and filtration to the supply’s chemistry, salinity, temperature and cleanliness. Plan freeze protection where the site needs it.

02 / Choose the materials

Choose compatible materials.

Stainless steels and carbon-reinforced PEEK support water-service designs through corrosion resistance and wear control. Select every wetted part, seal and fitting for the actual water and duty.

03 / Match the operation

Engineer the complete circuit.

Size for load, speed and operating cycle. Include pressure protection, secure load holding, safe isolation and access for servicing.
